Your basic theory for biogeography (chance) is based entirely on negative
evidence â€“ the absence of early fossils. However, the fossil record of
Meliaceae is very sparse, and there are endless debates about the
identification of all the earliest fossils.

Huge gaps in the fossil record are common. For example, in coelacanths
(order Coelacanthiformes; two living species, 150 extinct fossil species),
the youngest fossil is 65 million years old (Grande, 2016). In lampreys
(order Petromyzontiformes; 38 living species), the youngest fossil is 120
million years old. In hagfishes (order Myxiniformes; 20 living species),
the only fossil is 300 million years old. These marine animals live in
conditions that are much more conducive to fossilisation than rain forest
plants such as Meliaceae.

*Reference*

Grande, L. (2016). Donn Rosen and the perils of paleontology. In: Crother,

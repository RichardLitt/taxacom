> >
> >> I am seeking an answer as to whether the calcified microscopic fossil
> genus
> >> Calcisphaera Williamson, 1880 (treated mainly as a botanical form)
> should
> >> be treated as a microfossil incertae sedis (Calcitarcha) or assigned to
> >> charophyta (as representing a charophyte spore aka gyrogonite).
> Williamson,
> >> 1880 originally assigned 7 species to the genus but failed to designate
> a
> >> type; of these, C. laevis (and possibly all of the others but one) is
> today
> >> considered a calcisphere microfossil in the group Calcitarcha, while C.
> >> robusta is considered a charophyte spore. The correct assignment of the
> >> genus therefore turns on which species is considered the type.
> >>
> >> Andrews (1955 and 1970) in "Index of Generic Names of Fossil Plants"
> gives
> >> the type as C. laevis, however Index Nominum Genericorum gives the type
> as
> >> C. robusta following Miller, 1889 who designated that species as the
> >> lectotype. Apparently Andrews' designation (which was subsequently
> >> supported by Peck & Morales, 1966 and Grambast-Fessard et al., 1989)
> >> followed "Article PB. 4 of the [then current] International Code of
> >> Botanical Nomenclature" which states: "The type of a genus of fossil
> plants
> >> is the first described species which shows such characters as are
> necessary
> >> for distinguishing the genus from other taxa."" (that text from Peck &
> >> Morales, 1966), thus according to that logic, Miller's designation of C.
> >> robusta as the type was not only unnecessary but invalid.
> >>
> >> However subsequently Samoilova, 1985, in a proposal to Taxon, recognised
> >> Miller's designation of C. robusta as the type and treated Calcisphaera
> as
> >> a charophyte genus, with apparent priority over another genus,
> Trochiliscus
> >> Karpinsky, 1906, if the two genera are treated as synonymous. Bu in a
> >> response to that proposal, Grambast-Fessard et al., 1989 stated: "The
> taxon
> >> Calcisphaera has not been retained [in Charophyta] as it includes
> species
> >> belonging to distinct taxonomic units; the first one described, C.
> laevis,
> >> designated as the type species, is not a charophyte but belongs to the
> >> animal kingdom (Peck and Morales, 1966: 314)."
> >>
> >> Later, Versteegh et al., 2009, on formally proposing the group
> Calcitarcha,
> >> noted the earliest forms to be those described by Williamson, 1880
> >> as Calcisphaera, and included that genus name in their list of
> Calcitarcha
> >> genera without further comment regarding its typification.
> >>
> >> Currently AlgaeBase offers a confused statement: it recognises
> Calcisphaera
> >> as a charophyte, with C. robusta as type species, but references
> >> Grambast-Fessard et al., 1989 as the most recent treatment followed (in
> >> fact as stated above, those authors state the exact opposite).
> >>
> >> So the question arises, does the current (or perhaps even a former)
> >> botanical Code support Peck & Morales, 1966 assertion that in fossil
> plants
> >> where no type is selected, the first mentioned species automatically
> >> becomes the type? That statement appears to originate in the Paris Code
> >> (1956), probably also in the 1952 Code, which indeed states:
> >> "Article PB. 4. The type of a genus of fossil plants is the first
> described
> >> species which shows such characters as are necessary for distinguishing
> the
> >> genus from other taxa. The type of a species of fossil plants is the
> first
> >> described and figured specimen showing such characters as are necessary
> for
> >> distinguishing the species from other species." (
> >> https://nam10.safelinks.protection.outlook.com/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.iapt-taxon.org%2Fhistoric%2F1956.htm&data%7C02%7Ctaxacom%40lists.ku.edu%7C7ffa47ccf3da42ead3ac08dddc279286%7C3c176536afe643f5b96636feabbe3c1a%7C0%7C0%7C638908784693946717%7CUnknown%7CTWFpbGZsb3d8eyJFbXB0eU1hcGkiOnRydWUsIlYiOiIwLjAuMDAwMCIsIlAiOiJXaW4zMiIsIkFOIjoiTWFpbCIsIldUIjoyfQ%3D%3D%7C0%7C%7C%7C&sdataâ8L%2F1g6vni95q%2B5SEDljkygb3ledRFX6mcHumekAH4%3D&reserved=0)
> >>
> >> So Andrews in 1955 would (according to my reading) be correct in using
> C.
> >> laevis as the tye species and ignoring Miller's subsequent designation
> of
> >> C. robusta.
> >>
> >> However I am not sure that is the situation today; the current ICNafp
> does
> >> not seem to contain this Article or wording; the nearest I can find
> (which
> >> is not specifically dealing with fossil plants is:
> >> 10.5. The author who first designates (Art. 7.10, 7.11, and F.5.4) a
> type
> >> of a name of a genus or subdivision of a genus must be followed, but the
> >> choice may be superseded if the author used a largely mechanical method
> of
> >> selection (Art. 10.6). A type chosen using a largely mechanical method
> of
> >> selection is superseded by any later choice of a different type not made
> >> using such a method, unless, in the interval, the supersedable choice
> has
> >> been affirmed by its adoption in a publication that did not use a
> >> mechanical method of selection.
> >>
> >> (A "mechanical method of selection" appears to comprise taking the first
> >> species listed).
> >>
> >> So where does this leave us? Is C. laevis or C. robusta the correct type
> >> for this genus, and accordingly, is it to be treated as Calcitarcha or
> >> Charophyta? (If indeed it is to be placed as a charophyte, then C.
> laevis
> >> and other similar species would require a new generic name in order to
> be
> >> placed in the Calcitarcha).
> >>
> >> Your answers appreciated,
> >>

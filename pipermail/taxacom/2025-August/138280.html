In my database of generic names (515,861 as at just now), a query for
"authority includes &" yields 89,270 instances, of which I would expect a
bit more than 80% (around 71,000+) to be zoological names and the balance
either botanical (plants and plant algae, fungi, some bacteria i.e.
Cyanophyceae) or other (mainly bacteria). So in theory I could change the
botanical instances to say "et" instead of "&" but I do not see any user
demand for that at this time - and probably never...

Just to add another wrinkle, the bacteriologists appear to spell "and" out
in full, e.g. see
Acetonema Kane and Breznak 1992

> Recommendation 46C
>
> *46C.1.* After a name published jointly by two authors, both authors should
> be cited, linked by an ampersand (&) or by the word ?et?.
>
> *Ex.* *1.* *Didymopanax gleasonii* Britton & P. Wilson or *D.*
> *gleasonii* Britton
> et P. Wilson.
>
> *46C.2.* After a name published jointly by more than two authors, the
> citation should be restricted to the first author followed by ?& al.? or
> ?et al.?, except in the original publication.
>
> *Ex.* *2.* *Lapeirousia erythrantha* var. *welwitschii* (Baker) Geerinck,
> Lisowski, Malaisse & Symoens (in Bull. Soc. Roy. Bot. Belgique 105: 336.
> 1972) should be cited as *L.* *erythrantha* var. *welwitschii* (Baker)
> Geerinck & al. or *L.* *erythrantha* var. *welwitschii* (Baker) Geerinck et
> al.

> > How is the pronounciation in the spoken languages?
> >
> > In German we say "Miller et al", just pronouncing "et" and "al" as if
> > these were German words. This is an international standard expression
> > and we just take it as such.
> > If we read "&" we usually say "und", so reading "Miller & Hansen" we
> > would usually say "Miller und Hansen".
> >
> > In French "and" translates "et", which is the same word as in Latin. So
> > for French scientists citing autors in the form "Miller et Hansen" would
> > just be the usual way.
> >
> > In German if we read "Miller et Hansen" this would provoke a conflict
> > for the pronounciation. Some might be inclined to read "Miller et
> > Hansen" (pronouncing "et" either in the German mode, or perhaps more
> > likely in the French mode, "Miller e Hansen"). This might be one of the
> > reasons why writing "&" is preferred in such situations. The & might be
> > successful because people in either language are free to pronounce in
> > their own way.
> >
> > Pronouncing "et al." is relatively simple - but how would we pronounce
> > "& al."? Would we say "und al"? Perhaps not. I would personally treat
> > this like an orthographic error and ignore the &, I would just read "et
> > al".
> >
> > I am asking myself how many zoologists know what "al." means? Italians
> > and Catalonians might just read "altri" or "altres". Also in Romanian
> > and Greek "others" translates to a word starting with "al".
> >
> > I am not sufficiently firm in spoken English, and I wonder if there are
> > differences in the pronounciation between British, North American,
> > Australian or Indian English native speakers.

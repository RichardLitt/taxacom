On 20 June 2014 19:26, Doug Yanega <dyanega at ucr.edu> wrote:

> If the illustration's depiction of the hairs (evenly-spaced, thickened
> rami, arranged in perfectly opposing pairs) is accurate, then I doubt
> this has anything to do with an insect. If, however, we assume that the
> drawing took "artistic liberty" and those were simply branched hairs,
> then this would correspond in general shape and setation to the
> trochanter of a bee leg. I specify general shape, however, because a bee
> trochanter is somewhat asymmetrical, and figures 9a and 9b show this as
> being perfectly symmetrical. The shape is not quite right for an
> antennal segment (scape or pedicel), either, nor a tarsomere, but I
> suppose an antennal pedicel is not entirely out of the question (the
> distal aperture of a pedicel should be more nearly circular, not a
> tapered oval, but we're talking about a lot of species of insects so I
> don't want to say "it's impossible"). A lot depends on how accurate the
> drawings are.

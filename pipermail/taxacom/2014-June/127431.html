Michael sorry for the tardy reply but I am travelling quite a bit.
Going back to your email, I believe you are confounding dating the
object with dating the biological significance of the event. Fossils
can also be very accurately dated, but that is not the problem. What I
am simply stating is that rifting continents and rising mountains have
a beginning and an end, or at least a range from the earliest
inception to when they can be considered actual barriers. And just
because something starts it doesn?t translate into an outright
splitting of all taxa. Case in point the Red Sea or the East African
rift. Even when you have a proto-sea in between, how far would you say
it need be to split all biota?

In the end you also have to make an educated guess. Asking me to give
you a hard rule of thumb for fossils is pointless because it would
depend on each case. I would argue however that with ever more fossil
coverage uncertainty will be minimized. This is preferable to ad hoc
timing of any pattern that can be fit on to tectonics. As Richard
rightly pointed out, so many taxa and so few patterns. Timing is the
key and the circularity of your method makes it impossible to
determine without resorting to circularity.

John, I challenge you to find any statement where I deny vicariance as
a major mechanism in biogeography. Or that molecular divergences are
actual. Or that any fossil can be perfectly assigned within a
phylogeny. My position is that they are all estimates, including
tectonic-based calibrations.

Best

Jason

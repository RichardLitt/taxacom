Hi Ken,

The Mitchell study of the Anas ducks was calibrated with a Miocene fossil,
A. soporata. As for its identification, the authors wrote: 'Based on its
plesiomorphic morphology, we judged A. soporata to be an outgroup to extant
Anas'. No other details were given. But they admitted: 'We consider Anas
soporata to be a stem member of the genus; however, if this interpretation
is incorrect and A. soporata is in fact crown-Anas then this would lead us
to underestimate divergence dates'. So that's one real problem - placing a
fossil at a precise point on a phylogeny.

One other problem is: just how much older than the fossil can the clade be?
Mitchell et al stipulated, as a prior, that the group with the Miocene
fossil could be no older than the exact base of the Oligocene (33.9 Ma).
But why pick that age? Why not 34.0 Ma? Why not the middle of the Eocene,
or the base of the Eocene, or the Paleocene, or the Cretaceous...? There's
no reason, it's completely arbitrary.

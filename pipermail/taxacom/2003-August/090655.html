The cobra Naja siamensis was described by Laurenti (1768) on the basis 
of a (non-naturalistic) engraving in Seba's Thesaurus. Later, the cobra 
Naja naja isanensis was described by Nutaphand (1982), but without a 
type being formally designated. However, a photo was shown and 
represents an iconotype. The two taxa agree in terms of visible 
diagnostic features and locality of origin.

The matter was settled by desgination of a preserved single musem 
specimen as neotype for both N. siamensis and N.n. isanensis, consigning 
the latter to objective synonymy of the former.

Reference:
W?STER, W., D.A. WARRELL, M.J. COX, P. JINTAKUNE & J. NABHITABHATA 
(1997) Redescription of Naja siamensis Laurenti, 1768 (Serpentes: 
Elapidae), a widely overlooked spitting cobra from Southeast Asia: 
geographic variation, medical importance and designation of a neotype. 
Journal of Zoology, 243: 771-788. (pdf downloadable at 
http://biology.bangor.ac.uk/~bss166/Publications.htm)

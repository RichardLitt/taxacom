John et alia:

Dan is right on the money again when he wrote:
I am all for species level dets on the web from photos, but I think
that we should be brutally honest about not putting a species level
name unless we feel absolutely certain.

As a professional insect identifier for USDA as well as a scientist we
need to remember a couple of things:

1) What is the identification going to be used for, etc.  As Dan points
out that you can never be sure what "innocents" may do with your
identification. So, one needs to know who is requesting the
identification and for what purpose.

2) Science requires a high quality level. For science, we want to be
able to "re-test" our hypotheses, etc. and for identifications which are
to be the basis for scientific hypotheses we should be requesting /
demanding that there is a VOUCHER. I would like to say that that a
voucher always is a physical specimen, but, for Dan, I will agree that
we do not need to collect a King Vulture if we have a good picture. The
problem is whether non-specimen vouchers are good enough to show
sufficient characters to allow current and future workers to make a
proper identifications. For skippers and most flies, the answer is NO.

HENCE, I have started to tell people who sent me digital pictures that
I will not make specific identification without seeing a specimen and
also being able to retain that specimen in a public collections, etc.
Science demands at least that. For general public, etc., a common name
(group name) like "flower fly," "horse fly," "long-tailed skipper,"
etc., is sufficient.

John, my take on this is that we should not encourage det attempts on
photos past what we feel absolutely certain about, because the
innocents will put that species level name on that photo, and then
others will have photos that look "exactly" like it, and will not
have the benefit of our ifs, ands and buts.  And then pretty soon
every long-tailed Urbanus from S. America will appear with the name
Urbanus proteus (even that word "group" will disappear, and besides,
nobody but taxonomists knows what is a "group").  I have 7 of them
here in the ACG that look like that thing superficially (and the
greens above go all over the place, depending) and perhaps even more,
and who knows what will be in some Urbanus mess in S. America.

I am all for species level dets on the web from photos, but I think
that we should be brutally honest about not putting a species level
name unless we feel absolutely certain.  Upperside proteus group are,
in my opinion, simply not doable to speciers from photos or
otherwise, unless you already have the entire package of species for
your particular site down cold.

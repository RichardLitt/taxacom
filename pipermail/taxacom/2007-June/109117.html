I guess panbiogeography is still different in that it allows for the
possibility of using spatial information in phylogenetic reconstruction.


I sometimes wonder about the use of terms such as "modern" when applied
to techniques as if the conceptual foundations for interpretation are
also modern. One sees this in molecular studies for example, where
molecular clocks are supposed to be "modern" but the interpretation of
divergence is as old as the works of Matthew's Climate and Evolution. It
will be interesting to see whether "we can understand more about a
taxon's current and past distribution" really holds up.

There have been some publications dealing with GIS techniques and track
construction.

John Grehan

> Tony:
>
> Yes, in zoology, the Principle of Priority operates on family-group names
> in the same way as it operates for genus-group and species-group names.
>
> Acanthogorgiidae Gray, 1859 (type genus *Acanthogorgia* Gray, 1857) takes
> precedence over (= is the senior synonym of) Paramuriceidae Bayer, 1956
> (type genus *Paramuricea* K?lliker, 1865) when these names denote the same
> nominal taxon, unless the ICZN has ruled otherwise.
>
> /Thomas

> So far as I can see, the relevant portion of the ICZN Code is this:
>
> 23.3. *Application to Synonymy*
>
> The Principle of Priority requires that a taxon formed by bringing
> together into a single taxon at one rank two or more previously established
> nominal taxa within the family group, genus group or species group takes as
> its valid name the name determined in accordance with the Principle of
> Priority [Art. 23.1] and its Purpose [Art. 23.2] , with change of suffix if
> required in the case of a family-group name [Art. 34].
>
> *Example.* The valid name of a genus formed by the union of the genera
> *Aus* 1850
> and *Cus* 1870, and the subgenus *Bus* 1800 (transferred from the genus
> *Xus* 1758), is *Bus* 1800.
> So, can I re-phrase the question: is there any reason why an enlarged (aka
> synonymized) family that includes both the types of previous families
> Paramuriceidae Bayer, 1956 and with Acanthogorgiidae Gray, 1859 should take
> the later name (Paramuriceidae) and not the earlier one (Acanthogorgiidae)?
> If there is no reason according to the Code (and in fact such a choice is
> in contravention of it), I can inform the relevant person responsible for
> both the paper in which this is asserted, and the maintenance of the
> relevant sector of WoRMS by which this information is disemminated further.

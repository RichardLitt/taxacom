Michael wrote:
"Specifically, the subfamily name Pseudocrenilabrinae and a new subtribe
named  Pseudocrenilabrina would not be affected by invalidation of the tribe
name Pseudocrenilabrini, would they?"
Just to make it clear: There is no "new name" here and none should be
"proposed". All these names are one and the same and refer to the same
family-group name, and the authorship is Fowler (1934). The rule of Reversal
of Precedence (Article 23.9) only applies to cases of synonymy. The *nomen
ablitum* remains available and should be used as a valid name whenever the
*nomen protectum* is not synonymous, as it is the case of
*Pseudocrenilabrina* (whenever *Haplochromis* is not included in this
subtribe). The Example next to Article 23.9.2 demonstrates when a "nomen
oblitum* should be used as a valid name.

Stephen was apparently on the right path. If I have not overlooked something
important.

Decisive should be Art. 23.3.1:

"23.3.1. Priority of the name of a nominal taxon is not affected by
elevation or reduction in rank of the taxon within the family group, genus
group or species group [Arts. 36, 43, 46], nor by any mandatory change in
suffix of a family-group name consequent upon change in rank [Art. 34]."

If the action was taken under Art. 23.9.2 then the senior name as such was
fixed as not having precedence over the junior name. The term "synonym"
refers to a name, which can be extracted from the Glossary.

GLOSSARY
"synonym, n. Each of two or more names of the same rank used to denote the
same taxonomic taxon."

So under Art. 23.9.2 was determined that the senior tribe name should not
have precedence over the junior tribe name.
Art. 23.3.1 determines that priority is not affected by reduction of this
tribe name in rank to denote a subtribe name.
So the synonymy will also apply to the subtribe situations.

illustrate this case? Is it open access?

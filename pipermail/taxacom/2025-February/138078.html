Dear Erikjan,

I believe you are right. I cannot think of a circumstance where a species
name without parentheses in the authorship should have an earlier year than
its containing genus, so that should be a sign that something is incorrect.
My database (https://nam10.safelinks.protection.outlook.com/?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.irmng.org%2F&data%7C02%7Ctaxacom%40lists.ku.edu%7Cf38c2ca49b064e83341408dd54470eb2%7C3c176536afe643f5b96636feabbe3c1a%7C0%7C0%7C638759386356431140%7CUnknown%7CTWFpbGZsb3d8eyJFbXB0eU1hcGkiOnRydWUsIlYiOiIwLjAuMDAwMCIsIlAiOiJXaW4zMiIsIkFOIjoiTWFpbCIsIldUIjoyfQ%3D%3D%7C0%7C%7C%7C&sdata=AiBKIQJx3wK14zg17ACmzRkZw%2ByVEjMHB5WfYLBNHx0%3D&reserved=0) has inherited a large number of species
binomials from other systems, but they have not undergone rigorous checking
in this respect and possibly never will, until we have resources to devote
to that task. But for other systems with more available editor effort, this
could certainly be a consideration for auto-checking, I would say.

> There is the species name and a genus name:
>    Papasula abbotti Ridgway, 1893
>    Papasula Olson & Warheit, 1988
>
> These are, of course, literature references. Ridgway in 1893 described
> Sula abbotti [1]. It seems clear that parentheses are needed, like so:
>    Papasula abbotti (Ridgway, 1893)
>
> The year of the genus being more recent than the species name, i.e.,
> 1988 > 1893, already implied that parentheses are needed.
>
> It seems to me such cases can be easily found automatically and changes
> could be made, perhaps also automatically, or at least flagged to the
> editor(s) concerned as a change that is probably needed.
>
> Perhaps such changes or flagging could be implemented? It seems such a
> small and simple improvement.

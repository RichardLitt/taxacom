   Dear colleagues,

Since July 2004 the Instituto Butantan decided to create officially the
curator position for the 4 zoological collections holded at that
institution: Herpetological, Entomological, Acarological, and Arachnids
and Myriapoda.

In February 2005 the Arachnids and Myriapoda collection was locked by
the curator,  thus preventing access to the specimens by the other
researchers.

I've been working with mygalomorph spiders for almost 15 years now, 10
years as researcher of the Laboratorio de Artropodes. In this period I
produced some taxonomic revisions and worked on the biology  mainly of
theraphosid spiders. I've been also the responsible for identification
and inclusion of specimens in that mygalomorph collection. I worked
hardly in identificating the 11,000 specimens deposited there.

Then, suddenly, I and my students had denied the access to the
specimens, even being a taxonomist researcher. The Brazilian
Constitution warrants access to
information for every citizen, and so I looked for the justice to get
access to the specimens.

However, in April 01, 2005 I was compulsoryly transferred to a new
laboratory, Imunochemistry. Two weeks later I finally got a judicial
order to give me free access to the collection. Surprisingly, the
institution did not follow the judicial order ! Two months later, I
tried again to examinate some specimens, but the access was denied again
by the curator.

I am still awaiting to have access to that collection. A few specimens
was loaned for I finish some of my papers. However, for someone who
works with cladistics, it is necessary to compare many taxa in order to
have characters to make the analysis. Due to the difficulties in getting
specimens, this work has been very difficult.

In July, the alive specimens that were used for studies of biology,
among them specimens of the spermathecae-lacking species Sickius
longibulbi, were taken from me and Dr. Pedro Ismael da Silva Junior,
responsible for the specimens after I leaved the Laboratory. Thus,  many
works on  mate behavior, defensive behavior concerning urticating setae,
and so on suddenly stopped. Many students had their work affected by
that posture. That's not the adequate treatment that should be given to
official  researchers of a scientific institution, and Dr. Pedro asked
for leaving the Laboratory. I wrote a letter asking for an official
position regarding those specimens, but did not receive an answer yet.
We are very worried they can kill all mygalomorph specimens !

Despite all these troubles, we are far from giving up to work on
mygalomorphs.
We understand that some people are very interesting in giving this idea
out and we suspect some information stating this is being disseminate.

So, I would like to communicate to all arachnologists colleagues and
friends that we are still working with taxonomy and biology of
mygalomorphs and we have no intention of stopping to do it !

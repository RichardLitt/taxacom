> Hi All, all the info so far is on target.
> 
> I'll respond to Rod's comment about diminish content, yes, the past 2+ years
> have seen new BHL content plummet. That has mostly been a factor of
> digitization efforts slowing (or stopping) at some of our big partners. Example:
> Smithsonian went from 335K pages in 2019 to 19k pages in 2021, 7K pages
> 2022 (year to date).
> 
> BHL remains an active program of the Smithsonian with generous support. BHL
> also relies on it's 41+ global members and affiliates for funding that primarily
> support technical development as well as digitization of their collections.
> 
> BHL roughly adheres to US Copyright. When BHL began, the cut-off date was
> pre-1923 publications. The "wall" has been moving forward for the past few
> years, so as of now, January 1, 1927 is the cutoff date.
> 
> As others noted, BHL has actively sought permission from copyright holders for
> permission to make available (with the most liberal licensing) content that is
> (ostensibly) protected by copyright. The terms of each agreement varies, so
> there may be other limitations.
> 
> BHL works with the permissions holder and the BHL partner libraries that hold
> the physical copies to digitize additional content as the copyright wall moves
> forward. The past 2+ years have seen many partners stop or slow down
> digitization and things are only now getting started up again at scale. Which is
> to say, we're behind.
> 
> To a great extent, it is global copyright law and not necessarily funding that is
> the problem with moving forward with the vast amounts of material out there.
> For legal reasons, BHL is not able to be as aggressive in risk management of
> materials that are likely (legally) covered by legitimate intellectual property
> claims. <sad face>.
>

> Yes, the original cut off was influenced by US copyright, which moves forward a
> year at a time. In an ideal world this would automatically trigger additional
> scanning across the BHL member libraries, but that requires that those libraries
> have the resources to do this.
> 
> Note that there is BHL content that is much more recent, in some cases only a
> few months old. However, this depends on copyright holders being (a) willing
> to make their content available, and (b) BHL having the resources to scan
> and/or process that content.
> 
> My sense is that BHL's resources are increasingly limited, and the amount of
> new content being added to BHL (particularly, content related to taxonomy)
> has been diminishing recently. There are a few happy exceptions: a lot of
> recent Australian journals are being added, and most Mus?um National
> d'Histoire Naturelle journals are in BHL.

Hi All, all the info so far is on target.

I'll respond to Rod's comment about diminish content, yes, the past 2+ years have seen new BHL content plummet. That has mostly been a factor of digitization efforts slowing (or stopping) at some of our big partners. Example: Smithsonian went from 335K pages in 2019 to 19k pages in 2021, 7K pages 2022 (year to date).

BHL remains an active program of the Smithsonian with generous support. BHL also relies on it's 41+ global members and affiliates for funding that primarily support technical development as well as digitization of their collections.

BHL roughly adheres to US Copyright. When BHL began, the cut-off date was pre-1923 publications. The "wall" has been moving forward for the past few years, so as of now, January 1, 1927 is the cutoff date.

As others noted, BHL has actively sought permission from copyright holders for permission to make available (with the most liberal licensing) content that is (ostensibly) protected by copyright. The terms of each agreement varies, so there may be other limitations.

BHL works with the permissions holder and the BHL partner libraries that hold the physical copies to digitize additional content as the copyright wall moves forward. The past 2+ years have seen many partners stop or slow down digitization and things are only now getting started up again at scale. Which is to say, we're behind.

To a great extent, it is global copyright law and not necessarily funding that is the problem with moving forward with the vast amounts of material out there. For legal reasons, BHL is not able to be as aggressive in risk management of materials that are likely (legally) covered by legitimate intellectual property claims. <sad face>.

Yes, the original cut off was influenced by US copyright, which moves forward a year at a time. In an ideal world this would automatically trigger additional scanning across the BHL member libraries, but that requires that those libraries have the resources to do this.

Note that there is BHL content that is much more recent, in some cases only a few months old. However, this depends on copyright holders being (a) willing to make their content available, and (b) BHL having the resources to scan and/or process that content.

My sense is that BHL's resources are increasingly limited, and the amount of new content being added to BHL (particularly, content related to taxonomy) has been diminishing recently. There are a few happy exceptions: a lot of recent Australian journals are being added, and most Mus?um National d'Histoire Naturelle journals are in BHL.

There is a lot of taxonomic literature that has been digitised but which is not in BHL. I've been trying to keep track of some of this, e.g. https://nam10.safelinks.protection.outlook.com/?url=https%3A%2F%2Farchive.org%2Fdetails%2Ftaxonomyarchive%3F%26sort%3D-addeddate%26page%3D1&amp;data%7C01%7Ctaxacom%40lists.ku.edu%7C7ed17a28c56845c761d208dad1649dab%7C3c176536afe643f5b96636feabbe3c1a%7C0%7C0%7C638052527319184408%7CUnknown%7CTWFpbGZsb3d8eyJWIjoiMC4wLjAwMDAiLCJQIjoiV2luMzIiLCJBTiI6Ik1haWwiLCJXVCI6Mn0%3D%7C3000%7C%7C%7C&amp;sdata=xRGOsPNtZ33CgpR5InjqXISp6UrmjwkyCInPSrg20ZM%3D&amp;reserved=0<https://nam10.safelinks.protection.outlook.com/?url=https%3A%2F%2Farchive.org%2Fdetails%2Ftaxonomyarchive%3F%26sort%3D-addeddate%26page%3D1&amp;data%7C01%7Ctaxacom%40lists.ku.edu%7C7ed17a28c56845c761d208dad1649dab%7C3c176536afe643f5b96636feabbe3c1a%7C0%7C0%7C638052527319184408%7CUnknown%7CTWFpbGZsb3d8eyJWIjoiMC4wLjAwMDAiLCJQIjoiV2luMzIiLCJBTiI6Ik1haWwiLCJXVCI6Mn0%3D%7C3000%7C%7C%7C&amp;sdata=xRGOsPNtZ33CgpR5InjqXISp6UrmjwkyCInPSrg20ZM%3D&amp;reserved=0> which barely scratches the surface.

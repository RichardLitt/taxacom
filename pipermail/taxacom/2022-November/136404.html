> Hi All, all the info so far is on target.
>
>
>
> I?ll respond to Rod?s comment about diminish content, yes, the past 2+
> years have seen new BHL content plummet. That has mostly been a factor of
> digitization efforts slowing (or stopping) at some of our big partners.
> Example: Smithsonian went from 335K pages in 2019 to 19k pages in 2021, 7K
> pages 2022 (year to date).
>
>
>
> BHL remains an active program of the Smithsonian with generous support.
> BHL also relies on it?s 41+ global members and affiliates for funding that
> primarily support technical development as well as digitization of their
> collections.
>
>
>
> BHL roughly adheres to US Copyright. When BHL began, the cut-off date was
> pre-1923 publications. The ?wall? has been moving forward for the past few
> years, so as of now, January 1, 1927 is the cutoff date.
>
>
>
> As others noted, BHL has actively sought permission from copyright holders
> for permission to make available (with the most liberal licensing) content
> that is (ostensibly) protected by copyright. The terms of each agreement
> varies, so there may be other limitations.
>
>
>
> BHL works with the permissions holder and the BHL partner libraries that
> hold the physical copies to digitize additional content as the copyright
> wall moves forward. The past 2+ years have seen many partners stop or slow
> down digitization and things are only now getting started up again at
> scale. Which is to say, we?re behind.
>
>
>
> To a great extent, it is global copyright law and not necessarily funding
> that is the problem with moving forward with the vast amounts of material
> out there. For legal reasons, BHL is not able to be as aggressive in risk
> management of materials that are likely (legally) covered by legitimate
> intellectual property claims. <sad face>.
>

Leo,
My preference would be to do nothing about Hoser, but if that leads to his names being ignored by the rest of the herpetological community, then I think that the ICZN must step in and do something. The ICZN has no mandate to interfere in taxonomic matters, but there is nothing to say it can't interfere in matters relating to publication. If they are not satisfied that AJH is truly published in a Code compliant way, then they can issue a ruling to the effect that it isn't a valid publication, thus rendering all the new names published in?AJH unavailable. Hoser could publish them all again, but he loses priority on the currently contentious cases. Maybe the ICZN needs to?repeat the process maybe once a year, should Hoser continue to publish in nominally different journals. That is my sensible solution.
Cheers,
Stephen

"Naming glory" is nothing but a convenient camouflage for having no good arguments. The "right" to name taxa that you work with is certainly part of this issue, but it is not the main part by far, and this excuse for an argument gets waved around more in these debates than there is actually support for. The "mihi itch" that Michael refers to seems to affect Hoser and his kind far more greatly than anyone else. 

As I see it, from the state of my own field, this is about preventing unnecessary work in a discipline that -- for many organism group -- have to much work to do anyway. That is the main part of my previous post, which you seem to have ignored in favour of taking the easy way out and pretending it is mainly about "naming glory". This (my contribution here) is specifically about your suggestion that the herpetological community should just resign themselves to Hoser's antics, and have him be the "naming guy", which is and remains a weird and unhelpful suggestion which solves nothing and adds additional and unnecessary work for the future, work that may be delayed precisely because the unmanageable situation with the Hoseresque authors. As long as these kinds of clowns are not prepared to do a proper job the first time around, but implicitly relying on others to do that for them (either prior to, or eventually following, their own contributions), all they
 are doing is creating confusion and additional unnecessary job for others, before flitting away to the next taxon to do the same again. And your sensible solution is to just let them be and hope for the best. Must be nice to work with a group of organisms where there is so little to do that sitting around waiting for someone to create work for you is a sensible course of action!

Leo

>I don't see how it is more "sensible" to content yourself with redescribing species rather than describing species yourself.
>
>I'm currently redescribing lots of species and genera, precisely because we used to have a few Hoseresque people in my field, who would name species in private letters to colleagues, and somehow manage to convince (some of) these colleagues to use these names. Fortunately, none of them were as productive as Hoser is, and all of them actually also published some of their names in more proper ways (most of these taxa are entirely unidentifiable today, though, so their published work was often poor as well...). Nevertheless, advising someone to resign themselves to forcing future workers (or themselves in the future) to do a lot of work that should rightly have been done when the species was first described, is to invite tedium, a sense of doing lots of work for little recognition, and the endless hassle of trying to figure out what people who have been dead since you were born actually meant. Being 40-80 years down the line from where the herpetologists
 are today is not always a fun place to be, and I'm still in some ways in a better place than they will be, as at least I know that there is no one in my field alive today who behaves as despicably as Hoser.
>
>Sensible solutions to this sort of problem includes:
>1. The solution I outlined in my last mail;
>2. Trying to force Hoser (and people like him) to actually do things properly and stop behaving like a 5-year-old;
>3. Preventing Hoser (and people like him) from having their work taken seriously by the taxonomic community until 2) is achieved;
>4. Some combination of the above. 
>
>I don't see purposefully creating a lot of extra work for the future as being sensible in any sense of the word I am aware of. 
>
>
>Hiding behind the fact that his publications has little scientific content, only nomenclatorial content, is an unsatisfactory approach. It is undeniably the case that nomenclatorial acts are a central part of taxonomic work. The names I give to organisms is, or should be, the "receipt" that I've actually done some work on these organisms, and if I've spent years studying a group and find something new, I'll be damned if I will content myself with letting some nobody name these things after his mechanic just because he's bearing a grudge and can't behave like an adult. If you "can't do the science to back up your opinions", the correct, adult way to proceed is to find someone to collaborate with who can, not to turn yourself into a scientific Remora and publish a blog where you complain about those who actually can do the science. 
>
>
>While I certainly agree with you that until the commission has actually published something official on this, these things seem to be more or less valid (I haven't looked at all his various names, and am in no position to judge whether they are) and it's in general tough luck for the herpetologists for not naming new taxa in the same publications as their phylogenies, I don't see your solution as sensible, and I don't agree that this is not a serious issue.

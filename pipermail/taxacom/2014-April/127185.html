Not really! Who is promoting their considered view to others? Why is this "my most serious grievance about you"? Science may be a broader entity than scientific method, but the latter is the crucial methodological part of the former (by definition!) Scientific method is used to convince others that one's hypotheses are true. You seem to think that if some of your hypotheses (="intuitions") turn out to be true, then that is a win for you. Ray,
Actually you are right, it may not be chance. You may be a very intuitive herpetologist, whose taxonomic opinions are correct more often than not. But, here's the bit you just don't seem to understand: intuition is not science! Science is basically about convincing others that your intuitions are correct, using standard and agreed methods, which typically involve resources which you don't have. Anybody can publish their opinions, but if they aren't prepared to do the hard work to support those opinions scientifically, then nobody is going to be very interested. If anything, you are just giving away your intuitions for free to those who are prepared to do the work to prove or disprove them, but they are under no obligation to credit you with anything, not even the initial intuition. There are no "ownership rights" on intuitions, which is why it is usually a good idea to keep them to yourself until you can prove them to be correct, using science! I know it
 can be frustrating. I know vastly more about N.Z. beetles than I'll ever be able to prove scientifically. But that's life. The problem is that what you are doing runs the risk of actually giving your enemies exactly what they want, i.e. a mandate to ignore (without an ICZN ruling) other's available names. This could potentially seriously disadvantage any taxonomist who works outside powerful professional networks, many of whom make a very valuable contribution to taxonomy for relatively little monetary reward. For that reason, I consider you to be an insidious little misanthrope ..
Stephen

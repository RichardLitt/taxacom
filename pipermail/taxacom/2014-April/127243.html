"Identifier" was ambiguous.  We need good enough photographs, precise
georeferencing, and whatever other supporting information is necessary to
recognize what a particular specimen is.  But we also need a clear way of
saying "this particular barcode relates to individual XIF843275" such that
everyone knows that XIF843275 is the proper code to identify a unique
specimen record.  The identifier problem I was describing was not so much
the fact that the DNA sequence and the ID did not match the photo, but the
fact that when I gave the specimen reference number from GenBank I was told
that was not the correct reference number for them to tell which specimen
it was.  Also, they didn't tell me how to figure out which of multiple
reference numbers on the barcode website was the one that they wanted.

Of course, unambiguously associating a reference number with a specimen
that was captured and released is challenging as well; perhaps tagging
would work for some of those.



On Mon, Apr 21, 2014 at 3:55 PM, Schindel, David <schindeld at si.edu> wrote:

> This discussion immediately veered off-topic so I'll add some further
> information to try to get it back on track.
>
> A common complaint about online biodiversity databases is the lack of
> links to voucher specimens that can be re-examined to corroborate or refute
> the species name on the database records and the lack of quality control
> over species identification.
>
> The BARCODE data standard addresses the first concern directly by
> requiring a voucher ID in a standard format that will lead anyone with a
> question back to the repository, collection and catalog number associated
> with the voucher.  CBOL constructed the Global Registry of Biological
> Repositories (www.grbio.com, a merger with Index Herbariorium and
> Biodiversity Collections Index), as an authoritative look-up table for
> InstitutionCodes and CollectionCodes.
>
> The second weakness is more difficult to address so the Barcode of
> Wildlife is extending the BARCODE data standard by adding several metadata
> fields associated with the reliability of the species identification.  The
> include fields such as:
>
>    - Name of the identifier
>    - Date of the identification
>    - Type status of the voucher specimen (if any)
>    - Basis of identification (was the voucher specimen a complete adult, a
> pre-adult, a part, a tissue sample only, digital image only?)
>    - To what was the voucher compared?  Reference collections? Literature
> figures? Field guides? Morphological key?
>
> The question I posed is this.  Assuming it is out of the question to have
> a complete study skin or skeleton or liquid-preserved voucher, what proxies
> could be considered as adequate replacements for a traditional voucher?
>  Yes, it will vary by taxonomic group but assuming that we cannot kill and
> preserve a specimen, what's the minimum information and material we should
> require if we want to be able to corroborate the species ID independently
> from the barcode sequence?
>

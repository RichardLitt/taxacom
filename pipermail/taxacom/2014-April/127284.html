This thread has become rather braided, so excuse the multiple sub-topics
that follow.

Lyubomir - It sounds like I agree with you on the "data paper" concept and
I realize that Pensoft has the functionality to make these links from with
the publication.

Rod - I agree that insisting others act a certain way without providing
resources and/or guidelines is not a pathway to success. Many online
databases (including ours) provide a suggested citation for the database.
And I am all in favor of creating an archive space for data sets with UIDs
(does one exist already for biodiversity data?), that we construct as a
community resource. But I still think the responsibility of logging the
archive belongs to the user who aggregates, edits, enhances, or otherwise
alters the data. I also do not have the storage space to archive every
download from our system. But it IS the responsibility of the data provider
to track changes in his own database as a form of versioning (something we
can all do better).

Quentin brings up an interesting question about what gets an Unique
Identifier (UID). My view is that the database record is the metadata
associated with an object that has a UID. But one could give the record a
different UID as well (eg. aggregators may wish to track different records
from multiple sources for the same object). We give UIDs to "museum
objects" (slide preparation, pinned preparation, fluid vial) rather than
specimens. A slide or vial (or even a pin) may contain multiple specimens
(the number of which is noted in the database record). If a specimen is
removed from the preparation to form its own preparation it gets a new UID
and the old one gets updated. Generally this works well, but it is not
prefect (eg. imagine a holotype on the same slide as non-type specimens -
it would be nice to cite a separate record). We can put UIDs on parts of a
specimen, on gene sequences (one for every extraction, PCR, gene sequence
combination), but we don't HAVE to. One should exercise proper judgement
when deciding what gets an UID - if you probably want to reference it at
some point, give it a UID.

Yes, I am intentionally avoiding the term GUID and particular
implementations of them, like DOI. That can of worms can flood another
thread.

> Hi Peter,
>
> Whether freezing snapshots is the responsibility of the provider or user
> is worth discussing. I don't think it's necessarily "insane" to provide a
> GUID for each download, but I realise that this will generate a lot of
> identifiers that won't get used.
>
> Yes, the data set that a user ends up with after they have filtered,
> cleaned, and edited it should be preserved. Ideally the provider should be
> able to get that cleaned data back to make use of it. If you want to
> encourage such behaviour, then one approach would be to offer to host that
> cleaned data (and mint a DOI). That way you create an incentive for the
> user to (a) archive the data with an identifier, and (b) to give you back
> the cleaned data.
>
> Regarding citing collections, yes, having a paper that describes the
> collection is a good idea. When I developed phylogenetic software I did the
> equivalent, namely writing  paper about the software so people could cite
> that (if I hadn't, my citation count would look pretty dire). But asking
> people to cite the collection paper as well as the data set identifier is
> inconvenient (and it's not the thing they actually use in their work), but
> better to have a mechanism that automatically links the collection (or
> collection paper) to each data identifier.
>
> One thing that I'm constantly puzzled by is the frequency with which
> people insist others should do something: they should cite this collection,
> they should cite that collection. Surely it would be better to think about
> the behaviour you want (people citing your stuff), and then figure out how
> to make it a no brainer for them to do that. For example, if you want
> people to archive data derived from your collection, give them a reason to
> do so by providing them with that archiving service, and give them a DOI
> they can cite. Everybody wins.
>
> Regards

> On 26 Apr 2014, at 01:44, Peter Oboyski <pt_oboyski at hotmail.com> wrote:
>
> > A very topical discussion. Representing a museum generating data that is
> destined for the web, which will be picked up by aggregators and other
> users, I know this topic to be terribly complicated at several levels. But
> I will focus here just on the "snapshot" issue. I agree it is important to
> freeze a moment in time (as a snapshot) that represents the data used for a
> particular analysis/publication. But this task is the responsibility of the
> data user, not the provider. The user may draw data from multiple sources,
> do their own massaging, editing, vetting, etc. This unique data set should
> be archived (with a GUID or as supplementary data in a journal). But it is
> insane to expect a data provider to mint a DOI or other GUID for every
> download - most of which will not result in publication. Our database
> changes every hour. It behooves any data generating operation to track the
> addition and editing of data. This could be as simple as 'date-added' and
> date-edited' fields with a 'edit-remarks' field to note changes made during
> each edit. Most users will not make use of this information, but it is
> worth tracking.
> >
> > But this does not address the original point of this thread. We have
> spent a lot of resources and person-hours to build and populate our
> open-access database. We would like not only proper recognition for this
> effort, but also an opportunity to explain its sources, history, and
> limitations. This is best served by a published article that 'explains' the
> database, but does not provide the data itself (which is constantly
> changing). I would expect a user of our data to cite this publication
> (eh-hem, which we have yet to publish) as well as archive a version of the
> data they used for their analysis/publication. Likewise, I would expect
> aggregators to link back to our database website where this information is
> available.

Hi Ken and others,

Glycerol should be avoided at all costs for small things with broader or softer wings (such as parasitic wasps <2mm). It makes the wings very soft and floppy (as does acetic acid in AGA) so that it is difficult or impossible to dry them flat even if they are Critical Point Dried.

It seems that ethanol stronger than 70% (even 95%) is OK for smaller, winged insects especially if they are Critical Point Dried. It used to be thought that stronger alcohols made these insects brittle, but that was because of the addition of methyl alcohol to the alcohol or perhaps because the insects were stored for a prolonged period at higher temperatures or in daylight. Apart from making the smaller insects brittle, IMS (with methyl alcohol) will tend to make the insects lose their wings readily if the wings are moved at all during mounting. It is also said to destroy DNA. In my view IMS and isopropyl alcohol should be avoided, at all costs, when preserving smaller (<2mm), winged insects.

Sympatric speciation: the different races of *Rhagoletis pomonella
*(Tephritidae)
on hawthorn and apple trees are often cited as evidence for sympatric
speciation. Sequence data instead suggested allopatric differentiation
followed by overlap (Feder et al. 2003).

Sympatric speciation would lead to two species with more or less the same
distribution but different ecology. In the proposed cases I've seen, the
distributions are actually quite different - they just show some overlap.

I didn't mean to imply that all groups show high levels of geographic
structure. But marine biology and  microbiology have both undergone
'paradigm shifts' (their words) over the last decade as the sequencing
studies show such high levels of structure. These were completely
unexpected in the old dispersal model and reading the accounts you can
sense the excvitement and bewilderment at the new findings. The old idea of
'everything is everywhere' (and the environment selects) has been rejected.

Michael

> Q6, I?m afraid I have no experience in marine environments but a
> similar scenario can be seen in Europe. N and C European plains allow
> for easy migration of species E-W. However the glacial periods have

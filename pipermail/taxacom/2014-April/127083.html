Hi Stephen,? 

You say: 

"Also, I find it upalatable for so much scrutiny to be put on Hoser's work, when I have seen supposedly "bona fide" taxonomists do all sorts of silly and/or dodgy things like attributing characters to new taxa which the taxa don't have, massive disparities between written descriptions and accompanying illustrations, and all sorts of mistakes involving type designations, such as designating two different holotypes for the same new species!"

I think it's worth noting that while squabbles and sloppy work certainly exist in professional taxonomy, the venue for these disputes remains just that - professional. Rather than use a fancy blog and YouTube while operating under a perceived paradigm that the world is out to get you, taxonomists usually respond in a professional manner to?correct?or dispute mistakes in the peer reviewed literature. They don't send letters and petitions to "offending" authors' institutions calling for their dismissal or include accusations of rigging online competitions in their papers. It is for these reasons that I believe most professional herpetologists view his work as a somewhat morbid curiosity rather than just pseudoscience. While I understand that these sentiments have absolutely nothing to do with the code, I hope it provides some background as to why folks are so fast to defend good science. With statements from Mr. Hoser such as?"Yes if you buy any major
 books published on snakes in the last ten years you will see the names of Raymond Hoser and other Snakebusters team members cited as experts", how can an expert in the field not be somewhat taken aback?

Just to add a bit more to what Neal has correctly said, though I don't expect him to agree with me here, but taxonomists should not be allowed to make judgements on the availability of names, only on their validity. Otherwise, there is too much of a temptation to dismiss existing names and rename the very same taxa, thus claiming authorship/authority of the taxa. If a taxonomist has doubts over the availability of a name (or publication), the proper thing to do is for them to petition the ICZN for an official?ruling. Existing usage must be maintained until the ruling is made, which would mean in the present case that Hoser's names would stand unless officially decided otherwise in a published Opinion by the ICZN. The ICZN should also be impartial and objective in their ruling. This is the way things are supposed to work. Ahh, the perfect world, dream on!

>An author doesn't have to know if a specimen he chooses as a type is
>"actually" different as long as he says it is and he believes it. What the
>Code does is nomenclature. Hoser met the requirements of nomenclature to
>make the names available.
>
>What you are asking is to determine if the species is taxonomically valid.
>THAT is not what the Code does -- that is what taxonomists do. Your
>questions are of proper taxonomic protocol for a particular discipline in
>zoology, not nomenclature.
>
>The Code can minimize through its Articles and glossary the amount of
>taxonomic mistakes that may take place or minimize abuse of the privilege
>to do taxonomy correctly through following a nomenclatural protocol, but
>humans are at the other end of the problem and they can do whatever they
>want -- and taxonomic "mistakes" can only be fixed by other taxonomists.
>
>-Neal

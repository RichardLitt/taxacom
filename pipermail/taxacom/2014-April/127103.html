> "Holotype: A specimen in the Florida Museum of Natural History (FM) at the
> University of Florida, specimen number: 155266, from Liberty, Florida, USA.

> collection as laid out on their website at: http://www.flmnh.ufl.edu"

>
>
> No further description of the designated type material itself that could
> provide evidence that Hoser had ever seen at least a detailed picture of
> these specimens. However, as we all know, databases sometimes contain
> erroneous data. I am not sure if Hoser had requested further data on the
> designated species or had asked the curatorial staff of the FLMNH to put
> labels on these specific specimens marking them as holotypes for newly
> described taxa.
>
>
>
> Recommendations in the Code, although not mandatory, not only provide
> methods/actions for good practice, but are also given to avoid
> misunderstandings and misconceptions in nomenclatural acts and works. Hoser
> had deliberately failed to meet the recommendations such as 16C/72D,
> 16D/72D, 16F/72F.
>
> The curatorial staff cannot know all the more or less obscure
> self-published journals, so that the author should have contacted them to
> ensure that the designated specimens have been labeled as types. Presumably
> at that time, the misunderstanding that the catalog number refers only to
> an observation/sighting could have been cleared up. Obviously there has
> been no contact.
>
>
>
> As Doug already pointed out clearly, a holotype is a physical specimen (or
> at least an illustration of a specimen published in the original work or
> referring to such a published image). Since the catalog numbers chosen
> refer to a sighting rather than to a physical specimen in the collection,
> the provisions of the Code are not fulfilled in that Hoser i) did not
> designate a physical specimen nor ii) did he publish an image or iii)
> referenced to such an illustration.
>
>
>
> The fact that Thomas et al. (2014) had given the catalog numbers in their
> material examined section, may indicate that they had at least seen the
> picture(s) taken of the sighting.
>
>
>
> To make long short: Tough luck, Hoser! Next time you should ensure that
> you are referring to a physical specimen or at least publish an

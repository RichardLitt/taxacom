Alas, it happens all too frequently with organisms covered by the zoological code, too.  In my database, I have noted discrepancies between what was published and what I could find.  There are no consequences in the zoological code for an accident that results in non-deposition or even (purposeful) failure to deposit -- the publication is all that is required.  (In my case, it was a paratype, which has no nomenclatural standing, but when I got to the museum to which it had been sent, it was not there, and the curator to whom I sent it asserted he had no knowledge of it!).  Zoologists do not have isotypes, so I cannot respond to that, but under the zoological code, only in the original description can a holotype be designated, so there can be no such thing as a "new" holotype.

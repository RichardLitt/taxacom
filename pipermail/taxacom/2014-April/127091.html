Dear list,



Actually, I do not understand the clutter at all. Hoser designated two holotypes for snapping turtle subspecies and all the data he had provided in his original description are the catalog number, the locality, and the museum that provided the records. All the information provided by Hoser was given in the online collection database.



In the original Hoser paper we read:
?Holotype: A specimen in the Florida Museum of Natural History (FM) at the University of Florida, specimen number: 155266, from Liberty, Florida, USA. The Florida Museum of Natural History (FM) at the University of Florida, is a government owned facility that allows researchers access to the collection as laid out on their website at: http://www.flmnh.ufl.edu ?

and

?Holotype: A specimen in the Florida Museum of Natural History (FM) at the University of Florida, specimen number: 165801, from Alachua, Florida, USA. This is a government owned facility that allows researchers access to the collection as laid out on their website at: http://www.flmnh.ufl.edu?



No further description of the designated type material itself that could provide evidence that Hoser had ever seen at least a detailed picture of these specimens. However, as we all know, databases sometimes contain erroneous data. I am not sure if Hoser had requested further data on the designated species or had asked the curatorial staff of the FLMNH to put labels on these specific specimens marking them as holotypes for newly described taxa.



Recommendations in the Code, although not mandatory, not only provide methods/actions for good practice, but are also given to avoid misunderstandings and misconceptions in nomenclatural acts and works. Hoser had deliberately failed to meet the recommendations such as 16C/72D, 16D/72D, 16F/72F.

The curatorial staff cannot know all the more or less obscure self-published journals, so that the author should have contacted them to ensure that the designated specimens have been labeled as types. Presumably at that time, the misunderstanding that the catalog number refers only to an observation/sighting could have been cleared up. Obviously there has been no contact.



As Doug already pointed out clearly, a holotype is a physical specimen (or at least an illustration of a specimen published in the original work or referring to such a published image). Since the catalog numbers chosen refer to a sighting rather than to a physical specimen in the collection, the provisions of the Code are not fulfilled in that Hoser i) did not designate a physical specimen nor ii) did he publish an image or iii) referenced to such an illustration.



The fact that Thomas et al. (2014) had given the catalog numbers in their material examined section, may indicate that they had at least seen the picture(s) taken of the sighting.



To make long short: Tough luck, Hoser! Next time you should ensure that you are referring to a physical specimen or at least publish an illustration or refer to such. Accusing other people of lying isn't really the way science works, although, it may seldom occur?



Cheers,

Wulf

What is baffling is that Ken continues to assert a falsehood - that the
importance of dispersal is minimized in panbiogeography. Reminds me a bit
of American politics (perhaps politics in general) that if something is
claimed often enough it must be true. It is not. Hence the key
panbiogeographic formulation: dispersal = translation in space + form
making.

As for Madagascar, it is not about whether or not dispersal occurred, but
under what circumstances. Claims that much or all of the biota originated
via chance overwater dispersal from Africa may be reasonable as a
conjecture, but there is no analytic evidence in support (and molecular
divergence estimates based on fossil or island ages, no matter how recent,
do not .preclude earlier events involving continuity of land).


John Grehan

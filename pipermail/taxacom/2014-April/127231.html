Hi David, 

I am afraid that the second weakness cannot be addressed by adding mandatory metadata elements, because what we are looking at here is, in essence, an ontology. E.g., the same specimen can be identified in the field by the ranger, then in the lab by a forensic expert and then the photo shown to a taxonomist; each of them may or may not use one or many references for making the taxonomic assignment. Collapsing this ontology into the same flatfile with the specimen record is arguably more difficult than creating a separate audit trail pathway to track this history of taxonomic revisions linking back to the reference sequence (as a relational table). 
As you correctly note, different morphological character sets will be vital for identifying different taxa. As a mammalogist, I can argue that skulls are the most taxonomically informative part of an 'average' mammal, cheek dentition being the next best thing. However, this may not work if you want to retain info about geographical variants and will probably not work in most other vertebrate groups (let alone life forms that do not have a skull). You may have to come up with a large matrix of taxonomically informative character sets that will work for particular taxa (e.g., external habitus photo, lateral head closeup, primary feather, cheek tooth, etc.). Once you have it, you can require that each sample contains, say, two of these character sets documented. I am not sure how you can keep the system simple and yet informative enough for your referencing purposes. 

Best wishes 
Alex

This discussion immediately veered off-topic so I'll add some further information to try to get it back on track. 

A common complaint about online biodiversity databases is the lack of links to voucher specimens that can be re-examined to corroborate or refute the species name on the database records and the lack of quality control over species identification. 

The BARCODE data standard addresses the first concern directly by requiring a voucher ID in a standard format that will lead anyone with a question back to the repository, collection and catalog number associated with the voucher. CBOL constructed the Global Registry of Biological Repositories (www.grbio.com, a merger with Index Herbariorium and Biodiversity Collections Index), as an authoritative look-up table for InstitutionCodes and CollectionCodes. 

The second weakness is more difficult to address so the Barcode of Wildlife is extending the BARCODE data standard by adding several metadata fields associated with the reliability of the species identification. The include fields such as: 

- Name of the identifier 
- Date of the identification 
- Type status of the voucher specimen (if any) 
- Basis of identification (was the voucher specimen a complete adult, a pre-adult, a part, a tissue sample only, digital image only?) 
- To what was the voucher compared? Reference collections? Literature figures? Field guides? Morphological key? 

The question I posed is this. Assuming it is out of the question to have a complete study skin or skeleton or liquid-preserved voucher, what proxies could be considered as adequate replacements for a traditional voucher? Yes, it will vary by taxonomic group but assuming that we cannot kill and preserve a specimen, what's the minimum information and material we should require if we want to be able to corroborate the species ID independently from the barcode sequence? 

David

Yes, for animals U. antiqua would be the nomenclaturally correct name,
unless it happens to be a junior homonym or otherwise invalid.  Names that
were proposed as subspecies (and older names that were not clearly proposed
to be something lower than a subspecies) are regarded as species-level
names and have priority over later-proposed names at the species or
subspecies rank.  Similarly, a name originally proposed as a subgenus has
priority over a later name proposed as a genus.

As a more complex situation,  I have what seems to be a case where an early
author decided that a proposed new genus and species really belonged in an
existing genus and so demoted the genus name to a species name, dropping
the original species.  Does this constitute creating a new species name?
Does anyone have examples of this to show that it was occasionally
practiced?

Hi Jason,

Another colleague sent a good paper on the 'everything is everywhere'
debate - it's:
O'Malley, M. A. (2007). The nineteenth century roots of'everything is
everywhere'. *Nature Reviews Microbiology*, *5*(8), 647-651. 'The
identification of geographical patterns in microbial distributions has
begun to challenge purely ecological explanations of biogeography and the
underlying principle of 'everything is everywhere: but the environment
selects'.' It's a good review. Of course, no-one is suggesting that
dispersal does not occur, but chance dispersal/ecology does not seem to be
the explanation for these patterns.

You wrote: In regards to the range overlap of sister species, sympatric
speciation doesn?t have to result in broad sympatry. In fact it probably
will lead to the opposite result due to local adaptation'. But imagine a
species found in an area  of hilly rainforest say 50 km across that
differentiates by ecological adaption into a ridge species and a
gully species. Wouldn't you expect them to have more or less the same
geographic distribution?

Michael

Ross,



Its all well and good to claim that AM staff decided to victimize Wells but
its another thing to provide evidence. Making a defamatory comment like
this without presenting evidence of some kind is problematic, as is calling
it an ?act of bastardry?. Perhaps AM staff had good reason to exclude
access, perhaps not, but just saying so does not make it so.


No one on this list (as I my recollect) has said that Hoser is a dill (or
similar) when it comes to knowledge of the fauna he publishes on. The
current discussion is about the quality of his taxonomic work with respect
to type specimens and the code. Hoser continues to disparage in quite a
nasty way those with whom he disagrees. This use of language inevitably
makes me think that Hoser is a nasty person since I have no evidence to
contradict his language. Perhaps he is really a very nice person who
happens to prefer defamatory language about others. In biogeography there
are differences of opinion that are in severe difference at times, and yet
on this list all participants do, or make every attempt to, continue debate
in a courteous and civil manner even if differences of opinion are
expressed in a very direct way.



If the primary purpose of your message over whether Ray Hoser examined
specimens that are included in his descriptions of Macrochelys Snapping
Turtles is to highlight the point that access to specimens MUST be considered
a major factor, then one must ask whether Hoser was not provided
appropriate access to the Florida collection that led him to use a database
instead. I?ve asked Raymond about this in an earlier email, but no response
on that yet.



John Grehan

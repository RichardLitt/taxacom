> > Finally Doug, I think you should read the code you are a commissioner

> > behest of the author and within the (in our case in particular) heavy
> > constraints we face.
> This is EXACTLY what I mean by deliberately misinterpreting and
> misrepresenting the Code. No, the Code does not mandate the publication
> of photos or illustrations WHEN THERE IS A PHYSICAL TYPE SPECIMEN
> EXPLICITLY DESIGNATED. By omitting that latter clause, you misrepresent
> the Code. In your case, there was no such specimen. The Code DOES
> require that a holotype specimen exist, and, when designating that
> holotype, the ONLY thing that can *ever* be used in lieu of the physical
> specimen itself *for purposes of explicit designation* is an
> illustration or photograph OF THAT EXACT SPECIMEN. The authors who
> described vertebrates whose holotypes were released back into the wild
> included photographs of the holotype itself in order to make their names
> Code-compliant (and even that is pretty marginal). *You did not*. If the
> authors of those bird names had only said "We saw a bird, gave it a
> number, and released it, but here is its description" then THOSE names
> would have been unavailable, just like yours. They included photographs,
> however, so their names ARE considered available. Yours are not. This is
> really quite simple.
> > But talking code recommendations, it is clear that Thomas et al, have
> > stepped outside the ethics section, which I note you have yet to make
> > comment.
> No, they have not - that is specifically why they asked for
> nomenclatural advice - to make sure your names were unavailable before
> they published theirs. What is especially offensive about this
> accusation is that if anyone here is guilty of unethical behavior, it's
> you, for trying to "claim-jump" the authorship for these new taxa before
> the people who discovered them (as part of their phylogenetic research)
> were able to compose and publish a formal description. You almost got
> away with it, except you were careless about it. That is shameful, and
> if you don't believe you owe those people an apology, then clearly you
> have no shame. No one here is, or should be, weeping for you.

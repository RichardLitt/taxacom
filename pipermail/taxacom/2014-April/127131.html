Dear Stephen,

I have to disagree. Hoser's "holotypes" are specimens that never existed, because there are no museum specimens with the catalog numbers he stated. This was no lapsus calami. And his species-level taxa existed solely in his own mind, because he trusted an unverified (and unverifiable) sighting as being a live specimen of a clade actually constituted (determined if you wish) by other specimens. Without examining any specimen of his new taxa, and having as evidence only undeterminable sightings by others, his taxa remain purely hypothetical. A prediction, maybe... but hypothetical at any rate.

In addition, there is another major issue with Hoser's description. He provides not a single diagnostic trait for his new species and subspecies! All his verbosity refers to trends, averages, appreciations, and unspecified differences reported in others' work. This alone makes those names unavailable. 

Best,

Cristian

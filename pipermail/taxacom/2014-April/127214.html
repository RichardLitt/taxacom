Hi Curtis,

For me, panbiogeography is just looking at lots of distributions (of plants
and animals) and the regional geology, in detail.  The theory is secondary,
and the tools of the trade are good, big monographs, maps of your area, a
gazetteer and a good pair of boots.

Accepting mutation-driven evolution, or any other theory, is certainly not
necessary. But Nei's idea is very interesting, and it means that you don't
have to accept that particular morphology has been 'designed' for the
'good' for the species. Apart from Nei's book, you might like:


Lynch, M. 2007. The frailty of adaptive hypotheses for the origins of
organismal complexity. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Science USA*,
104, S8597-S8604.



Lynch, M. 2007. *The origins of genome architecture*. Sunderland, MA:
Sinauer Associates. (Especially the last chapter).



Michael

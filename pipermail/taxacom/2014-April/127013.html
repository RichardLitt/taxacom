Hi Jason,

Getting back to the facts, a taxacomer just sent me a classic that I hadn't
spotted: a chameleon on Socotra that has a diverse sister group
widespread through the Sahel (Mali to Somalia), around the Mediterranean,
Saudi Arabia and east to India (not on Socotra). It's in Macey et al., 2008
Mol Phylogen Evol 49: 1015.

This is the sort of thing you see in many groups in the Caribbean and the
SW Pacific. It's very simple to explain the pattern by an early break in a
widespread ancestor, somewhere around Socotra. If you want to explain it by
the usual dispersal from the mainland, you have to bring in other, ad hoc
hypotheses. But the main point is just how starkly and beautifully it
highlights the significance of Socotra, whatever the actual mechanism.

Michael

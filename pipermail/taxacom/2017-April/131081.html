After a first glance and taking character claims at face value I found of
the 133 characters that 17 were autapomorphies and 19 were found in both
the outgroup and ingroup. Together with two characters that were not
recorded in the outgroup, that leaves 95 that are putatively informative.
But there are quite a few characters for which there is no sampling of one
or two of the major outgroup taxa, and many of the remaining characters are
a mixture of different features combined together so that even though they
may all be in the outgroup, they are represented in different combinations
in the ingroup. I have doubts if this kind of combining makes much
phylogenetic sense.

I'm not going to take the time to investigate the veractity of individual
character state claims as it would take a lot of work, and anyway, when one
points out unsubstantiated characters the response is just to ignore it (in
hominid evolution anyway, which is like a Titanic that bounces off all
icebergs).

John Grehan

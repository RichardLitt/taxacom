Dear All,
      Until now I have never suggested what taxa might best be used to root molecular trees for the Mollusca.  But I have long thought the best choice would be members of Phylum Brachiopoda (or perhaps another lophophorate phylum Bryozoa).  If Bivalvia are actually the earliest molluscs, then the brachiopod lophophorates would be an excellent choice (since they too are bivalved).  One might also use (or include) bryozoan lophophorates in the outgroup to Mollusca, especially bryozoans with cyphonautes larvae (which are also bivalved).   Using such lophophorate taxa to root molluscan molecular trees would be better than using ecdysozoans (such as arthropods), because ecdysozoans seem to be a lot less closely related to Mollusca, and I strongly suspect Lophotrochozoa is actually paraphyletic with respect to Ecdysozoa (not sister groups).  Very small Brachiopoda and Bivalvia would have coexisted among the worldwide "small shelly faunas" of the Upper Precambrian and Lower Cambrian, and they would have been preceded by their unmineralized ancestors.
       Anyway, I just discovered a 2017 research paper showing Mollusca as sister group to "Kryptrochozoa" which includes Brachiopoda and Phoronida.   This makes me even more confident that Brachiopod sequences could be the best to use to root molluscan molecular phylogenies.  And the authors state: "We show that lophotrochozoans share many gene families with deuterostomes, suggesting that these two groups retain a core bilaterian gene repertoire that ecdysozoans (for example, flies and nematodes) and platyzoans (for example, flatworms and rotifers) do not."  Perhaps this also indicates that using ecdysozoans to root Mollusca could result in mis-rooting (and especially if the ecdysozoans used are very derived).  So if one wants to try exploring and thinking "outside the box", using lophophorates as outgroup to molluscan molecular phylogenies might yield some very interesting results, and even revolutionize our understanding of the evolution and interrelationships of Bilaterian phyla. Paleontological, anatomical, and genomic data strongly support the
conventional understanding.  Bivalves are not the basal mollusks, they are
not paraphyletic to other extant mollusks, and euthyneuran gastropods are
derived within gastropoda, not giving rise to other groups.  Radulae have
been lost numerous times within the Mollusca.  The paleontological evidence
points to single-part shells deriving from multipart.  It's not explainable
by misrooting, as rooting is based on including other phyla, not on
assuming a configuration within Mollusca,

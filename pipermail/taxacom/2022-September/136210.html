Hi John,

Without immediately considering the panbiogeographic implications, He &
Lamont's recently published paper "Ancient Rhamnaceae flowers impute an
origin for flowering plants exceeding 250-million-years ago" is certainly
very interesting; their main dated phylogeny is available here:
https://nam10.safelinks.protection.outlook.com/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fars.els-cdn.com%2Fcontent%2Fimage%2F1-s2.0-S2589004222009142-gr1.jpg&data=05%7C01%7Ctaxacom%40lists.ku.edu%7Ce69318dab3954c7ce5b708da8c4b8602%7C3c176536afe643f5b96636feabbe3c1a%7C0%7C0%7C637976553245409411%7CUnknown%7CTWFpbGZsb3d8eyJWIjoiMC4wLjAwMDAiLCJQIjoiV2luMzIiLCJBTiI6Ik1haWwiLCJXVCI6Mn0%3D%7C3000%7C%7C%7C&sdata=dlAE6awkK47v07JRdySuzlOrfCl1XfHXq7HEhqITNJ8%3D&reserved=0 . I
guess I am largely waiting to see what the published reactions are to this,
and whether it ends up being "accepted wisdom" or challenged by others, on
the basis that it indeed suggests an ancient origin for angiosperms, noting
however that it is calibrated by just 2 fossils that are dated to the
Albian i.e. mid ("late early") Cretaceous period, that are 100 (113)
million years old at most.
Input from others more knowledgeable myself would of course be very welcome.

John et al.,

This is a common problem in taxonomic papers.

Packer et al. showed a few years ago that only 2% of papers in entomological journals used a combination of all 3 relevant factors for species identification, namely citing the taxon concept and identification method used, and whether any vouchers were collected (they didn’t mention photo vouchers, unfortunately). The identification method was mentioned only in less than half the papers:

Packer L. et al. (2018)
Validating taxonomic identifications in entomological research.
Insect Conservation and Diversity (2018) 11, 1–12
https://nam10.safelinks.protection.outlook.com/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fonlinelibrary.wiley.com%2Fdoi%2F10.1111%2Ficad.12284&data=05%7C01%7Ctaxacom%40lists.ku.edu%7C589837243ae043e75e6308da98d992ea%7C3c176536afe643f5b96636feabbe3c1a%7C0%7C0%7C637990357505639815%7CUnknown%7CTWFpbGZsb3d8eyJWIjoiMC4wLjAwMDAiLCJQIjoiV2luMzIiLCJBTiI6Ik1haWwiLCJXVCI6Mn0%3D%7C3000%7C%7C%7C&sdata=MHQBrrt9kfBN5un9qVtQy1kyNkZRsyDDARLLFJwjq8o%3D&reserved=0

I wonder if similar studies have been done in other taxonomic groups, outside entomology.

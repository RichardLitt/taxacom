So Rich, as I suggested already, why not get the following rule formally put into the Code: 

 

*Duplicate records of a journal (including the original record) are all deemed to be associated with an archive from the earliest date on which any one of them was explicitly associated with an archive.*

 

The reason why I don't think it a good idea to leave it as an informal "rule of thumb" is that hardly anybody trying to use ZooBank to determine the date of availability of a name (or even just verify that it is available) will know to check for duplicates of the journal record in cases where the one actually used has no specified archive. They will simply and straightforwardly conclude that the registration was ineffective due to lacking an archive. Somebody else checking the same name, down the track, after you have done a merge, will find very different results! It is this retrospective "revisionist" approach that I'm not comfortable with.

 

Cheers, Stephen

On Tuesday, 6 September 2022 at 08:18:13 pm NZST, Richard Pyle <deepreef at bishopmuseum.org <mailto:deepreef at bishopmuseum.org> > wrote: 

 

 

Hi Stephen,

 

> If someone preregisters an article on ZooBank

> today, creating a new journal record without a specified archive, then the

> journal record used has no specified archive, so the registration is ineffectual

 

You can choose to maintain that opinion if you wish, but I would strongly advise against it.  Because there is nothing in the Code or any other formal ICZN documents (e.g. formal ZooBank policies), I cannot say objectively that your interpretation is incorrect (not can you say that my interpretation is incorrect).  However, we do have some guidance in the form of Art. 8.5.3.3 ("An error in stating the evidence of registration does not make a work unavailable, provided that the work can be unambiguously associated with a record created in the Official Register of Zoological Nomenclature before the work was published.")

 

Obviously, we're not talking about an error in stating evidence of registration in this case, so this Article does not directly apply.  However, it does suggest that the spirit of the Code is that inadvertent errors related to ZooBank are not to be used to treat works as unpublished in the sense of the Code.  If I were helping to draft the Amendment text today (with the benefit of hindsight), I would recommend an additional Article similar to 8.5.3.3 indicating that an inadvertent association of a registered work with an incorrect (e.g., duplicate) Journal would not make the work unavailable either.

 

Sure, you could be a stickler for precision and argue that until the journal record is explicitly identified as a duplicate of another journal, then Art. 8.5.3.1 has not yet been fulfilled.  However, if you want to play that game, then I should point out that there is nothing in the Code or any other official ICZN Documents that states that Art. 8.5.3.1 can be fulfilled by linking a work to a parent journal, and having the online archive "inherited" from the parent journal.  In other words, a strict reading of the Code could be interpreted that each individual article registered in ZooBank must directly indicate the name and Internet address of the online archive.

 

Obviously, such a strict interpretation of the Code would be disastrous for nomenclatural stability!  So it's not in anyone's interest to interpret the requirements this way.

 

Indeed, I know I'm not the only Commissioner who follows a rule of thumb whereby when the Code requirements can be interpreted in more than one way, then it's in the interest of nomenclatural stability to favor the interpretation that assumes a work or name is available.  Why?  Because the majority of taxonomists (who are not "Code Warriors") would most likely make that assumption as well, when confronted with an otherwise Code-compliant work, name or act.

 

Thus, from my perspective, the error we're talking about (duplicate journal) was made when the user accidentally selected the option to "Create a new Journal", rather than select from a list of existing journals. As such, the common-sense interpretation is that the work should have been linked to the correct journal at that time, and therefore the inadvertent error should no more "penalize" the work than an error in stating evidence for registration (per Art. 8.5.3.1) would have.

 

Again, your free to interpret the rules however you wish in this case, because there is no objectively "correct" interpretation.  But I would urge you to consider following one of the legitimate interpretations that favors nomenclatural stability, as I believe my interpretation would.

 

Having said that, ever change to every record/field/table in the database behind ZooBank is logged and time-stamped.  Thus, I actually could determine (and display) the date & time when a duplicate journal was merged with its correct counterpart (and the associated affected works within the journal).  I just don't think there is much to gain by doing so.

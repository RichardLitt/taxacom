Hi Ken,
Regarding the hippos.  Were the ones being eaten still alive, or were
they dead on the ground?
In Wood Buffalo National Park in northern Alberta, anthrax is spread
from one healthy bison from a dead one when the healthy one comes
over to sniff and lick at the fluids oozing from various places on the
dead one lying on the ground.
There is no eating, but there may be licking and sniffing.  I kinda suspect
that this is what happened with the hippos.
Robin

> the greatest).  Lots of birds (in particular) are cannibalistic, with
> siblings eating siblings, or adults eating the young or eggs of others
> of the same species, but disease transmission is probably low in such
> cases (in nature). The same is probably true in arthropods (where
> females sometimes eat their mates, or even babies eat their mothers).
>        But what I am most interested in is adults eating adults of the
> same species.  Hyaenas and monitor lizards, among others, seem to do so,
> but they have strongly acidic stomachs that would presumably tend to
> protect them from getting infected from such cannibalism.
>      However, today I found a VERY interesting case of hippos in
> western Uganda spreading anthrax, which was implicated, at least in
> part, due to adult hippos eating other adult hippos.  This is rather
> startling since they are mainly vegetarians.  The first wave of such
> hippo deaths occurred in 2004, and another wave occurred recently in the
> summer of 2010.  This is the kind of situation in nature that may be
> most instructive in avoiding disease being spread (by human stupidity)
> among adults of their domesticated animals.
>            ----------Ken

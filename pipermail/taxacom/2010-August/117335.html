Jason,
I assume you were talking to me in particular? You misrepresent my views. Think 
of it this way: there is an effectively infinite amount of taxonomic work 
needing to be done before we can fully understand the world's biota. Therefore 
we need to prioritize. The only thing I object to is wasting time and resources 
on molecular taxonomy of "cryptic species" when it is relatively remote from 
human experience/perception, only verifiable by people with access to the 
relevant equipment (thus alienating most of the world), and there is a whole 
heap of more *basic* taxonomy left to do in many groups (like mites and beetles, 
etc.)  Somehow, *basic* has become synonymous with *not worth doing*, and it 
looks better on a CV to have done molecular stuff instead. But *basic* actually 
means fundamental - the basis upon which all else is built. If this makes me a 
"creationist", then "praise the Lord"!
Stephen

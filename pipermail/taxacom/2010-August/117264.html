yeah, I don't think we really disagree about anything much at all really, just 
perhaps some very general issues:

>In the mean time, we need some sort of estimate to allow organisations to plan 
>the task ahead and to know where to put resources

hmmm ... I think we need to be careful here for two related reasons:

(1) organisational planning involves managers who are typically very busy and so 
need figures to be self-evident to interpret, rather than having to wade through 
the "fine print"

(2) the organisational planning involved is fairly insensitive to the exact 
numbers, so it is probably best to keep the figures as approximate and general 
as possible for this purpose (order of magnitude stuff), and make this self 
evidently so. Otherwise there is a danger of people reading too much into the 
numbers

Also the "when will it happen" response to a more definitive list is easy to 
answer: never, unless we aget cracking! 

There is a danger that these overall estimates of biodiversity may actually 
become surrogates for the definitive list and thereby take resources away from 
the latter ...

BTW, I think it is a very good idea to make the figures dynamically updateable

Stephen

Sure, Stephen.

If you look at the publication, and the text, it explains where the Minimum and 
Maximum come from and how the "World Described Accepted" was determined - in 
that case from Reid in the ABRS Publication.  Many times I made judgement 
decisions and I guess I will live and die by those.

I have tried to explain what the minimum figure I have found is, and what is the 
Maximum.  This publication is not meant to be a full study, but is a current 
summary of available (mostly published but some pers. comm.) information.  Sure 
- we can wait until we have a full lists of definitive names - but how long will 
that be?  In the mean time, we need some sort of estimate to allow organisations 
to plan the task ahead and to know where to put resources.

With respect to the Hymenoptera - yes - all are approximate and in this case is 
obviously an estimate as indicated by the rounding.  I could have added an "~" 
in front of virtually every number but advantage would that have given -  I 
think that is explained in the introduction.  Numbers for Insects have varied 
vastly, and that is why, in this edition, I have gone down to the Order level, 
and sought figures where available at that level. The previous edition did not 
do this for insects because the data was not available at the time. As a result, 
I think we have arrived at a much better figure and with a smaller error range.  
But only time will tell, and that is why I hope we can develop this as a living 
document so that we can continually update the figures. 


I hope, that over time, our graph will approach an asymptote as our figures 
become more accurate - in the mean time - I hope the publication proves to be of 
value.  One spin-off of the publication of this document is that many people are 
looking more closely at the numbers and as a result we have had a number of 
publications refining the numbers.

regards

Arthur

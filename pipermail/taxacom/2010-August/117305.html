 I´ll begin by saying that I fully agree that a morphological description in Zoology/Botany is necessary, but only because of the issue of accessibility and retrievability. There are many researchers who are using DNA data as well as morphology to carry out work and doing a good job I think. It is not a choice between good and evil, you can use either or both tools depending on the problem at hand. Sure, some people stink at it but there are cowboys in every field, no biggie. I think it would be better to argue that without morphological descriptions and background, the new species cannot be integrated into the existing body of work or be useful to those without the necessary facilities. 

So in my books a species may only be diagnosable by non-morphological traits and it doesn´t spell the end of civilization, but by adding extra information your description becomes accessible to all.

As for the paper in Inv. Tax., I don´t have access to it but it appears to be an opinion piece, so if you are opposed to the idea and have the time then publish a rebuttal.

And for those interested another good read:

http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/17135463

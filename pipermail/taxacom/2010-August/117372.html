  I tend to agree with this view, which prompts the rhetorical question, 
what is the benefit to knowingly excluding observations when inferring a 
phylogenetic hypothesis? There seems to be an unspoken assumption that a 
phylogeny is some sort of entity divorced from the very activities of 
making observations of the properties of organisms, for which a 
phylogeny has it's only relevant basis. This reduces systematics to 
little more than a cook book, knee-jerk reaction of following 
'established' protocols, without understanding why it's really done.

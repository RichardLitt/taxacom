Sure, Stephen.

If you look at the publication, and the text, it explains where the 
Minimum and Maximum come from and how the "World Described Accepted" was 
determined - in that case from Reid in the ABRS Publication.  Many times 
I made judgement decisions and I guess I will live and die by those.

I have tried to explain what the minimum figure I have found is, and 
what is the Maximum.  This publication is not meant to be a full study, 
but is a current summary of available (mostly published but some pers. 
comm.) information.  Sure - we can wait until we have a full lists of 
definitive names - but how long will that be?  In the mean time, we need 
some sort of estimate to allow organisations to plan the task ahead and 
to know where to put resources.

With respect to the Hymenoptera - yes - all are approximate and in this 
case is obviously an estimate as indicated by the rounding.  I could 
have added an "~" in front of virtually every number but advantage would 
that have given -  I think that is explained in the introduction.  
Numbers for Insects have varied vastly, and that is why, in this 
edition, I have gone down to the Order level, and sought figures where 
available at that level. The previous edition did not do this for 
insects because the data was not available at the time. As a result, I 
think we have arrived at a much better figure and with a smaller error 
range.  But only time will tell, and that is why I hope we can develop 
this as a living document so that we can continually update the figures.

I hope, that over time, our graph will approach an asymptote as our 
figures become more accurate - in the mean time - I hope the publication 
proves to be of value.  One spin-off of the publication of this document 
is that many people are looking more closely at the numbers and as a 
result we have had a number of publications refining the numbers.

regards

Arthur

As Bob said, it's not limited to molecular evidence of species divisions.
We morphologists often encounter cases where we see consistent morphological
differences, but don't feel confident that sufficient genetic isolation
exists to warrant recognition at the species level.  Would these two
populations interbreed?  Do they? When did they last? When will they next?
I see no conceptual difference between such morphology-based cases and cases
where genetic information reveals patterns of potential difference, but not
sufficiently so to establish new species-level names.  The FIZ paper
(rightly, I think) warns of the perils of not getting coordinated on this
reality soon ("this reality" being a new fire-hose of genetic information
that may potentially influence our interpretations of what sets of organisms
are best represented by Linnaean species names).  But I agree with Bob that
the issue is not limited to genetic data.

I am struggling to see the relevance of the Hirudo medicinalis candidate
name example used in the paper, viz "Hirudo medicinalis [Ca3 Siddall et
al. 2007]".  As one would expect there are oodles of existing old names
available for European Hirudo spp, and the sequence pointed to - EF405599
- happens to be of H. orientalis, redescribed in 2005 before the Siddall
paper in 2007, and described (invalidly) as a variety name before that way
back in 1962, and indeed mentioned in the Siddall paper. All 3 species in
Siddall are already described.

I think the authors should have pointed out this was an artificial example
and that the "candidate" species already has a name. In this case one
could say the old taxonomists were way faster than the molecularists!

This idea seems to be legitimizing molecular workers to avoid (even more
than they do already) doing the hard yards of investigating what is
already known in the parallel science universe of morphological taxonomy.

Geoff

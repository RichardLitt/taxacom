Dear all,

As many of you know, the Paris Museum is facing a tragedy similar to that
of the London Museum, with not only the moving of the collections to Dijon
(more than 2 hours from Paris) but also the destruction of the libraries
specialised on taxa (the working libraries of taxonomists) and the physical
destruction of books, periodicals and reprints accumulated for 250 years.

See the petition:

https://nam10.safelinks.protection.outlook.com/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.change.org%2Fp%2Fsauvons-les-collections-du-mus%25C3%25A9um-national-d-histoire-naturelle&data%7C01%7Ctaxacom%40lists.ku.edu%7C6d56eb3b361f4d19ac3e08db798df3cf%7C3c176536afe643f5b96636feabbe3c1a%7C0%7C0%7C638237424303223178%7CUnknown%7CTWFpbGZsb3d8eyJWIjoiMC4wLjAwMDAiLCJQIjoiV2luMzIiLCJBTiI6Ik1haWwiLCJXVCI6Mn0%3D%7C2000%7C%7C%7C&sdata=JKWzQElwq38xXhfM4qSsW7k0bV0SsyHSWzpDQsGf0mg%3D&reserved=0

> The hypothesis has been expressed that commercial governments supported
> museums because natural history knowledge could promote economic growth,

> I've tootled for some decades that if humanity is to live in

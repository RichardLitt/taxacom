Croizat correlated biological patterns to each other first, and only then
to geological features. Craw et al. correlate biological patterns to
geological features first, and then to each other.
To me, that is a big difference. In Craw et al.'s approach, it seems much
more difficult to arrive at general conclusions (about common geological or
geographical causes) that are not already implied in the premises.
Their major conclusion seems to be that the "major biogeographical regions
are (...) the modern ocean basins.". I think that proves my point.

Time for a few quotes again.
 From Croizat 1968:
(p. 117, about Corner's phytogeography): "Part of it (...) is steeped into
the juices of tradition, and Vansteenisian in its subservience to (...),
the unthinking stuff in sum which fills the larder of <<orthodox>>

(p. 165, about Backer & Van Steenis' account of Sonneratia for Flora
Malesiana): "Had a record of this kind been written, or checked, by a
taxonomist trained in solid biogeography (...)"
 From Croizat 1962:
(p. 129, discussing the interpretation of some floral structure): "Why not
bring in, too, pterodactyls, Ariovistus, "co-existence", the U.N.O.,
"Alliance for Progress, and the blessed like?""

But then again, it may be my imperfect understanding of the subtleties of
the English language, that prevents me from reading these statements as
simple expressions of well-meaning criticism.

>And to assign the same (Indian ocean) baseline to the tracks displayed in

>The distribution of Pelargonium (3-3 b) is massed around the Indian Ocean
>so an Atlantic or Pacific, or Southern basin could not be applied. The same
>applies to the distributions in 3-4.

This exactly illustrates my misgivings. Panbiogeography (sensu Craw, Grehan
& Heads) refuses to look at the independent evidence provided by organismal
distribution patterns. It attempts to force the multitude of patterns onto
a preconceived scheme based on geography or geology. In this case - a
scheme in which only four baselines are allowed.
If a track is not Atlantic, Pacific or Southern - well, then it must be
Indian Ocean. This way one runs the risk of ignoring the evidence (plainly
there in the examples) that there are different general patterns crossing
the Indian Ocean basis in different ways.
As far as I can make out, it is also a long way from Croizat's approach -
Croizat plainly refused to believe anything that was contradicted by the
distribution patterns he observed.

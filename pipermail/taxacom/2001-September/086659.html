>A fairly limited view of the developments in vicariance biogeography. Even

>1. It considers biological evidence independently from geological data

If this 'independence' is the only shared feature with panbigoeography it
seems to me to be little substantiation vicariance biogeography developed
from the method of panbiogeography.

>2. It tries to incorporate the extra detail that resolved phylogenies
>provide, especially with regard to the historical component (an expansion
>of Croizat's method, which has no way to deal with historical information
>of this kind).

Without a homology relationship between biogeography and geology I don't
see how this extra resolution can accomplish anything other than a
non-spatial hierarchical arrangement of areas or taxa.

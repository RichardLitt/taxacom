There is no continuum between "0 scientific data has been published
attached to an unpublished name" and "at least 1 piece of scientific
data has been published attached to a published name later found to be
unavailable". There is a difference between names that have been
published (available or not) and names that are so far not published.

When it comes to names that are already published, and which are later
discovered to be unavailable, I largely agree with you. But the case
we're discussing is not one of those cases. It is a case of "we are
going to intentionally publish a nomen nudum, attach a lot of data to
this nomen nudum, and hope someone else will make the name available
in the future", not a case of "hey guys, this name we've been using
since 1832 is actually unavailable due to a technicality, but since we
all know what species we're talking about, let's just continue as
normal and hope it's not a cryptic species complex, but someone should
really have a look at it at some point".

It is at best disingenuous to pretend that these cases are the same.

Otherwise, I could easily generate a couple of thousand genus-level
names, stick them in a preprint archive (thus making them visible to
others) and then demand that my colleagues should behave as if they
are available in case someone makes them available in the future. The
synonymy lists would be formidable.

Cheers,

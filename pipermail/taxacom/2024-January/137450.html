The name resulting from a change in rank is sometimes called a "nomen translatum", but, as Thomas said, such a change does not constitute a nomenclatural act under ICZN4. In the World Register of Marine Species, "superseded rank" is one of the reasons one can state for a name not being the current name.

Changing the taxon Taenioides cirratus (Blyth, 1860) from species to subspecies is a change in rank, which is sometimes given as "stat. nov." [i.e., status novum]. It is not a nomenclatural act but a taxonomic opinion (as Adam just mentioned).

On Sun, 14 Jan 2024 at 20:15, Thomas Pape via Taxacom <taxacom at lists.ku.edu<mailto:taxacom at lists.ku.edu>> wrote:
A new combination is defined in the Code Glossary as:
"The first combination of a generic name and a previously established species-group name."

Changes in rank from species to subspecies (or vice versa) will not qualify as new combinations as these changes do not involve the combination with the generic name.

/Thomas

> Tomiyama (1936) treated cirratus Blyth, 1860 as a subspecies of

With all due respect to Gary, I do not think "nomen translatum" is applicable in this case (or any others in the species- and genus- groups); it is applied when the name itself has been changed (i.e., different termination) to denote the changed rank. The only cases in which this applies is in the family-group, where the termination itself is indicative of the rank of the name.

I do not see "nomen translatum" defined anywhere in the Code, but in Hawksworth, 2010, "Terms used in Bionomenclature", it is stated:
-----------------
nomen translatum, [nom. trans.]: (zool.) a name that has been changed in hierarchical rank with a changed suffix.
-----------------

I believe the above to be the correct usage of that term.

Regards - Tony

The name resulting from a change in rank is sometimes called a "nomen translatum", but, as Thomas said, such a change does not constitute a nomenclatural act under ICZN4. In the World Register of Marine Species, "superseded rank" is one of the reasons one can state for a name not being the current name.

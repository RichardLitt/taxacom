> Is there an actual reason to make this a vox hybrida (half-Greek,
> half-French)...?
>
> My inclination would be to treat Uleiota as a latinization (with change
> of ending) of ὑλειώτης (uleiôtês), which Greek dictionaries translate as
> 'forester' -- itself formed from ὕλη (ulê), forest + -ώτης (-ôtês), a
> Greek masculine noun-forming suffix, used i.a. to form some demonyms.
> (In English, this suffix becomes -ot, as in 'Cypriot', an inhabitant of
> Cyprus; this derivation is consistent enough with Latreille's subsequent
> statement that the name means "habitant des forêts".)
>
> Although ὑλειώτης is clearly masculine in Greek, Uleiota "takes the
> gender normally appropriate to [its] changed ending", which makes it
> feminine under Art. 30.1.3. (This would be true even if Latreille had
> treated it as masculine or neuter, explicitly or implicitly, I believe.)
>
> Latreille himself subsequently treated the name as feminine ("Uleiota
> testacea") in this : 
> https://nam10.safelinks.protection.outlook.com/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.biodiversitylibrary.org%2Fpage%2F41892702&data=05%7C02%7Ctaxacom%40lists.ku.edu%7C6fca61b78934466481ce08dc1d018c9c%7C3c176536afe643f5b96636feabbe3c1a%7C0%7C0%7C638417139696993095%7CUnknown%7CTWFpbGZsb3d8eyJWIjoiMC4wLjAwMDAiLCJQIjoiV2luMzIiLCJBTiI6Ik1haWwiLCJXVCI6Mn0%3D%7C3000%7C%7C%7C&sdata=4tTQHvOd9dXttvpT7AbQJc4Y4oOdvV%2BEcY57sr2To9M%3D&reserved=0.
>
> However, Uleiota appears indeed to have often been treated as masculine
> -- e.g., see the many hits for that "Uleiota planatus" produce in Google
> -- presumably because it was given the gender of the Greek word which it
> was derived from.
>

>> du nom Uleiota, en français Uléiote, dans Hist. gén. partic. Crut.

>>
>> Uléiote [...] et nous en avons formé le genre uléiote (habitant des

>> « ule » est tiré du grec pour « forêt », le suffixe « -iote » est 
>> français à valeur diminutive dans la formation des adjectifs et de 
>> même origine (latine) que le diminutif bien connu « -ette » 
>> (allumette, lunette, opérette, majorette, etc. Cf. Oxford English 
>> Dictionary à « -ette »).
>>
>>      Le mot est donc formé sur le modèle de Pholiote (fr.) > Pholiota 
>> (Psalliota, Lepiota, etc., tous des féminins en latin botanique).
>>

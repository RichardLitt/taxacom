Hi David,
     I think of a taxonomic treatment as a work that is broader in scope
than just a classification.  Depending on the scope of the taxonomic
treatment, it can also contain descriptions, diagnoses, keys, distribution
data, specimen data, drawings, photographs, and so on.
     I suppose a comprehensive taxonomic "monograph" could be considered the
most complete form taxonomic treatment.  And I guess you could characterize
a barebones classification as a "minimal" taxonomic treatment, although
extremely important as a basic organizational tool (for data storage in the
present and data retrieval in the future).  That's my two cents worth.

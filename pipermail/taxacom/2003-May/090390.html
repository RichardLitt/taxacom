>I've run into conflicting stories on a sign used by entomologists and
>geneticists to refer to virgin Drosophila and other insects. I had learned
>from list members some years ago that the symbol was the same as that of
>the planet Mercury, which looks like the female symbol with horns--a circle
>with cross below and arc above, which in ASCII would look something like:
>
>U
>O
>+
>
>only joined. A correspondent on the Unicode list has suggested instead that
>the horns are actually a V, representing "virgin":
>
>V
>O
>+
>
>Thus, a virgin female.
>
>The latter story seems more logical, with the Mercury sign being used as a
>replacement in type because it was more widely available. Arguing to the
>contrary, I have seen in entomological usage a symbol for hermaphrodite
>consisting of a single circle combining the appendages of both the female
>(Venus) signs and male (Mars) signs. In other contexts, Mercury is often
>used to represent hermaphrodite, but a meaning of virgin in entomology
>would necessitate a different sign.
>
>The reason it matters is that if Mercury is the correct symbol, it is
>already encoded in Unicode, but if "Female with V above" is really what is
>intended, it perhaps should be encoded separately.
>
>If you have any familiarity with this situation, please let me know (1)
>which way you first learned it, and (2) any published accounts that might
>clear it up.
>
>Thanks!

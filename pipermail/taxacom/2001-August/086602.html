At 08:12 AM 8/23/01 +0200, you wrote:
>Apart from dinosaurs (and my kids were even younger when they learnt the
>names) virtually all bacteria are known by their scientific names. In some
>cases one uses "common names", but these are usually names associated with
>a disease - "the plague bacillus" or "meningococci" (associated with
>meningitis and Neiserria meningitidis). Otherwise there is a large circle
>of end users ranging from medics and the pharmaceutical inductry to your
>average yoghurt package (check out if Lactobacillus is mentioned).

And of course many plant common names used every day are generic
names  (petunia, gloxinia), sometimes misapplied (nasturtium, geranium),
sometimes anglicized a bit (rose not Rosa, lily not Lilium).  It's
basically an anti-intellectual attitude that leads people to demand
"common" names.  Hence my comment that it's better to educate the user
community.

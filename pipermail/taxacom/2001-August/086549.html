Much of this depends on the actual wording of the author in the original
description. If the author states that they are naming the organism after
only a man or woman then that calls for a male or female ending - which can
take several forms. If the author specifically states that they are naming
the organism for both the Douglases (who are mentioned as a male and a
female) then the ourm (of the) ending.

On a slightly different vain, I personally favor leaving individual
epithets as put forth in the original description regardless of generic
alignment.  I see no point in changing the endings to agree with generic
genders when every few years the species may be shuffled from one to
another to another. If it was originally a ...lata don't change it to
...latus just because some workers think it is now part of a Us genus.
Some things should be changed to meet a proper spelling - but unilaterally
properly spelled names should be left along.  This is the easiest part of
"stability" to institute.

Sirs,

For what it is worth, in the field of law generally, when one is trying to
interpret a statute (law) or regulation, if the meaning of the wording is
at all ambiguous (as it almost always is when lawyers get their hands on
something), it is permissible to attempt to determine the "legislative
intent" to resolve the ambiguity.  In other words, one is not limited
completely to the words of the rule (statute) itself.

Although consulting the individuals who wrote the law is one way to attempt
this, another, probably better avenue is to consult written records of
legislative sessions, including debates of those writing the law, prior to
its enactment.  Such records, if any, are more objectively verifiable than
what even the sponsor or writer of a law says happened at the meetings or
was the intent.  In other words, the "legislative intent" is more than the
intent of any one person, unless it is done by fiat without any input from
more than one person.

So, are there minutes of meetings, emails, or other discussions at the time
the provisions at issue were being produced?  I would think these could
validly be consulted in order to help interpret the ICZN, ICBN, or other
nomenclatural provisions.  Of course some things just aren't anticipated by
writers of provisions and in this case later interpreters are back where
they started until the next Botanical or Zoological Congress etc. (whose
minutes by the way could also be consulted for guidance if these decisions
are made at one of those conferences).

Steve Manning

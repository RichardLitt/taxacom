John,
     I have little doubt that Dietes was once more widespread---I figured
that pretty much goes without saying (unless some Dietes seeds or plants
were somehow transported all the way from Africa to Lord Howe Island).
     In my opinion, the most likely wider distribution was southern Africa,
across parts of Antarctica, to the Australian region.  The cause of
extinction in Antarctica would have almost certainly been temperature
related (which again should go without saying).
     What possible alternatives are there?   Africa through southern Asia to
the Australian area, followed by total extinction across southern and
southeast Asia?   Or island hopping across the Indian Ocean followed by
extinction on all those intervening islands?  Remote possibilities, but
extremely doubtful in my opinion.
      Dietes may well have arisen in southern Africa and then spread across
Antarctica and to the Australian area (although I would personally lean more
towards it having arisen in Antarctica and spread in both directions).   If
you can provide empirical evidence that Dietes naturally occurs in some
place like Malaysia, I will retract my statements and admit I am wrong.
Otherwise I will assume Dietes most probably once lived in Antarctica.  I'd
rather call this one of the standard "Gondwanan" tracts (not an Indian Ocean
tract).  Is it this sort of thing that makes panbiogeography so unpopular?
            ------
                 Ken Kinman
P.S.  I see no good reason to disbelieve that Lord Howe Island has been
above sea level for only around 7 million years and that its Dietes
population most likely came from Australia, New Zealand, or New Caledonia
(which are where most of its flora and fauna came from).  Maybe I am
over-extrapolating, but then again I think you may be prone to
under-extrapolate (especially when it comes to Antarctica).  I think science
advances more quickly with mild over-extrapolations than it does using too
much conservative under-extrapolation.  Isn't that what Darwin meant with
his famous quote about speculation?

>Although Ken is not sure about Dietes being a standard Indian Ocean pattern
>I can say with surety that it is because its distribution includes Africa
>and the SW Pacific. This is a common pattern for Indian Ocean groups. Its
>as simple as that.
>
>As to Lord Howe Island, it is possible that the specie did not come from
>any surrounding island, but represents local survival from a widespread
>ancestor that occupied Africa and the SW Pacific. One might argue from a
>stratigraphic point of view that Lord Howe Island is not old enough, or
>been above water long enough. These are geological theories that may or may
>not be true, and are contingent upon theories about the geological
>formations of the immediate area. Thus one cannot presuppose that these
>theories are anything more than just that and the biogeographic picture
>does not have to be made subordinate to those theories - whether or not
>Dietes is ever discovered to occur in the surrounding region.
>
>There is no biogeographic evidence (i.e. spatial homology of the known
>distribution) to suggest either way whether or not Dietes once occupied
>Antarctica. There is certainly no empirical evidence of it having
>'originated' anywhere beyond its current track.

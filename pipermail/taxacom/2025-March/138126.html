Geoff,



Interesting to see your feedback.  You conclude that the PNAS article and
does ?seem? [what does ?seem? mean?]  to be a very thorough even-handed
assessment of all the possibilities in their discussion. I agree fully if
?thorough? and ?even handed? means that its even-handed to misrepresent
fossil calibrated ages as actual or approximate rather than minimums, to
represent their six areas as having some kind of empirical existence rather
than fabrications (from where one knows not), and to be thorough it to
ignore (or worse be ignorant of) a competing biogeographic hypothesis (The
Pacific tectonic model). Yep ? if all those practices are ?thorough? and
?even-handed? then it?s certainly that.

Regarding your reference to humans having observed iguanas making landfall
on islands. That demonstrates nothing at all about allopatric divergence.
All it shows is ecological dispersal effecting range expansion of a species
range. No different to some bug being blown onto my vege garden (to my
detriment)

So, when you say that ?they seem to cover every prior hypothesis?. Well,
they don?t and that?s a fact.

Not sure what you mean when you say ?Don't go way up the classification.
Do that and anything is a possibility.? Not really. The point is that the
iguanas and their sister group is allopatric other than Madagascar. If one
wants to invent all sorts of wanderings, how is it that over all this time
these taxa are so allopatric, and as Heads notes, why do details of Old
World distribution match standard biogeographic breaks? Vicariance  origin
would give a coherent explanation that allows for subsequent range
expansion of either or both groups in what is now Madagascar.

That the Fiji iguana are most closely related to iguanas in America has
never been at issue. Not sure the context of your comment.

Altogether a great paper if one is into sensationalist fabrications that
leave out completing research findings. All very much the order of the day
in many biogeographic works

One of the popularist outlets included ?expert reaction? commentary ? from
a dispersalist of course. I suggested they include my ?expert? reaction as
well. This went to the Australia contact and they kicked the can to their
NZ office. Perhaps I am a cynic, but I am guessing I won?t be surprised at
the outcome (knowing what a closed shop the NZ institutional biogeography
situation is).



Cheers, John

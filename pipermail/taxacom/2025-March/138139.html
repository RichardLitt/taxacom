"  Clearly at the time there would also be considerable rafting required to
arrive in Fiji from the West or from the Southeast, as well as direct from
the Northeast (California)."

Which ignores the extensive former subaerial topography of the Pacific -
massive island arcs (half the size of Mexico for example) that spanned
eastern Pacific, not to mention massive magmatic plateaus (e.g. Ontong Java
Nui) and associated islands that could have been inhabited by a widespread
iguana ancestor with a range spanning the Pacific basin as well as central
Americas that became tectonically displaced through formation of the East
Pacific Rise. None of this was addressed in their paper - obviously because
they limited that phylogenetic age to 30 odd Ma even though there is no
empirical basis for that.

How do observations on dispersal in *individuals* relate to *clade
*distribution?
Every *individual* has dispersed to its present location, and so it is
imagined that a *clade* must develop its range in the same way. (Mayr
argued that every clade except very local endemics starts at a point and
attains its distribution by moving out from there).  But clades - unlike
individuals - can develop their distribution in situ, by vicariance and
without any dispersal, e.g. northern hemisphere Cupressaceae diverging from
southern hemisphere Cupressaceae.

  The break between iguanids in Fiji and their sister group, agamids, in
PNG - Eastern Australia is also seen in many other family-level groups,
e.g. the plant family Degeneriaceae of Fiji, sister to Himantandraceae of
PNG-eastern Australia.

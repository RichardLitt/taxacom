I'm inclined not to agree. If one wants peer reviewers to be only those who
know all the literature on any one subject then I think one may be asking
for the impossible. For example, when I agree to review a paper on some
taxonomy of my group of interest I do not pretend to have total recall of
the literature and all its content, and necessarily even then how to always
be able to judge what is cited and to what degree, and that's just the
simple case of taxonomic work. Perhaps in the future AI could be used by
journals to 'scan' the literature in some way, and perhaps at least alert
to any potential citations that may be addressed (or not). In this case for
the iguanas - I am not blaming editors or reviewers for missing content or
other issues. That is for the authors. And most biogeographers don't have a
problem with what I see is a problem anyway.

Cheers, John

Geoff?s latest comments in quotes below.

"That the Fiji iguana are most closely related to iguanas in America has
never been at issue." Thanks John. However, that's what the paper is about."

 No, that is not all that the paper is about. It's also about biogeography.



?Really, of course you are free to criticize?

Agreed ? I am free to criticize their paper for transmogrifying minimum
ages into actual or maximal to preclude earlier origins. I am free to
criticize the paper for analyzing areas that have no empirical existence. I
am free to criticize the paper for creating dispersal scenarios using a
recipe that cannot empirically distinguish between vicariance and chance
dispersal.


 ?but I don't think these authors deserve your scorn.?

Maybe, maybe not, but its irrelevant. Science is not the study of
reactions, but the study of evidence. And in terms of evidence this paper
falls rather short.



?They present their data, they discuss it thoroughly, and they include as
possible explanations similar scenarios to those you believe in even if
they don't use the specialist wording you would use.?

Actually they do not include ?similar scenarios?. They do not address the
allopatry of the agamid-iguanid sister groups - which is very pertinent
when inventing chance dispersal to supposedly explain part of that
allopatry.


?It's a molecular paper. Let's assess it on that basis.?

Did I not? There are hundreds, if not thousands of biogeographic studies
supporting vicariance using molecular evidence. Only in this case the
authors chose to ignore that.


?molecular data from a wider array of species might confirm their
conclusions or might have a different interpretation. This is the usual
progression, as we know.?

There is no progression in center of origin-chance dispersal approaches
based on transmogrified fossil ages, areas that do not exist, and recipes
that cannot provide empirically based evidence of vicariance or chance
dispersal. There has been no progress in center of origin-chance dispersal
biogeography from the time of Darwin, so why expect anything different in
the future? After all, center of origin-chance dispersal fails abysmally
when it comes to making any novel predictions about geological history, let
alone any that have been independently corroborated.


Cheers, John

Also, I think we should not have discussions like this here again on such an
important topic without being informed what is happening in the wide world
of open access.
A good starting point is http://www.earlham.edu/~peters/fos/fosblog.html
which also has an introduction to open access.

Finally, open access, for taxonomists specially, has only have ways to do
with a free pdf over the net: It is about access to content, content that
can be harvested by machine. This, for example would be one way to keep
track of what is being published regarding new, redescribed, synonyized
species, or simply to know, where which species occurs without getting
cilions of journals. One example, still very limited, is here
http://plazi.cs.umb.edu/GgServer/search where you could search for
Country=Colombia or any other and get a list of descriptions that include
species described from this place.

Donat

Yes, but open access is more complex than just you "author" have to pay now.
It also means, your institution does NOT have to subscribe anymore. So,
there should be a shift from the library to the author.

Furthermore, USD5K is on figure, something like USD1.5K more the norm in BMC
or PLoS, but the more successful journals in our field, Zootaxa as hybrid
journal where one either can pay page charges and make it fully open access,
or not and then one has to pay charges something like USD20 per page. As far
as I know, Zookeys, a Gold Road open access journal charges everybody, with
the usual exception in this field, ca USD12/page. Both Zootaxa and Zookeys
are businesses that florish, that is they continually publish and can even
have a market share of over10% as is the case in Zootaxa. There are also
other cases, like the publications at AMNH that are open access. Even if you
have as an institution to pay something to keep the organization running, it
is really to consider how many more people access an open access journal,
and that ought be ultimate goal of a funding agencies and the author.

Donat

Open Access can be lucrative to publishers. I recently responded to a
survey by Nature Publishing regarding a possible Open Access journal
they are contemplating. The last question was "Would you be willing to
pay US$5000 for publishing an article?" I replied "Laughable."

Only people with NIH grants would be able to participate in Open Access
if all publishers were this avaricious, grading to venal and mercenary
if payment helped ensure publication. Open Access can open a can of
worms.

Hi Robin,
 
         Agreed.  But the question is how many of those workers are splitting for good reasons, not simply because they were taught that paraphyly is unnatural and always bad (and that splitting could resolve the so-called "paraphyly problem").  One example of workers splitting for good reasons (based on increased knowledge) might be Platnick splitting spider genera (many of which badly needed to be split), although only time will tell if he may have overdone it a bit in some cases.
 
                     ---------------Ken
 
P.S.  Although most of APG's lumping of angiosperm Orders was a good thing, I am still hopeful that a few cases where they seem to have overlumped them (their bloated Order Poales for example) that they will eventually rectify those examples of lumping gone too far.

> Well, it depends on what organisms and at what rank. In general, I
> would say that the more you know, the more you lump. It was certainly true
> for angiosperm orders and families (which were terribly oversplit), once the
> molecular data came flooding in during the 1990s. Also true of many
> vertebrate genera and species during the first half of the 20th Century
> (after being oversplit during the 19th Century). There was move back to
> more splitting during the last quarter of the 20th Century, but that was not
> necessarily due to more knowledge, but perhaps more often due to a shift to
> a phylogenetic species concept.

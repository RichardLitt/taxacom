The Samyn and De Clerk paper contains a very important statement that perhaps deserves highlighting:?

another underestimated part of the problem is that most taxonomists sensu Joppa et al., that is ?those individuals who describe new species? can only devote a fraction of their time on the description of new species. apart from the multitude of tasks that refrains many from inventorying and describing biodiversity, taxonomists also face a hypothesis driven scientific system that is ruled by Impact Factors or other metrics. This reality seduces many taxonomists to publish an ecological, evolutionary or biogeographic story rather than descriptive taxonomic papers.?
The real impediment to the description of new species is not necessarily the availability of people with the knowledge and skills to do it but the fact that it is widely accepted in biological journals that a species name can be used without citing the original description. ?

A new species is an original idea in the same way that a new phylogenetic hypothesis might be - it is formed through the process: data, data analysis, results, discussion, conclusion. ?The hypothesis is: this sample (group of samples) is distinguishable from other known samples at a level that fits a concept of what a species is. ?Clumsy wording and I'm sure someone else can do better. ?The reality is that new species descriptions allow new work in all fields of organismal biology and help improve the understanding of ecology, phylogeny etc. ?

Many readers of this list edit journals. ?I would appeal to those editors to ensure species descriptions are cited to properly acknowledge the science that led to the descriptions. ?That way descriptive taxonomy will have the same standing as other branches of biology and citations, impact factors, H factors and the rest will all follow. ?There is an opportunity to put basic taxonomy back at the top table in science but we need to support ourselves and realise we have some control over that future.?

Action points: cite references to all species you use in a paper and insist as an editor or referee that such citations go in. ?

We are in an under-cited discipline and the solution is at least partially in our own hands.

Alastair

P.S. Catalogue of Life does not claim to be taxonomically authoritative, it is simply trying to offer a platform for component databases to offer their view of a taxonomic group, and to provide a management classification for navigation purposes. ?Wherever there are two taxonomists there will be at least two differing opinions :-)

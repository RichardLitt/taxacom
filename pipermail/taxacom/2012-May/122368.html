Sorry, ALL, but ...

The remark "... There is a lot of simple examples (most of mammals, birds, 
etc) where the species are morphologically weel-defined. But in others 
groups (worms, fishes, insects, etc), the delimitation of species could be 
not so easy. " clearly reflect a lack of understanding of at least INSECTS 
and their taxonomy.

Many insects have been and are still being described from SINGLETONs. I 
have, for example, a very pretty new species of Ceriana (Diptera: 
Syrphidae), a flower fly, from Central Africa. There is no question that 
this represents a new species as the differences between ALL previously 
described ceriodine flower flies are so obvious.

Why a singleton? Poor sampling. And this is a mimetic species, therefore of 
low population levels.

BUT given that our task is to try to enumerate the biodiversity of our 
world, then describing new species in groups like insects is and will remain 
critical.

On the issue of QUALITY CONTROL of taxonomic work, for me the critical 
factor is the traditional factor of HOW WELL did the author integrate new 
finding with old ones. Unfortunately (at least with insects) the answer is 
usually not well. The common approach is simply I have a wonderful, distinct 
new species, etc. and no effort is spent on assessing what other "wonderful, 
distinctive new species" have been previously described!

So, Today a paper that describes new taxa without proper and comprehensive 
assessment of all prior work / described taxa is poor quality, but common.

SECOND and at least for insects, many workers still do not understand that 
taxonomy is a SCIENCE, not a hobby like stamp collecting. New species are 
not new "stamps," in the sense of something different. New taxa are new 
scientific hypotheses.

Why is this critical? In the assessment of older taxa (that is, taxa 
described in earlier times) descriptions provide minimal character 
information. Fortunately in many cases there are VOUCHERs (that is, type 
specimens) to allow subsequent workers to mine additional character 
information. However where vouchers are not available, does not mean that 
those older/earlier hypotheses should be ignored. Simply that those 
hypotheses must be evaluated on terms of what was provided / published and 
resolved accordingly, etc. Not simply ignored as nomina dubia, etc.

Oh, well ...

We have a wonderful, diverse World. We need to make an inventory of this 
diversity before much of it is lost forever. So, yes, our samples may be 
small, but with what we have we should try to understand our biodiversity, 
which means integration of past, current and future work on that 
biodiversity.

Sincerely,

Chris

Hi Stephen, Jim, all,
?
Actually I prefer a conceptual demarcation I got from Rainer Froese some years ago ? there are clean lists and there are dirty lists; dirty lists accept names ?in the wild? as (for example) misspelled in the text of non-systematic papers, museum specimen labels, etc., while clean lists attempt to include only verifiable spellings/orthographies i.e. as in the original publication (or sometimes subsequent published misspellings also). That is not to say that ?clean? lists may not contain a residual small percentage of transcription errors but it should be an order of magnitude smaller than ?dirty? lists. So on that basis, IRMNG attempts to be ?clean? rather than ?dirty? by relying on notionally clean sources, although some bits of dirt do remain (in the main from a couple of early sources used which turned out to be less clean that I expected, but these are gradually being found and fixed, starting with the genera).
?
So according to the above categories, GNI, GBIF, OBIS, OZCAM and similar would be ?dirty? lists (no slur intended, that is their function) which can at least be retrospectively cleaned up (i.e. misspellings identified) by reference to suitably ?clean? reference lists, insofar as these exist.
?
When ?clean? lists disagree, for example as regards to the spelling or status of a scientific name, then that is a stimulus for a deeper investigation.

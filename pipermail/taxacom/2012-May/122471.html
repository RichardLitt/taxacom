Hi Geoff,     I see no problem with those three examples.  They are catchy, informative, and in good taste.  The trouble is that such trends in today's world eventually end up inviting low-brow copy-cats who stoop to being misleading or even vulgar (just to stand out and attract attention).  When editors allow that sort of thing to creep into the scientific literature, that's when it will no longer deserve our approval.  Not that I think it will happen anytime soon, but in a world that increasingly celebrates self-serving sensationalism which overshadows any higher purpose (information), such examples are bound to crop up eventually.                  ----------------Ken

The simple answer to your tongue-in-cheek comment, Paul, is that there is no secretiveness involved, just a time lag. 

As the new Secretary of the General Committee for Nomenclature, I have the updating of this database on my list of things to look into in the near future. However, it is lower down the list than such matters as ensuring that the new General, Permanent and Special Committees are all getting started on the next few years' nomenclatural work leading up to the next IBC, in Shenzen, China, in 2017.

This database has been very much Dan Nicolson's baby, and we should all be extremely grateful to him for setting up this convenient source of information on nomenclatural conservation and rejection cases.

My intention is for us to get the database up to date and keep it that way. There is a plan to list such names as an electronic appendix to the Code (now the International Code for Algae, Fungi and Plants, for those who haven't caught up with this change made at the IBC last year), so I will be consulting John McNeill, Nick Turland and others on the Editorial Committee, as well as the folk at the Smithsonian how we can best manage this work.

Regards
Karen

Yes, the "Proposals and Disposals" site at the Smithsonian
can be very useful, but the Malus domestica proposal is not
listed, nor are the actions taken on the Malus toringoides 
proposal, although this has been reported on by the special 
Committee and the General Committee, and has been accepted 
by the Melbourne Congress. The Malus domestica proposal 
was published in April 2010, so it looks as if the site lags more 
than two years behind? Perhaps an additional person should 
be assigned to keep it up to date?

Publishing the deliberations of a Committee looks to be a very
attractive idea, but perhaps for some groups of taxa the outside
pressure is felt to be too severe for such openness to be risked?

Paul

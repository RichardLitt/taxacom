There are an number of isssues with the Wikipedia philosophy that have
deterred me from contributing, although I use it all the time as a
reasonhable starting point for almost anything.

'No Original Research' is not one of these show stoppers - our own
data compilations such as APNI and APC work the same way, in that you
don't just make stuff up, you reference it and link it. If you want to
make stuff up elsewhere and reference it - that's fine, that's how
science works... (in spite of what Stephen quoted from the WP editor
earlier, I am pretty sure that Wikipedia does not require sources to
be in refereed works, but I may be wrong).

The big sticking point is that of 'notability'. Who decides? In
biology, and taxonomy in particular, every fact, no matter how
trivial, no matter which idiot published it, is notable and has to be
accounted for. I have a big problem with a clique of self appointed
editor, perhaps not even working in the field, deciding what's in and
whats out, both in topic and in detail. Deletionism is what kills
Wikipedia for me...

jim

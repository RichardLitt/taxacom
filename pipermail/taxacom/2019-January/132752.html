It is becoming evident that my first message wasn't very clear! I know that
taxonomists can name new species after anyone they like and it's nice to
name them after people who might benefit in some way/be eternally grateful
etc.

So my more specific question is:

"Does anyone consider a collector to be the discoverer of a species if the
collector did not realise it was new to science?"

So would it be correct to state in print "Ali discovered S. wallacii"?

Thanks!

George

On Thu, 3 Jan 2019 at 18:07, John Grehan <calabar.john at gmail.com> wrote:

> I would agree in general principle to the 'greater good' - whatever seems
> most appropriate to the describer/s at the time. There is no rule book. I
> have one case of naming a species after a collector who was not the
> original collector of the specimen, but who received it from another
> collector, but brought it to my attention. I noticed that some of the names
> in the article were compound names. One of my colleagues has indicated to
> me a disinclination for such as they are often long and multisyllabic,
> increasing potential for misspellings and challenging pronunciation. But I
> have done so in a couple of cases where it was a condition of the collector
> to access and describe the specimens.

Dear Richard,

Since it appears that nobody seems to have given you the courtesy of a reply, maybe I can give you my personal and very unofficial opinion of how you should proceed.

First of all, I am sure you have already analysed the taxonomy and determined which are the valid taxa that you are dealing with in your paper. The next step is to determine which names have been used for these taxa up to now. If there are none then go ahead and propose them as new taxa with new names. Where there are existing names then follow the Code in determining which is the earliest valid name applicable. If you believe that any of these names may be invalid then cite the chapter and article of the Code and the reasons why you consider the name invalid. Those names that are synonyms of earlier names in terms your taxonomic analysis should be listed as such under the appropriate taxon. Finally, reference all the publications used to determine these outcomes both in the text and in your references section. This way future workers will know that you have considered all the available literature at the time of writing. I don’t think that ignoring anything that you don’t like (or others don’t like) will do you any favours. However, if you can show the valid or invalid status of those particular names by  showing how they meet or don’t meet the Code then this will be a useful contribution.

I am copying my response to the two discussion lists concerned so that anyone who disagrees with these views has an opportunity to have their say.

Regards,

Lynn

Can I even list his entities named within the works since the year 2000, including even the bibliographic citation of the articles that form the basis of the Application at this time? Now I require an interpretation strictly by the Book (i.e. the Articles of the 4th Edition)...so what should I do?

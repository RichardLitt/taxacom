If a collector says he thinks it is different and they are known for knowledge of the local fauna or flora, I would favor giving him credit. It costs nothing to the person who describes it but might mean a lot to the collector.  New to science, yes, that is why the person would not get authorship of the paper (at least, not sole authorship).  To me saying "yes but the collector did not know it was new to science" is being picky and unreasonable. 
Mary

>> I have been debating with historians of science and others for a

>> was it Alfred Russel Wallace or his assistant Ali? They are arguing

>> great injustice it was named after Wallace (in fact I have been called “racist”
>> for arguing against this point of view!).
>>
>>
>>
>> Wallace wrote in his book *The Malay Archipelago* “Just as I got home 
>> I overtook Ali returning from shooting with some birds hanging from 
>> his belt.
>> He seemed much pleased, and said, "Look here, sir, what a curious bird,"
>> holding out what at first completely puzzled me. I saw a bird with a 
>> mass of splendid green feathers on its breast, elongated into two 
>> glittering tufts; but, what I could not understand was a pair of long 
>> white feathers, which stuck straight out from each shoulder. Ali 
>> assured me that the bird stuck them out this way itself, when 
>> fluttering its wings, and that they had remained so without his 
>> touching them. I now saw that I had got a great prize, no less than a 
>> completely new form of the Bird of Paradise, differing most 
>> remarkably from every other known bird.”
>>
>>
>>
>> As a taxonomist my argument is that while Ali collected the first 
>> specimen he did not know it was a species new to Western taxonomy. We 
>> are talking about species new to SCIENCE, not simply new to Ali. 
>> Unlike Wallace, Ali was illiterate and was not a 
>> biologist/taxonomist. Wallace, however, thanks to his in-depth 
>> knowledge of the birds of the region, immediately recognised that it 
>> was a new species. He excitedly wrote to his agent
>> Stevens:
>>
>>
>>
>> "...I believe I have already the finest & most wonderful bird in the 
>> island [Bacan, Indonesia]. I had a good mind to keep it a secret but 
>> I cannot resist telling you, I have got here a new Bird of Paradise!! 
>> of a new genus!!! quite unlike anything yet known, very curious & 
>> very handsome!!!
>> When I can get a couple of pairs I will send them overland to see 
>> what a new Bird of Paradise will really fetch. I expect £25 each! Had 
>> I seen the bird in Ternate I would never have believed it came from 
>> here, so far out of the hitherto supposed region of the Paradiseidae. 
>> I consider it the greatest discovery I have yet made..." (Wallace, 
>> 1858)
>>
>>
>>
>> So Wallace clearly thought HE had discovered it.

>>

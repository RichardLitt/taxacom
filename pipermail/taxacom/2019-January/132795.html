Many thanks to all have responded on list and off who have provided me with
some thoughtful reflection on my approach. Two issues have emerged in
addition to the one I raised. With the first, it is a matter of whether I
find sufficient distinction in a non-genitalic feature to go on that alone.
I have yet to see if that may be the case. I certainly agree with the main
point that it is better to have a series than a single specimen since there
is always the question of variability. In this particular case I have to
make a judgement call that may or may not be supported in the future. I
will have to make my best 'guess' and if it is not supported than, as noted
on this list, synonymy may result. I am dealing with moths (genus Aenetus)
that appear to have a low ecological dispersal capability in general (other
than the miraculous kind that may be invoked to explain their occurrence
thousands of miles apart) and I am dealing with specimens from different
islands of the Lesser Sunda. So if I find differences even for single
specimens on different islands it will be sufficient for me to predict a
different species (the large islands of New Zealand and New Caledonia each
have only a single species, but New Guinea has many).

Given their rarity of collection and advancing age I have determined that
it is better to name them now than not. Also, these specimens are expensive
(at least for me where hundreds of dollars are involved for any one
specimen) and I have been fortunate to have the collaboration of an
individual who can bear those costs and make the material available for
description. Its a sort of once in the lifetime opportunity so I am going
for it.

The last issue concerns gender. In this case for the most part the
specimens are of the same sex, but I have one island specimen that is a
male. If the females are all different on different islands then I am going
to guess that the male is different also even though I cannot make direct
genitalic comparison. At this time I also do not know how much palps differ
between sexes in this genus, but I am going to check with a colleague who
has worked on the group in other places.

Thanks again for all the interesting feedback. I am in a situation where I
do not have immediate contact with colleagues other than on the web so even
for such mundane and perhaps obvious questions such as this it is nice to
have that response.

John Grehan

> > I am a botanist, so I possibly face more problems with hybrids and
> genetically
> > abarrant individuals than zoologists generally do, but my view on this,
> both
> > personally and as editor of a major journal in the field, is that
> presumed
> > taxa only known from a single individual should better not be described
> and
> > named. At least as long as the single individual known has not been kept
> in
> > cultivation, observed for a long time and been genetically investigated,
> the
> > risk is far too large that a single individual with unusual morphology
> is a
> > unicum, possibly a hybrid or genetically defect individual, rather than a
> > member of stable population of a species.
> >
> > Furthermore, I use to argue that since it is impossible to know anything
> about
> > the range of variability of a taxon only known from a single specimen it
> is
> > simply not possible to provide a morphological description of it that is
> > useful. In my own research, I continiously have to struggle with taxa
> > described from very scanty material and I have several cases with
> species that
> > I feel I have to treat as if they have never been refound/recollected
> although
> > there are modern collections of specimens that may possibly belong to
> these
> > species but which are not identical in all respects with the original
> diagnose
> > and type material and which I does not dare to identify as members of
> the old
> > species (in particular not since that would have large implications for
> > various conservation measures.
> >
> > However, if you are publishing a revision/treatment of the relevant
> species,
> > it may still of course be a good idea to briefly mention and describe
> whatever
> > deviating specimens you may have, just to make people aware of their
> existance
> > if or when similar specemens are recollected in the future.

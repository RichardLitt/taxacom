Test plants needed for biological control project on common tansy.
 
A project to test possible biological control agents for common tansy
(Tanacetum vulgare) has been started, with funding from a number of Canadian
and US sources, coordinated through the Alberta Invasive Plants Council and
the Minnesota Department of Agriculture. Scientists at the CABI laboratory
in Del?mont, Switzerland, are studying the biology and host-specificity of a
number of insects that may have potential as biological control agents
against this invasive plant in Canada and the northern USA. 
 
As part of this project, we need to grow a number of plants (mostly
Asteraceae) to test the host-specificity of these potential biological
control agents. We have already found most of these, but a few species are
proving harder to track down. If any readers of BEN can help find seeds or
propagation material of any of these species, please contact Alec McClay at
biocontrol at mcclay-ecoscience.com:
 
Tanacetum bipinnatum (L.) Schultz-Bip. ssp. bipinnatum (Alaska, Yukon)
Matricaria occidentalis Greene (OR, CA)
Artemisia scopulorum A. Gray (western US: CO, MT, NM, NV, UT, WY)
Artemisia biennis Willd. (widespread, US and Canada)
Picrothamnus desertorum Nuttall (western US: AZ, CA, CO, ID, MT, NM, NV, OR,
UT, WY)
Hulteniella integrifolia (Richards.) Tzvelev (Alaska, BC, Yukon, NWT,
Nunavut) 
 
For more information on the project, go to the Minnesota Department of
Agriculture site at
http://www.mda.state.mn.us/plants/badplants/tansycontrol.htm.

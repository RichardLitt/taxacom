Hmmm. Stephen,

You'd do that without listening or reading about it?  That is tantamount to "My head is made up.  Don't 
bother me with the facts."

The Neuropterida is a natural group, so whether you consider it on a par with Holometabola, or as a subgroup, it 
doesn't matter.  As you know what is a subfamily today is a family in the making tomorrow (e.g., Family Thomisidae, 
with the subfamilies Thomisinae and Philodrominae about 10 years ago).  Today we have Thomisidae and Philodromidae,
and I have not seen any dissention among the troops on this.

Often what happens is that with more work done, the distinctions between two subfamilies become more distinct. 
I can cite other examples of this for the family Amaurobiidae.  What was once considered 1 family with a number 
of subfamilies is now a whole bunch of erections to family level.

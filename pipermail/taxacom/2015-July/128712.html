On Fri, Jul 31, 2015 at 10:35 AM, Fautin, Daphne G. <fautin at ku.edu> wrote:

> I agree entirely with Elena -- when students (especially, but not only)
> say "just sequence it," I point out that an accurate library of known
> sequences is needed, and they are almost universally dumbfounded.  A small
> detail seems to have been omitted in their training.
>
> Plus for the group I work on -- anthozoans -- no single gene has been
> considered diagnostic, and I believe that is still the case, so the
> "library" is full of holes and far from complete taxonomically.  The
> "universal" Barcode (what I term a capital-B Barcode, to distinguish it
> from a lower-case barcode, which seems to have become the buzzword of
> choice for a sequence), COI, obviously works only for animals, and then for
> only some of them.  It does not work for anthozoans -- yet I often receive
> a message that someone has submitted COI sequences to some resource (they
> may differ but they are pretty much the big players) and a match has been
> found -- so, presto, that is the identification. Yes, I point out, the
> closest match will always be specified -- but that does not prove anything
> other than the sequences are similar (not necessarily identical -- more
> alike than anything else in the database).  For anthozoans, few COI
> sequences have been submitted in recent years because of the known problems
> with the gene for identification, so that makes the "match" all the more
> problematic.

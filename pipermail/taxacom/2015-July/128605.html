 > > Hi Stephen,         
            
    My biggest problem with this
 > > classification (and many others) is that the
 subtaxa within a taxon 
 > > are alphabetically arranged, which automatically
 and immediately 
 > > affects their naturalness from the start. 
 Paleognathae is thus 
 > > placed after Neognathae, and worse yet Aves is
 placed before all the 
 > > other reptiles.  The Superclass Angiospermae
 is a mess, which for 
 > > some reason they only classify as a single
 Class.  Therefore, the 
 > > subtaxa are an unnatural mess.  Although I
 nitpick about the details 
 > > of the cladistic classifications of angiosperms,
 at least they are 
 > > fairly
 > natural (even with their unnatural rejection of a
 > > few very natural and useful taxa).   
                
           Neomycota
 > > is listed as Dikarya, so it comes before
 Eomycota.  And they don't 
 > > even mention the name Eumycota (which has always
 been more 
 > > informative than "Fungi").  The mammal orders
 Cingulata and Pilosa 
 > > aren't listed together at all, and how many
 non-mammalogists even 
 > > know what animals those taxa include?  And
 totally separating 
 > > Archaebacteria from their relatives Posibacteria
 is a horrible 
 > > oversight that has plagued classifications for
 well over 20 years.
 > >

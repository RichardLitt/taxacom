I have no problem with alphabetical sequencing of taxa at each level, in fact I prefer it. My only gripe is that the paper be seen for what it really is, i.e. simply a choice of classification, based in part on PRESENTLY available evidence. Also, classifications should really be specified from the bottom up. Top down approaches just push a lot of unresolved rubbish down to the lower levels and ignore it.

Stephen

 Sure, one could argue
 indefinitely about how to contruct a "better"
 consensus classification.  But these LITERALLY
 endless arguments are exactly
 why no such
 comprehensive classification has previously been presented
 FOR
 THE PURPOSES INTENDED BY THIS
 classification.  The value of this listing is
 not the "classification" part, so
 much as it is the "consensus" part.
 Consensus is not the same thing as unanimity. 
 You can certainly convey your
 personal
 opinions (as listed below) to the architects of the list,
 and maybe
 a future version will be altered
 to reflect them (assuming your opinions
 resonate with the broader consensus).
 
  
 
 In any case, sequencing of names in a list is
 an incredibly BAD way to
 represent
 phylogenetic information; ESPECIALLY in the context of that
 this
 particular list is intended for.  If
 you want to represent affinities of
 taxa,
 there are three basic ways to do it:
 
 1)      Come up with a mark-up scheme (like
 yours)
 
 2)     
 Acknowledge names at ever finer units of intermediate ranks,
 so that
 relationships can be represented
 within nested sets
 
 3)   
   Draw a cladogram
 
  
 
 For the purposes of this list,
 any sequence other than alphabetical
 (particularly if some sort of information is
 intended to be conveyed by the
 particular
 sequence) would have been a very silly thing to do.

 Rich

 > >

 past.

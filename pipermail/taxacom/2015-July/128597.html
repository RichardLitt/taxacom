Hi Stephen,                         My biggest problem with this classification (and many others) is that the subtaxa within a taxon are alphabetically arranged, which automatically and immediately affects their naturalness from the start.  Paleognathae is thus placed after Neognathae, and worse yet Aves is placed before all the other reptiles.  The Superclass Angiospermae is a mess, which for some reason they only classify as a single Class.  Therefore, the subtaxa are an unnatural mess.  Although I nitpick about the details of the cladistic classifications of angiosperms, at least they are fairly natural (even with their unnatural rejection of a few very natural and useful taxa).                              Neomycota is listed as Dikarya, so it comes before Eomycota.  And they don't even mention the name Eumycota (which has always been more informative than "Fungi").  The mammal orders Cingulata and Pilosa aren't listed together at all, and how many non-mammalogists even know what animals those taxa include?  And totally separating Archaebacteria from their relatives Posibacteria is a horrible oversight that has plagued classifications for well over 20 years.                            ---------------Ken----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

> The problem with it being "followed in future by both ITIS and Catalogue of Life" is that new developments, based on scientific evidence, will not be considered (at least not until some big revised version of the present paper is published). So, bioinformatics and science part ways for a while. It is really just a case of "choose your own classification", and if you wish to choose to follow Ruggiero et al.'s paper, you can, but there may be better alternatives.

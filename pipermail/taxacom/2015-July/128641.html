Richard,

I can appreciate that there can be differences in understanding of a term.
My little dictionary here (American Heritage) gives for consensus 1)
collective opinion, 2) general agreement or accord. The first is definitely
my understanding of consensus - a collective opinion and by collective to
mean everyone (one sees this in politics where reaching consensus only
refers to everyone buying into a decision). The second reference to
'general' agreement may be more problematic as 'general' may mean not
everyone - but in such cases how general becomes general?

With a consensus cladogram the operation leaves unresolved conflicting
trees (whatever the criteria) where a choice cannot confidently be made for
any one of the conflicting trees. In that operation there is no 'consensus'
of opinion other than by said criteria a choice cannot be made.

Semantics perhaps, but science lives through language.

John Grehan

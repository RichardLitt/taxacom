Hi Stephen,

Speaking as one with a general interest in higher classification of
organisms, and as a taxonomic database manager in particular, I am very
glad Ruggiero et al. have come out with a comprehensive product that I and
others like me can at least consider for alignment of our systems into some
degree of interoperability. When I started my main IRMNG database in 2006,
the most recent offering was Parker et al.'s "Synopsis and Classification
of Living Organisms" which was already almost 25 years out-of-date. Having
something considered, current and comprehensive (at least for living taxa)
and also with a fair degree of intellectual weight behind it (consider the
list of authors as well as the cited construction process) is a definite
assistance to the organisation of taxonomic information and one which has
been awaited for many years. At least for now there is a standard (official
or unofficial) towards which taxonomic information managers and users can
gravitate, much as the APGIII system has become a de facto standard for an
increasing number of herbaria and plant information systems around the
world.

Of course that does not mean that one day there will not be an APG IV or
CoL 2 system, that is inevitable, but for now we at least have a system to
be going on with. I suspect users will be voting with their feet on this
one since it removes the need to research and maintain hundreds of
individual/system specific classifications out there.

Just my 2 cents worth, of course...

Regards - Tony

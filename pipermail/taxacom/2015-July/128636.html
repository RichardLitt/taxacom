Interesting that since it is a majority view according to Karen, that the
classification is actually not agreed upon by all of the participants.
Hence it is not a consensus classification at all. The only consensus would
seem to be by the minority views agreeing to subsume their disagreement/s
in favor of the majority. So the classification is a representation of a
majority of the participants it would seem.

Consensus is often misapplied is systematics - in my opinion. Even for
something that is claimed by some to be a systematic fact (and therefore
unassailable) there may not be consensus. To use my favorite example, there
is no consensus that chimps and humans are sister taxa. The number of
people who disagree may number less than the number of digits on one's
hands, but its still not a consensus. There may, however, be a consensus
among smaller coalitions such molecular practitioners as I have not seen a
single one who has opposed the chimp theory of relationship (but if anyone
has found an exception please let me know!).

John Grehan

Rich,
Stability in classification is not something that can be imposed for convenience, however convenient it may be. Rather, we hope that stability will result in the long run from convergence of scientific evidence. Of course there is an element of subjectivity (lumping/splitting) in higher classification, which is a further source of classificational "diversity". Most likely, Ruggiero et al. will just contribute further to instability by providing yet another classification that some people will choose to follow and others won't.
Stephen

 Hi Stephen,
 
 Again, you seem to continue to misunderstand the purpose of
 the CoL Consensus classification.  Yes, it is indeed
 intended to represent a "standard" of sorts (although I'm
 not sure exactly what you mean by "official"), and the POINT
 is that it is STABLE and adopted by a wide array of data
 content providers.  The point is NOT to represent the
 most recent speculation about evolutionary/phylogenetic
 relationships (I chose the word "speculation" here to be
 deliberately provocative, and in line with the gist of
 recent excellent posts by Pierre and Robin, but you can
 substitute the more science-y "hypothesis" if it makes you
 feel better).  
 
 In this context, the only way an alternate classification
 could be "better" is if it is either: 1) MORE stable (i.e.,
 LESS responsive to changes in available
 phylogenetic/evolutionary evidence); or 2) MORE widely
 adopted by biodiversity information content providers. 
 Note that the criteria for "better" in this context does NOT
 include "reflects the most recent evidence concerning
 evolutionary/phylogenetic relationships".  Indeed,
 applying such a criterion to the CoL Consensus
 Classification would actually make it WORSE in this context,
 if doing so caused it to be either 1) less stable, or 2)
 less widely adopted.
 
 Obviously, the "consensus" part is important, and generally
 implies that a majority of contributing experts feels that
 it represents the "least of evils" approach to representing
 roughly the general evolutionary/phylogenetic relationships;
 but I hope by now you will understand that this is not the
 primary purpose of the consensus classification (as
 explicitly stated in the abstract of the Ruggiero et al.
 publication.
 
 I have now made this same point several times on this forum,
 and I honestly cannot think of any other way to make it more
 clear.  If the virtues of a stable, widely adopted
 general classification at ranks of Kingdom to Order are not
 useful to you, then by all means, don't use it!  Those
 of us who need a stable and widely-adopted generalized
 higher (Kingdom through Order) classification to facilitate
 exchanging biodiversity data remain quite pleased that this
 is now in published form!

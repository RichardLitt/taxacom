 Not that well said, actually! The term "natural
 classification" refers to a
 classification based on evolutionary/phylogenetic
 relationships rather than
 on subjective judgements of similarity and/or "groups of
 convenience". As I
 said earlier in this thread, one is free to choose a
 classification, and
 that of Ruggiero et al. is a choice one may choose, but
 there could be
 problems resulting from sticking too rigidly to it for too
 long, that's all.
 There may be better alternatives. I would really hate to see
 it adopted as
 some kind of "official standard", when all it is is one
 suggestion.

  all classifications are concepts - there is no 
 classification without a
 classifier - there is no "natural"
  classification standing in itself out there in the
 natural  wild
 (proponents of "natural" classifications should 
 meticulously explain what
 they mean)
  
  useful classifications serve communication purposes
 (talking  of things or
 'objects'  by meaningful classes rather  than on by
  one)
  
  many classifications can be useful, provided that they
 fit  their goals
 (what kind of information do we want to convey
  ?) and their specification (what are the desired
 properties  of such a
 classification, given its goals ?)
  
  in the present case, the classification of Ruggiero et al.
  should be evaluated according to its specific goals
 (some  kind of informal
 "majority rule consensus" for practical  purposes of
 stability on a 5-years
 basis)
  
  criticisms in terms of "scientificity" or "naturalness"
 are  largely
 missing the point
  
  Best,
  Pierre

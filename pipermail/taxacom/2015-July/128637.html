Data bases of classified taxa are valuable, but tend to be monolithic because of the problems involved in reflecting the ever-changing results of new study and new opinion. Consensus or what passes for consensus is thus at least a temporary target if not a strongly defended goal. 

One way to deal with this is to, yes, do a monolithic data set with "best" classification, but make sure that every taxonomist has a "kit" of critical floristic and faunistic literature for every region, published by a variety of authors. In particular, new taxonomists often ask around to see if out-of-print literature is available. 

It is possible that, given the present biodiversity emergency, an organization like a United Nations Environmental Protection Agency might buy the copyrights to out-of-print taxonomic works considered absolutely necessary for biodiversity research for each world region, and make them available in PDF form.  Thus, new taxonomists would still have to pay for in-print volumes but would be given free PDFs for those difficult to obtain books they need.

This blunts the objection that consensus must rule taxonomy, and puts the onus on publishers who will not reprint major works because the costs would exceed projected net sales.

Exactly, Dick.  This is intended as a pragmatic way to arrange/link information about all organisms so that the information can be made available electronically, no more nor less than that.

It is unrealistic to think that such a classification can reflect all of the most up-to-date research findings - or that it can be changed very frequently, given the intellectual and keyboard effort needed to make such changes carefully to avoid losing links to the information.

Congratulations to my former colleagues at Species 2000 and ITIS for producing this updated classification for the Catalogue of Life (and for use by any other digital system that needs one).

For those picking holes in certain areas of it: you could help to improve future versions by getting involved with the project and contributing your expertise.
Just a thought.

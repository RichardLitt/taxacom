Hi all,

A couple of observations:

First, as Richard Pyle noted, consensus does not necessarily mean
unanimity.  If so, then most political bills would never be passed.  Think
of 'consensus trees"; these reflect areas of agreement among competing
views and leave the differences largely unresolved.

Second, perhaps I missed something in Stephen's last note; isn't any
classification (except for those that are purely fanciful) limited by what
is presently known?

Third, I haven't read the paper, but comments made by several participants
certainly come close to reflecting the thinking of numerical taxonomists,
whose classifications were based on overall patterns of agreement among all
characters available (or all characters used in the particular analysis).
These classifications were intended, in keeping with ideas expressed by
Gilmour, to be useful and, perhaps, serve as the basis for inferring
components of evolutionary relationships (although this was not the goal).
Based on what I've read here, the new classification attempts to provide a
useful consensus.

Cheers,

Dick J

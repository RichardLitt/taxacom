>> Hello again,
>> 
>> I very much welcome this debate and appreciate all of the comments and
>> perspectives that have been shared over the past 2-3 weeks (even in cases
>> where I disagree, I have found that the discussion has brought up several
>> points of views that I either did not know or had not thought about in that
>> particular way). Thus, at least on a personal level I want to thank anyone
>> that has contributed.
>> 
>> And I realize that in my previous emails I did not state my preference,
>> which is pretty much to ignore the gender agreement. I just do not think
>> that this is the key point or the main problem (I may be wrong on that
>> assumption, I am not claiming here that I have THE correct position!). But,
>> in my opinion, having master lists for taxa (and having those lists
>> compiled by experts working together) would solve the vast majority of
>> those problems, and then some more.
>> 
>> Scott wrote that " Its not about making lists, lists are easy and anyone
>> can do that." There, I respectfully disagree. If list were that easy and
>> anyone could do that, how come they are not available for many taxa? How
>> come we are not even able to answer such a "simple" question like "how many
>> wasp species are"? [You can change "wasp" for "beetles", "moths" and pretty
>> much any hyperdiverse taxon that you like]. And how come when one looks
>> into the many websites, databases and many biodiversity aggregators that
>> have been mentioned here, the data for the hyperdiverse taxa is either very
>> incomplete or even worse: misleading? It is not that easy to compile that
>> info, no matter how smart, skilful or passionate IT people are, and unless
>> we get the wider taxonomic community involved and committed to get those
>> master lists, we will continue the same way, doing some progress (like Rich
>> Pyle and others here are doing) but still missing the mark... And yes, more
>> money and resources would certainly help, but it would also help to get the
>> community that so lively discusses here more involved on things like that.
>> 
>> Of course, lists are not magical resources that would solve all problems,
>> but I think they would help with things like gender agreement (and
>> misspellings, as someone else said today, and other similar issues).
>> 
>> In the end I am happy to follow what the ICZN decides (hopefully based on
>> the input of as many colleagues as possible). And so far I have dutifully
>> followed the gender agreement even though I would prefer not to (in that
>> way I am a "pseudolepidopterist", at least I can claim that my study group
>> is the main group of Lep parasitoids! ;-)
>> 
>> But what I think the last few days of discussion here show is that perhaps
>> we could channel all of those ideas (and enthusiasm!?) to take even bigger
>> steps... At least I want to end my day today thinking that could be
>> possible. Once again, thanks to everyone for the shared wisdom.
>> 
>> Cheers,
>> Jose

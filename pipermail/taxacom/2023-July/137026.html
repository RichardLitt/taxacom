The mentioning of AI in various messages made me curious what ChatGPT currently thinks about naming species and gender agreement. And if the current state of AI (which our field currently really does not use) could be of any help. Here is a small conversation I thought is fun to read:


> How would I name the species Abrolophus pulcher (Canestrini, 1885) when I place it in the mite genus Grandjeanella?

ChatGPT: If you want to rename the species Abrolophus pulcher and place it in the mite genus Grandjeanella, you would follow the standard binomial nomenclature format. The new scientific name for the species would be Grandjeanella pulcher. Please note that the process of renaming a species and assigning it to a new genus should ideally be performed by taxonomic experts and published in scientific literature after careful examination and consideration of relevant evidence.

> How would I name Lytarmes viatrix (Schulz, 1906) when I consider it to be a subspecies of Lytarmes fasciatus instead?

ChatGPT: If you consider Lytarmes viatrix to be a subspecies of Lytarmes fasciatus, you would use the trinomial nomenclature to indicate the subspecies. The revised scientific name would be Lytarmes fasciatus viatrix. Again, it's important to note that taxonomic decisions should ideally be made by experts in the field and published in scientific literature after thorough examination and consideration of relevant evidence.

> Is gender agreement needed for the epithet viatrix in this case?

ChatGPT: No, gender agreement is not necessary for the specific epithet "viatrix" in this case because it remains the same regardless of the gender of the species or subspecies. The specific epithet "viatrix" is a Latin word that does not change based on gender. Therefore, whether the species or subspecies is male, female, or of indeterminate gender, the specific epithet remains unchanged as "viatrix."

> I was considering to call the subspecies Lytarmes fasciatus viator (Smith, 1863) instead. Do you think that is wrong?

ChatGPT: I apologize for any confusion caused. If you intend to name the subspecies as "Lytarmes fasciatus viator," you would indeed need to ensure gender agreement with the specific epithet. In this case, "viator" would be the masculine form of the specific epithet, whereas "viatrix" would be the feminine form. If you consider the subspecies to be male, "Lytarmes fasciatus viator" would be an appropriate name. However, if you consider the subspecies to be female, "Lytarmes fasciatus viatrix" would be the correct form. It's important to note that taxonomic decisions should be made in accordance with established guidelines and preferably validated by experts in the field. Additionally, any proposed taxonomic changes should be published in scientific literature to ensure recognition and dissemination within the scientific community.

> Thanks. What gender does the specific epithet fasciatus have in this case?

ChatGPT: The specific epithet "fasciatus" is a Latin word and, in general, does not have inherent gender. In Latin, nouns can have different endings depending on the gender of the word, but the specific epithet "fasciatus" is a neuter noun. Neuter nouns in Latin do not have gender-specific endings. Therefore, in the case of "Lytarmes fasciatus," the specific epithet "fasciatus" does not indicate a specific gender. It remains the same regardless of whether it is applied to a species or a subspecies.



The AI knows quite well about the code and its rules, but the application of gender agreement (I assume mixed with an organisms gender) and Latin needs improvements.

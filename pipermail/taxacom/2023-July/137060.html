Tricholoma album (a mushroom)

Correct spelling or not?

Who will be faster to answer this question: 

A. Those who have a basic knowledge of Latin and Greek and follow the rule of gender agreement, or 

B. Those who advocate that one should apply the orthography of the first publication of the name?

My prediction is that (A) should take at best a few seconds, whereas (B) could take anywhere from one to many minutes, depending whether you know how and where to search.

Don't forget that both (A) and (B) constitute rules that have to be followed. And when it comes to rules, if nothing else matters, the one which is more practical should be followed. And what is more practical than saving time by being predictive and resolvable without having to consult the (not always readily available) literature? (And by the way, that literature is often in Latin... or in a typeset many of us will have difficulties to read)

Abandoning (A) in favor of (B) would be getting rid of one rule but adopting another one that is for sure more confusing and more time consuming, because everytime you have the feeling a termination is wrong, you would have to go back to the protologue to check. And we all know that misspellings occur in almost every resource and publication.

By the way, it is not necessary to actually know or learn the basics of Latin and Greek to understand rules of nomenclature. Just download the PDF of Stearn's Botanical Latin here: https://nam10.safelinks.protection.outlook.com/?url=https%3A%2F%2Farchive.org%2Fdetails%2FBOTANICALLATINWILLIAMSTEARN&data=05%7C01%7Ctaxacom%40lists.ku.edu%7C5a0e2b78df454108103b08db7e142683%7C3c176536afe643f5b96636feabbe3c1a%7C0%7C0%7C638242397232271419%7CUnknown%7CTWFpbGZsb3d8eyJWIjoiMC4wLjAwMDAiLCJQIjoiV2luMzIiLCJBTiI6Ik1haWwiLCJXVCI6Mn0%3D%7C3000%7C%7C%7C&sdata=PYfIOQnWNokxehBUVieb9XrB2ECMzzhOUsMv2su2crM%3D&reserved=0
The rules apply equally to zoological nomenclature and practically all cases are explained, in a way easy to understand. Checking something in here is still faster than going back to the protologue for each name, and it is predictive, while protologue checking is not.

This also seems to miss the point.

The point, as I understand it, is that instead of having A-us b-us (for
instance) as a universal pattern within a genus, you would have to remember (or look up) whether it is A-us b-us, A-us b-um, A-us b-e, A-us b-a, etc., individually for each species. There would be no way to ever predict what the name ends in for anyone, regardless of whether you had a classical education, speak a Romance language, or just like Latin. It would all be based on whatever someone thought was the prevailing usage within the group at an arbitrarily chosen point in time.

I don't care much either way when it comes to gender agreement but this seems to be unnecessarily complicated for no clear reason. It reminds me of nothing so much as when some phylocoders got the great idea to freeze 
genus+species for each taxon so that the genus name was preserved even
if the species changed clades, and there would be no predictability of relationship based on the name that is used.

Cheers,
Daniel

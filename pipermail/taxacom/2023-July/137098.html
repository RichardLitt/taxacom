 Jared,
Just for clarity, my argument is that whatever the outcome in terms of the Code, taxonomists will continue to do whatever they like about gender agreement and other Code related issues. Many of them choose to follow the Code, but many will, reasonably enough, consider gender agreement to be an unimportant little nomenclatural technicality and will either make only a token effort to respect it, or disregard it altogether. Hence, my view is that we need a robust coping system for actual taxonomic practice. This is more important than adherance to somewhat idealistic notions of respecting traditions with little or no modern relevance. So, my point was that spelling variants due to gender are just a subset of other alternative names, combinations, misspellings, synonyms, etc., of a taxon. All we really need is a robust and efficient way of tracking these alternative names and spelling variants in a way that keeps together published information about a taxon. Hence my other point about making sure that anyone who publishes information associated with a taxon specifies the original combination (basionym in botany) of whatever combination that they prefer to use.

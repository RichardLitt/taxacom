I've added:

1) Sentiment analysis of ResearchGate comments on Guedes et al.'s (2023)
proposal to eliminate all eponymous organismal names. There is an
eye-watering 95% opposed reaction among RG scientists.

2) Consideration of the gateway effects of eponymous names -- e.g., their
popularity and continued use in medical science and their positive values
in education.

3) A useful perspective showing how a stable English bird name opens a vast
literature compared to its scientific names and how changing would create
an instant linguistic barrier -- using Swainson's Warbler as an example
viewed through history using Google Books Ngram Viewer.

4) The example from medical anatomy showing that a top-down standardization
of nomenclature *without* eponyms was still not being accepted after 20
years.

Best, K.

But what is it about these papers that provides you with evidence for
doubting that "they got here by long distance (please define that)
transoceanic migration... More likely boating from Siberia along the coast
to our Pacific Northwest" As far as I can see, there is nothing in the
'coastal' hypothesis that precludes broader ocean travel (here the problem
is what defines 'coastal' travel vs coastal landings). Croizat suggested a
north Pacific track that may well be empirically indistinguishable from a
'coastal' route/s. Whether you call it coastal or anything else, Croizat
was, or was among, the first to seriously propose a non-terrestrial
mechanism, and at a time predating the anthropological story at that time.

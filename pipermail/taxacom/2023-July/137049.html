You are right about that. However, I think give it a few years and everyone would accept the changes and even changing nearly 20% of the names would promote stability because once changed they would never change again. I have always been amazed at how quickly the general public accept name changes. People grumble about it for a few months and then accept the change. Just look at the bird people who accept vernacular name changes with hardly a twitch (and that seems to happen much more than changes in the scientific names). Most people I know use the vernacular names for bumblebees as well so that is possibly not a good example. It is usually only the specialists that use the scientific names.

Trichogramma is derived from hair line. So far as I remember gramma used in this sense is feminine. However,  I cannot argue against the code on this, but I think your example, and other similar examples, clearly illustrate how unnecessary this part of the code is. Drop gender agreement and you simplify the code at a stroke. The code should really only be there to determine availability and precedence, gender agreement should have nothing at all to do with it because it has nothing at all to do with taxonomy.

By dropping gender agreement no taxonomist would ever have to do a linguistic search again and they would also not have to consult a list of names. Your argument goes both ways.

All the best.

John

And you work, by and large, with species names that rarely enter the non-taxonomic literature. In contrast, using a few other hymenopteran genera as examples, consider that many of the most common bumblebee species in the world, and many of the most common ants, and many of the most common paper wasps, would all suddenly have to change spellings because they have been in masculine genera for over 100 years, but their original Linnaean or Fabrician genera were feminine (e.g., Apis, Formica, and Vespa). The many people - including laypeople - who are familiar with common European species like Bombus lapidarius, Camponotus herculeanus, and Polistes gallicus wouldn't be happy *at all*.


The best estimate is that around 1/6th of the most well-known animal species in the world would have to change their spellings if we revert to original spellings (excluding butterflies). That would be the absolute biggest public relations nightmare we could ever ask for. If you think the furor over "offensive names" is bad, just imagine how much worse the public outcry would be if we told everyone that 1/6th of the animal species they know suddenly have different scientific names.


Maybe, if you worked on a group in the public awareness, you might feel a little differently about the matter.


> That way it would be completely objective and 100% stable.

You seem to have missed the point I was making, that we don't want
anyone to "argue over" anything, ever again. Complying with gender

Case in point, under Article 30.1.2 of the Code, all names ending in
"-gramma" are neuter, because "γράμμα" (gramma) is a Greek neuter noun
transliterated directly into the Latin alphabet, and usage by the
coining author is explicitly not taken into consideration.


"30.1.2. a genus-group name that is or ends in a Greek word
transliterated into Latin without other changes takes the gender given
for that word in standard Greek dictionaries"

Accordingly, we appear to have an argument on our hands. However,
*arguing* is a waste of your time and my time. It would save everyone a
lot of annoyance if you were able to simply consult an official list of
genus names and suffixes, where it says "-gramma" is always neuter and
"-gramme" is always feminine. *Then there is no more argument*. Same for
declaring that names like "flavus" are always adjectival, even if
someone, somewhere, used them as a noun. If we try hard enough, can we
dig up obscure references that use nouns as adjectives, or adjectives as
nouns? Maybe we can, but linguistic research is *not our job*.


No taxonomist should *ever* have to do linguistic research again; we CAN
eliminate that, by having lists, and still keep gender agreement
completely intact as a practice. All of the alternatives to official
lists are much worse, including the status quo.

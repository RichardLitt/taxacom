This also seems to miss the point.

The point, as I understand it, is that instead of having A-us b-us (for 
instance) as a universal pattern within a genus, you would have to 
remember (or look up) whether it is A-us b-us, A-us b-um, A-us b-e, A-us 
b-a, etc., individually for each species. There would be no way to ever 
predict what the name ends in for anyone, regardless of whether you had 
a classical education, speak a Romance language, or just like Latin. It 
would all be based on whatever someone thought was the prevailing usage 
within the group at an arbitrarily chosen point in time.

I don't care much either way when it comes to gender agreement but this 
seems to be unnecessarily complicated for no clear reason. It reminds me 
of nothing so much as when some phylocoders got the great idea to freeze 
genus+species for each taxon so that the genus name was preserved even 
if the species changed clades, and there would be no predictability of 
relationship based on the name that is used.

Cheers,
Daniel

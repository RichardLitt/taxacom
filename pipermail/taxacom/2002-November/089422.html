I believe the expression that "phenetics is dead" was used in reference to choosing
a method for constructing classifications.  If one's goal is to construct a
classification that is intended to provide a hypothesis for evolutionary
relationships, then most would agree that phenetics is dead (although phenetic
methods are still used to do so in a variety of contexts).  But, phenetic
classifications can satisfy the same criteria set forth for cladistic
classifications: stability, predictivity, information content.  To my knowledge, no
one has been able to demonstrate that either phenetic or cladistic approaches are
always superior for any of the general criteria for a classification. A variety of
simulation studies have lead to differeing conclusions with respect to which
methods performed best. And, proponents of both approaches have their favorite data
sets that demonstrate the inability of the other to reflect what is known (or
assumed to be known) about the taxa in question.


I believe that Stephen is right in his comment that phenetic methods are most
valuable at the genus level and below.  After all, in the end, the things that are
being classified by cladistics are typically taxonomic entities that have been
recognized by implicit or explicit phenetic analyses.

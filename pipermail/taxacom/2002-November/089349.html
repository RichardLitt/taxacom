The situation I had in mind was where a taxon has been referred to in unpublished reports by a vernacular name or by a formula such as "Xus new species B."  The point of including that name and citation in a synonymy at the start of a published monographic account of the species would be to provide continuity -- perhaps particularly as an aid to the agencies that may have oversight responsibility for the taxon.
Granted, the equivalency could be stated in a sentence somewhere in the Remarks section, but is there any reason to prefer that over the efficiency of standard synonymy format to convey the same information?

I prefer NOT to include names that are Not Validly Published (as defined by
ICBN) in the formal listing of nomenclature that usually starts off the
treatment of a species in a monographic account. They have no standing
under the Code and emphatically are NOT names as defined by the
Code. Instead, I would mention them in the text, saying, "Johnson (1878)
called this Planta alba but that name was never validated ..."

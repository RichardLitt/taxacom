Hang on there. Cladistics may be "the best and most objective means of
reconstructing phylogeny," but there is a big jump from that to "once you
have reconstructed phylogeny".

The kicker in cladistics is that getting a multifurcation as a result of
parsimony analysis is the only acceptable time a cladist throws up his/her
hands and says geeze we can't make a decision. This is nonsense. Suppose we
have three alternative branches and one of them is supported by only one
character more than the others? E.g. when closely related branches A, B and
C are resolved as ((AB)C), but AB is supported by 3 characters and AC by 2
others and BC by 2 others? (I given this example in previous messages.
Almost nobody reacted.) Have we established a phylogeny?

If two of the three branches have their support from shared ancestry, then
the other two got their support randomly from convergence (autapomorphies
happening to be the same).  Well, support from 3 advanced characters is real
close to support from 2 advanced characters. We haven't established a
phylogeny.

You need a statistically significant number of characters supporting a
"best" arrangement of any three branches in a rooted tree against random
convergence between those three close lineages before you can say you have
established a phylogeny at some confidence level. This goes for both
morphological and molecular data.

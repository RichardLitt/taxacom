Thank you, Ken, for taking up this subject. I have in my heart never really
accepted the absolute turn to strict cladism, even though I approve of the
cladistic method to reconstruct phylogeny.

Let's cut to the chase here (in order not to become drowned in discussions
about fighting spouses etc.):

The cladistic method is the best and most objective one (in a matter of
degree) to reconstruct phylogeny with. But once you have reconstructed
phylogeny, does classification necessarily have to reflect phylogeny
exactly? In some cases I (and many others with me) feel that it is better to
retain certain paraphyletic groups in order to maintain surveyability and
stability.

Surveyability: cutting up a classification into thousands of monophyletic
branches clearly does not contribute to accessibility to an already complex
biodiversity, unless we make classificatory chunks that intuitively make
sense.

Stability: new data might turn the phylogeny upside down again and therewith
classification, unless the basic classification agreed upon is not really
affected.

The bottom line: you have established a phylogeny, bravo, but let's include
some extra evolutionary (phenetic) information in the classification. No
need for doing things twice.

Cladists have thrown out the baby with the bath water, when getting rid of
evolutonary systematics. Still, I feel many people out there are
crypto-eclecticists, because strict cladism regularly yields unsurveyable,
unstable, counter-intuitive classifications.

At 08:03 AM 11/18/02 -0500, john grehan wrote:
>Ken wrote:
>>John,
>>     What I see as "germane" is that strict cladists are increasingly
>>encroaching on the freedom of traditional eclecticists to pursue science in
>>their own way.
>
>If you are saying that cladists are preventing non-cladistic methods from
>publication I would agree that it is a problematic situation. I still see a
>lot of phenetic studies being published (including phenetic molecular
>studies dressed up in cladistic terminology) so it would not seem that they
>are entirely exclusive.

This is interesting.  I remember being told, when a graduate student in the
1980's, that "phenetics is dead" as a methodology.  If not, my gut reaction
is that perhaps it is still useful and used more in revisionary work at the
genus and lower taxonomic levels, especially in relatively speciose taxa;
and increasingly less so as one moves up the taxonomic hierarchy to family
and higher levels.  Is there any validity to this perception?  Also, might
it be useful to clarify the differences, if any, between phenetics and the
non-cladistic parts of eclecticism?
I'm not sure I see any.

Cheers,
Steve

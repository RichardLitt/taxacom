At 11:06 AM 11/1/02 +0000, John Noyes wrote:
>>We use high quality CDs - and have been told they should last a few hundred
>>years if properly cared for.
>
>We shall know that only in a couple of hundred years, but will we still
>generally have the technology available to read them? Who, at present, can
>read the original IBM 7.5" floppy, or even the more recent 5.25" floppy.
>The latter was still in use only 10 years ago, but who can read it now. We
>will soon be using micro discs, about 1.25" in diameter, that may be able
>to hold more than a 10gb of information. I daresay some sort of solid state
>plug-in which can permanently record humungous amounts of electronic data
>may be available in the not too distant future. Where will CD's be then?
>
>As I see it, the real attraction of CD's as a means of making available
>taxonomic information is their low cost and ease of production. However,
>the cost consideration is very insignificant when compared with the actual
>cost of the taxonomic work itself. A sizeable revision may cost $10,000 to
>publish as hard copy, but the actual cost of getting that far may be
>>$300-500m. The ease of production is a real attraction, but I have the
>constant worry that this could be open to abuse as there is absolutely no
>quality control of the product which, theoretically at least, is present
>with hard copy. The same could be said of web-based taxonomy.

Just to cut through the issue here a bit. I am going to state what
herpetology will not say they want from all this. I am not saying I agree
or disagree with what they want, but I will state that I do not think the
World Congress of Herpetology is a good reviewer in all this. The
organisation has almost no interest in taxonomy, or nomenclature and do not
overly welcome contributions on either. I do not consider phylogeography as
taxonomy which is as close as they get. Thier membership is not made up of
a group who would understand the intricacies of nomenclatural issues for
the most part, with a very few exceptions.

What the herpetologists want is an end to the constant naming of taxa with
no scientific evidence that entities are actually taxa. So yes they want
the science judged with the nomenclature. The point I tried to make to
Hinrich the other day was that the Code endeavors to deal with nomenclature
only. In any case herpetologists want this connected because a number of
them do not wish to feel compelled to use names that have no scientific
basis. I get the point made anyone can name anything they see is a species.
The problem with a vertebrate group like reptiles or amphibians is the flow
on impact to conservation legislation. Legislation which can take from 2-10
years to to pass through. Impacts on CITES and other international
agreements and their local support legislation's.

So while we discuss this, be aware, that the reason behind some of this is
a push to have more control on what evidence is required to have a
scientific name brought into usage, and that this evidence being presented
as a requirement for the validity of the nomenclatural act.

Cheers, Scott

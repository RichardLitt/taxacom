I am not sure I agree. The statement in Article 8 is absolutely specific that the date of publication should be included in the article itself. If you accept that by all definitions the date must include the day and month of publication then the statement is black and white with no grey areas. Article 21 provides only a method for estimating a date of publication where that date given in the publication is incomplete. I am also not sure that the analogy with an error in evidence of registration is true either because the date of publication in the article itself is one of only three specific criteria that must be met to make an electronically published work available under the ICZN and is a completely separate requirement from ZooBank registration. If we were to follow through using your argument then the date of publication is completely irrelevant because it can be "corrected" at any time and so it could be excluded as a requirement.

My point was to show an analogy with Article 8.5.3.3, which states that "An error in stating the evidence of registration does not make [an electronic] work unavailable ..."
In the same way, an error in stating the correct date will not make an electronic work non-compliant. A date given as "2016" may be seen as an error for the actual date.

Am 10.01.2017 um 14:12 schrieb Thomas Pape:
> Note also, that the Code does not regulate cases, where the date of publication is given in error.
I disagree with this statement. Art. 21.4 rules such cases. A date that was given incorrectly still meets the requirement of a specified date. 
The date must be corrected by the community (for example in ZooBank entries), but the availability should remain unaffected.

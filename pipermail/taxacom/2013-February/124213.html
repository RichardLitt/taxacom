Mike,

The ARTICLES OF ASSOCIATION OF THE INTERNATIONAL TRUST FOR ZOOLOGICAL NOMENCLATURE are online:
http://iczn.org/sites/iczn.org/files/ITZN_Articles_of_Association_2011.pdf
I am not sure whether the US thinking of 'ownership' goes together with a UK charity, but I might be wrong.

The location London seems to be necessary because most submitted Cases need extensive checking by the Secretariat before they can be processed. A large historical library is still needed (hopefully not for too many years longer) to do this.

Seconding Stuart's question, I have a few others, formed from my experience as a former board member of a much larger organization, and a former Member (i.e. owner) of a Foundation:

Who actually owns the Trust?  How are those owners chosen, and by whom?
Does anyone paid by the Trust directly participate in choosing them? Do those legal owners practice their expected oversight with appropriate participation?

Who actually makes the hiring and firing decisions? Who sets salaries? Who reviews those decisions?

Why, if the cost of living in London is a major problem, does the Trust not do what the Royal Entomological Society did, and move?

There are more, but those will do for a start.

Mike

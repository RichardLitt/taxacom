Having been away for a few days, and with a lecture to write, and so not
much time, there is nevertheless perhaps a bit of confusion here worth
mentioning.  This quote as much refers to communication as grouping, as did
arguments over species/genus/family circumscriptions in Darwin's time.
Assuming a phylocode with names at all nodes of a resolved tree of life, or
even a much less resolved tree, more or less as at present, I presume
textbooks will focus on particular nodes, and different textbooks will end
up focussing on very largely the same nodes, or a subset of them (not that
all general books will be identical in this respect).  Otherwise I might
well prefer Babel after the event.

There is this small issue of quotidian communication that seems sometimes
to be forgotton.  Such communication generally involves conventions: in
this case, what chunks of nature we are going to focus on in everyday talk.
This is an issue in all complex systems of naming.  From this point of view
a phylocode name would end up with an equally ridiculous definition.

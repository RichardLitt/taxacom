Dear Dottore Pietro,
I'd like to have your e-mail address, for we are living in adjacency and we have similar flora and fauna. Our entomologists were exchanging their insects in past, too.
 
In fact, you surprised me with your message, too, especially for message of Ronaldo that I red before. he wrote me this:

"Dear Sir:
    In central and southern Philippines some insects are called "bao-bao".  
"Bao" that is not repeated is the word for tortoise.
   As a backgrounder, historically, the Philippines was part of the Vishayan 
empire and for 300 years was a Spanish colony.
    The common language (used in the households) is Cebuano Bisaya and is of 
Malayan origin."
Rodolfo C. Kintanar, M.M.

 
I don't know what to think now... 
This word is common in Bosnia and in inland parts of Dalmatia. The part of that population imigrated in Croatia from Bosnia and emigrated in Italy and other countries in time of Turkish empire, but the word "baja" can be even of Spanish origin, or Jewish, for Sefards from Spain that came to Bosnia in Middle Age, or of Arab origin, who traveled a lot in past, too but it can be imported also from distant Asian languages (Indoeuropean origin) This is a long way for such a small word!

I want to appologize to all people who don't like this subject and to thank to all people that answered.

Why I am interested in this?
I am born in part of Croat population that use term "baja" for insects, mostly coleoptera. That term is strange for other parts of Croat population and the term will extinct as many other ancient words and costums, but it is still in use uf our population here. I tried to prepare for exhibition about world of insects. I wanted to light up the all the terms related to insects and to show that local people paid attention on insects, in past too. At first I asked our best etymologist for answer about origin of this word, but it was not familiar word to him and I asked other people after that.

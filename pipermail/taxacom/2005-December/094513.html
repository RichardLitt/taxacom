An international consortium of major natural history museums, herbaria
and other organizations has launched an ambitious project, the 'Barcode
of Life Initiative', to promote a process enabling the rapid and
inexpensive identification of the estimated 10 million species on Earth.
DNA barcoding is a diagnostic technique in which short DNA sequence(s)
can be used for species identification.

The scientific benefits of DNA barcoding include: (i) enabling species
identification, including any life stage or fragment, (ii) facilitating
species discoveries based on cluster analyses of gene sequences (e.g.

cox1=CO1, in animals), (iii) promoting development of handheld DNA
sequencing technology that can be applied in the field for biodiversity
inventories and (iv) providing insight into the diversity of life.

The first international scientific conference on Barcoding of Life was
held at the Natural History Museum in London in February 2005 and in
this volume we review the scientific challenges discussed during this
conference and in previous publications.

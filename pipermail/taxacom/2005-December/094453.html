Dear Luis, F. Christian and taxacomers,

"Glabella" (dim. glaber: hairless) and the same to "aemula" (dim.v.
aemulor: emulate) are Latin names.
Probably the epithet "aemula" was assigned to a species that resembles
(morphology?) to another one.
All the best,
Vitor.

> Glabella: feminine diminutive of hairless, bald, smooth: glaber (m.:
> glabellus).  Not sure about aemula, but could be from emulatus, rivaling
> (emulating), hence aemulor, to rival or envy, thence a feminine.  Not
> very likely to have been names!

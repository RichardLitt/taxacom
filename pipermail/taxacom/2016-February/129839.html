Incongruent trees for different characters (including genes) are very
common and are a fundamental aspect of life. They are usually explained by
introgression or by incomplete lineage sorting (ancestral polymorphism).

In the latter process, the ancestor is polymorphic with, say, a north-south
break or cline, BEFORE the new phylogenetic break starts to form. This old
variation can be inherited. The new round of phylogeny can result in, say,
an east-west break or cline, and the two sets of variation now present in
the descendant are incongruent, in phylogeny and biogeography.

This is discussed in my Australasia book in the sections 'Incongruence in
patterns of variation in different genes and characters' (p. 27) and
'Examples of incongruence and incompete lineage sorting' (p. 29).

By whom?  Exactly. A label in a museum collection of "holotype" when the
original paper did not name one is just not to be trusted on its own. 
Museum curators decades later may have thought they had identified a
holotype, added the label for convenience, but be mistaken. There may even
have been past curators who selected a holotype on their own initiative  -
one would hope not too many of those about - altering the evidence in
effect.

>I see no recommendation that for lectotype designation a specimen
labelled (by whom?) as a holotype should be given preference<

Commonsense dictates that (all other things being equal) it is better to
designate as lectotype the specimen that was already labelled holotype.
Only if there is some really good reason should one designate another
syntype as lectotype. The Code does not state this, but it is commonsense
not to be more disruptive than is absolutely necessary.

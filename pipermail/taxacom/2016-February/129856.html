No it is not a side issue. It is central to what action to take if you're
going to be rigorously cautious. If an author designated a specimen as
holotype it is highly likely that he would happily record that fact in
1916. Therefore it is reasonable to assume it is more likely that it is a
later labelling by a curator, and thus just a syntype at this time. Noting
it was mentioned that a later author had decided they were syntypes (did
this person see the specimens and labels?). Depending on how the museum
keeps its records it might be possible to tell if it was labelled in the
museum registry as a holotype at accessioning/registration of all the
specimens sent in, or is a post hoc supposition (say written in a
different ink or handwriting). I'm assuming the museum keeps separate
records of holotypes.

Maybe I'm too cynical, but strange things can happen in one hundred years
of storage in a museum.

Geoff

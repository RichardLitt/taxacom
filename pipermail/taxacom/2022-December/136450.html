Thank you for the literature, this helped me a lot.

At least three syntypes were reported in the original publication (Denny 
1842: 41, 91).

Najer et al. 2020: 2, 18 assumed a holotype, this was not in accord with 
the original description.
Mey 2020: 160 was correct that it was not a holotype, but his 
assumptions concerning Najer et al.'s intentions seem unsubstantiated. 
In footnote 5, Mey 2020: 160 stated that Najer et al. (2020: 32) claimed 
that P. reguli was described "only" from a female. I could not extract 
such an assumption in Najer et al.'s 2020: 32 discussion, they just 
talked of "the female described as Ph. reguli" which could have meant 
"the female name-bearing type specimen that we examined and that Denny 
described under the name P. reguli".

Denny 1842: 91 did not exclude the additional specimens from the type 
series. It is possible that he studied only the first specimen for 
compiling description and figure, and that he obtained the other 
specimens after that. But the publication provides evidence (in the 
sense of Art. 72.4.1.1) that Denny did obtain the additional specimens 
before finishing the manuscript, that Denny considered them as belonging 
to P. reguli, and that the statement on the infraspecific variation was 
actually based on the information Denny extracted from the additional 
specimens. They were clearly syntypes.

Preserved syntype:
Najer et al. 2020: 18 reported a female specimen, "H. Denny leg.", NHML 
NHMUK010710741, ecat. 8192226.
Mey 2020: 160, footnote 6 reported that following Thompson 1937, Clay 
1947 and Hopkins & Clay 1952 no specimens of P. reguli were preserved in 
the British Museum (= NHML).
Danny 1842: 91 reported that he had not collected the specimens himself, 
but that he had obtained them from others.
So a specimen "H. Denny leg." would probably not be a syntype, because 
this indicated that Denny collected that specimen. It is possible that 
this was a mislabelling, but this is a speculation. Maybe it can be 
verified who labelled that specimen, to clarify that.

Mey 2020: 160 appears to have been correct in his assumption that all 
syntypes were lost. He clearly satisfied Art. 75.3.4.

 From this point of view, and since Najer et al. 2020 have cast doubts 
on the identity of P. regulus, the conditions of a neotype designation 
("exceptional need", Art. 75.3) were satisfied. On p. 160 Mey 2020 
explained this need (satisfying Art. 75.3.1).

This does not mean that Mey's 2020 neotype designation is automatically 
valid, we have to look at the other conditions closely.

Najer et al's 2020 examination of the so-called holotype is not entirely 
meaningless (because it provides evidence that real-life animals that 
corresponded with the original description of P. reguli Denny, 1842 did 
indeed exist), but that specimen has no nomenclatural relevance.
The statement by Mey 2020: 161 sounds quite strange in a scientific 
publication:
"(...) it is consistent to completely suppress the allegedly syntypic 
individual with dubious host origin, i.e. to exclude it from any further 
taxonomic evaluation as if it did not even exist."
Had he written "nomenclatural evaluation" this would be less problematic.

It is a requirement of the Code that if a neotype is designated, to 
provide "evidence that the neotype is consistent with what is known of 
the former name-bearing type from the original description and from 
other sources" (Art. 75.3.5).
Najer et al. 2020 found a specimen that they reported to correspond with 
the original specimen Fig. 4, and Mey 2020 accepted that this speimen 
had a doubtful taxonomic identity. So it would have been necessary for 
Mey to compare that specimen with the specimen of Fig. 4.
The thorough examination of a specimen that was presumably collected and 
determined by the original author Denny, even if not a syntype, falls 
clearly into this requirement. Mey's 2020 statement was a pronounced 
violation of Art. 75.3.5.

Art. 75.3.6 requires that the author provides evidence that the neotype 
came as nearly as practicable from the original type locality. Mey 2020 
did not discuss the type locality, he only reported that the neotype 
came from eastern Mecklenburg, northeastern Germany. Britain (the 
original type locality in the sense of Art. 75.3.6) is an accessible 
country, it would certainly be possible for a European scientist to 
obtain material from there, or to explain why this was not practicable. 
NE Germany was not "as nearly as practible" from Britain (the cited 
specimen No. 4 from eastern Holstein came from a locality closer to 
Britain - Mey did not explain why this was ignored and No. 2 was 
chosen). Art. 75.3.6 was clearly violated.

Art. 75.3.3 requires "data and description" sufficient to ensure 
recognition of the specimen. Mey provided collection numbers and a 
figure of male genitalia of the neotype. So this was satisfied, however 
we have to rely on the detailed description of P. gustafssoni provided 
by Najer et al. 2020 (to which Mey referred bibliopgraphically, 
satidfying Art. 75.3.2) for extracting the taxonomic identity of the 
neotype. Mey 2020: 164 provided a figure with setae of a so-called 
"neoparatypus", but he did not indicate which one of the four listed 
candidate specimens it was. This was not a very careful study.

Because the requirements of Arts. 75.3.5 and 75.3.6 were not satisfied, 
I would qualify Mey's 2020 neotype designation as invalid.

Now you are assuming that Mey should have seen that the figured specimen 
of the original Denny 1842 publication differed from his neotype in some 
taxonomically relevant characters. Mey 2020 apparently did not see a 
difference between the figure and his neotype, however he violated Art. 
75.3.5 anyway and did not consider the importance of the original 
description and the need to examine the British material. Mey did 
consider Denny's illustated specimen as conspecific with his neotype 
specimen.

I cannot judge the setae in that certain area you are reporting, I am a 
malacologist and have to rely on your thorough examination and expertise 
in this point.
I also understand that occasionally such lice are found on the wrong 
birds and then they are called stragglers. It seems to imply that they 
must have other hosts, unknown to the researcher.

I understand that Denny 1842: Pl. VI Fig. 4 (reproduced by Mey 2020: 162 
Fig. 4) do not show these setae, that P. gustafssoni in Najer et al. 
2020 (p. 21 Fig. 6, p. 24: Fig. 7, p. 26 Fig. 8C-D) do show these setae. 
I also understand that experts would agree that these setae are 
taxonomically relevant. E. Mey was an expert - why did he not consider this?

Mey 2020 did not know these differences and assumed that Denny's figure 
and this neotype were conspecific. This means he would have validly 
designated a neotype, had he not violated Art. 75.3.6 (and very probably 
also 75.3.5).

The next step would consist in either validly designating a neotype, or, 
if the original description and Fig. 4 is sufficiently clear, designate 
the specimen illustrated in Fig. 4 as lectotype.
A neotype would have to be consistent with the original description (= 
the same species as the lectotype, so, probably no exceptional need for 
a neotype), in terms of the setae, and come from Britain. And it must 
refer to a species that is not in accord with the traditional 
application of the name. Before a neotype is designated again, a serious 
author would examine again the British candidate syntype specimen and 
verify the labels.

If this is considered to distort nomenclature then the Commission can be 
invoked. If the name P. reguli is widely used (I guess it is), then 
designating a neotype under the plenary power can be an option. This 
could be done under Art. 75.6, which requires that the existing type is 
not in accord with the prevailing usage. Designating a lectotype would 
be a precondition for applying Art. 75.6.

It is rare that the original description provides a clear figure, and 
the prevailing usage refers to a different species for a very long time. 
It is an unusual case in this respect.

Summary:
Mey's 2020 neotype designation is invalid for having violated Art. 
75.3.6 and probably also 75.3.5.
The original description Denny 1842 reported at least 3 syntypes.
Currently available information suggests that no syntype has been 
preserved. The specimen in the Natural History Museum should be verified 
again closely.
If the original figure is sufficiently clear in that it represents a 
different species, not the one for which the name has been used, then 
either the Commission needs to be invoked, or P. gustafssoni would have 
to be used.

If this helps

Best wishes
Francisco

>>> Daniel:

>>> As for your question on standards and possible limits for "common
>>> usage", one of the underlying principles upon which the Code is based
>>> is that "There is no "case law" in zoological nomenclature. Problems
>>> in nomenclature are decided by applying the Code directly, and never
>>> by reference to precedent. If the Commission is called on to make a
>>> ruling on a particular case, the decision relates to that case alone".
>>>
>>> As for the specific issue, the authors behind Set 1 base their
>>> decision on the wrong assumption that the syntype specimen is the
>>> holotype. This specimen is a syntype (if it was one of the original
>>> specimens seen by the author in 1842), but the fact that it is the
>>> only specimen recovered does not give it any priority over the other
>>> (and potentially lost) specimens in the type series. Any specimen in
>>> the type series is eligible for being designated as a lectotype, but
>>> considering the syntype to be a holotype does not qualify as a
>>> lectotype designation for designations made after 1999. Set 1
>>> therefore does not solve the central issue of a possibly mixed type
>>> series.
>>>
>>> Set 2 rests on their arguments that what has been called a syntype
>>> specimen is actually NOT part of the original set of specimens seen by
>>> the author in 1842. If their arguments are accepted, their designation
>>> of a neotype is valid (if fulfilling other conditions provided by the
>>> Code), and the name proposed by the authors of Set 1 is a junior
>>> synonym. [NB: neoparatypes are not recognized by the Code.]
>>>
>>> A thorough assessment rests on whether or not the single syntype is
>>> actually a syntype, and whether or not this specimen is in a condition
>>> that allows for a reliable taxonomic decision.

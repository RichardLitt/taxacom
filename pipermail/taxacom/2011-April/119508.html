Sorry folks but, from the standpoint of someone primarily concerned with
interpreting findings from field work, this thread suggests you have
lost the plot.  The important thing is to be able to recognise an
organism, if possible, from a published description and the name is
absolutely fundamental.  That, of course, is what Linnaeus tried to do.
Decent indexing should allow tracking changes - Donat Agosti's Antbase
and my own more restricted "Ants of Africa" achieve this to a great
extent within our fairly narrow field. One then can compare findings on
biology, behaviour, ecology, etc. Trying to get political support for
and possibly understanding of biodiversity and its importance is not
going to be helped by pedantry.

So please try to focus on the real world.

Brian

While I might regard molecular phylogenies as phenetic, they are used

“fossil calibration (using minimum age as a the fine edge of the wedge to

Actually we don't confuse them. The minimum age issue is one – and it is

“generally weaponizing the widely acknowledged limitations of all of these

Don't understand that one. Many molecular studies admit that their findings

“Firstly because Croizat quite simply rejected cladistics, and if you

Panbiogeography uses cladistic phylogenetics and so in this respect accepts

“bcause CP rejects dispersal”

No it does not. Repeating this false claim (without evidence) is a bit like

“Trying to relabel cases of dispersal as "range expansion" is sophistry

Ecological dispersal is an observable empirical phenomenon that does indeed
cause range expansion. Attributing the origin of allopatry to one off
(usually) chance dispersal is another matter – purely conjectural. Not
empirical.

“Biogeographic and fossil calibrations are complementary, not competing,
approaches to constraining molecular clock analyses, providing alternative
constraints on the age of clades that are vital to avoiding circularity in
investigating the role of biogeographic mechanisms in shaping modern
biodiversity." (2016).

I couldn't agree more – fossil calibrations give minimum dates, tectonic
calibrations give approximations of actual dates. Those are definitely
complementary. If a tectonic date is older that is complementary to the
minimum date by providing additional information on an older origin which
is consistent with the fossil calibration not precluding an older origin.

I don't think any biogeographer could disagree with this statement,

As above, I for one do not.

“he unfalsifiability of CP in al its glory. If something disagrees with CP,

Jason, what you and probably others are looking for here is an umpire who
can stand in and call out 'true' or 'false'. In this situation we are
dealing with competing research programs. Arguments in the philosophy of
science have been made that research programs include a hard core of
unfalsifiable assumptions that are necessary to make their research
programs work. Simple falsification falls down in this situation. There is
a nice paper on this problem for biogeography if anyone is interested.

“ML or Bayesian models do not assume point dates but a probability

potential tectonic events (in other words the divergence estimates are
treated as actual ages – otherwise they could not be used to exclude
earlier origins and earlier events).

This is a pattern. I have kept correspondence for the last 10 years on
these exchanges and it is always the same rigmarole. But I still bite
because it is important to warn newcomers.

In science some issues are long standing, and by their nature, often
repetitive. For the 'newcomers', some summary points:

   1.

   Panbiogeography accepts dispersal as an ecological process of range
   expansion or persistence.
   2.

   Chance dispersal explanations for allopatry are often based on
   assumptions about phylogenetic age that may be underestimated.
   3.

   Chance dispersal explanations of allopatry are always problematic as
   they involve mutually exclusive requirements of isolation and accessibility.
   4.

   Tectonic correlation represents evidence of a historical relationship
   and origin that may be contested by competing research programs.
   5.

   Fossil calibrated divergence ages are always minimum estimates
   6.

   Priors of how much older are always guesses
   7.

   A molecular divergence estimate could be seen as a falsifier of younger
   origins just as a tectonic calibration could be seen as a falsifier of
   younger origins.
   8.

   A fossil calibrated molecular age is consistent with an older tectonic
   correlation age since the former does not exclude the latter.
   9.

   Many molecular papers are problematic because their biogeographic
   reconstruction treats molecular ages as actual or maximal when they are not.

May 2021 continue to be a good year for systematic and biogeographic debate.

Greetings all, a couple of points maybe..

(1) "Koala bear-like animal with carnivorous teeth" - no bear references
PLEASE!! Koalas are koalas, pure and simple, and about as far away
(phylogenetically) from bears as you can get and still be a mammal. However
the claimed taxon in this case is clearly a hoax (hypothetical if you
please).

(2) RE Nessiteras rhombopteryx  - my conclusion, from reading the article,
is that the authors were not attempting to perpetrate a hoax, but genuinely
were trying to provide a Code-compliant name for the "large animal species
living in Loch Ness" for which they believed they had photographic
evidence, in the future scenario that it may need legislative protection;
the existence of the animal was not hypothetical, but actual, in their
minds. So I am in agreement with Richard (and subsequently, Thomas) in this
respect and have annotated my database's record for "Nessiteras"
accordingly - see https://www.irmng.org/aphia.php?p=taxdetails&id917066
-  indeed I had such a record previously, to 2016 (based on the Nomenclator
Zoologicus entry for the genus name), then in 2016 it was deprecated as
being an "imaginary animal" or similar, however as a result of this
discussion I have decided to reinstate it. If there are other genus names
(as opposed to species) deserving of such treatment (arguably available
names proposed by their authors in good faith, for taxa now considered
non-existent) that I do not already list, perhaps interested parties could
advise further.

Fossils described once as biota, now considered abiotic, are a separate
IRMNG category, under the heading "Questionable / non-biota (fossil)",
already covered at https://www.irmng.org/aphia.php?p=taxdetails&id . The
names remain as described for the purpose of homonymy in the various Codes,
and can also be transferred back to "Animalia", Plantae", etc. if in the
future they may be re-interpreted as indeed representing the remains of
actual organisms.

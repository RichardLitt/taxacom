In addition to the points Fred made (below), it is my impression from 
having similarly googled 'evo-devo and intelligent design' that the ID 
folks are insisting on mutations as the means by which ontogenies drift, 
and their incredulity that any given mutation could possibly result in a 
seriously different outcome without being fatal. What they don't seem to 
address is that what may change is the interaction of genes via their 
products, so that any given gene's expression occurs under different 
circumstances and so leads to different outcomes.  They focus on 
molecules, rather than interactions between and involving molecules.

Suppose you have gene complexes responsible for producing a diesel 
engine. Easy to imagine subtle shifts in ontogeny leading to a gasoline- 
or LNG-fueled engine (or to a 2-stroke engine). But the way the genes 
control the development of pistons and cylinders could equally lead to 
the flowering of a new lineage in which pistons and cylinders operate in 
a different functional realm, manipulating limbs or tools. Changes in 
the expression of genes affecting the distal ends of spinning shafts 
could produce divergences between wheeled (e.g. quadrupeds, arthropods) 
and propeller-driven 'organisms' like birds and bats, or fish and 
cetaceans. Just a matter of how you program the machine shop. And those 
are just examples from a single domain, arthropods apart.

And Fred is especially right in referring to patterns of biological 
diversity, and the wonders of homology, whether in animals or in plants 
(Hofmeister! all the work on what can be done controlling the patterns 
of cell division and differentiation in apical meristems! positional 
effects!).

John Grehan and many others have pointed out repeatedly that telling 
others (e.g. religious fundamentalists, ID folks, climate change 
denialists, Trumpies) that their point of view is just dumb and contrary 
to science is not an effective way to change their mindset. It takes way 
more patience than many of us have to find common ground from which to 
reach an understanding of why people believe as they do, and to find 
meaningful points of departure from which to challenge their beliefs 
without being demeaning.

---tad.

Hi everyone,

Sorry for being late to this thread... For what it is worth, my two cents
below.

We taxonomists sometimes agonize for no real reason. But this should have
an easy solution: illustrate the structure 'in addition' to name it. For
example: "X structure Y-shaped (as in Fig. Z)", where X is the
structure/feature you want to discuss, Y is the term you want to use
to refer to such a structure and Z is the image that shows what you mean.
That way you can be as fancy as you want and use as many Greek, Latin or
any other words as you want to use AND the reader would understand what you
mean, even if "eyebrows" could be interpreted differently and/or no one
would know what "ophrysiform" is. In these days of digital photography
widely available and electronic publications (usually) not charging extra
for color images, there is really no need to agonize about wording and
instead rely on the old, true and tested "A picture is worth a thousand
words".

Speaking of which, I wanted to mention that yesterday we published a paper
in ZooKeys with a key to European genera of a difficult group of parasitoid
wasps. Difficult not only because of being a very diverse group, full of
homoplasies and with many morphologically indistinguishable species, but
also because different workers have used different terminologies over the
years, especially for wings. We decided to illustrate all systems of
venation used historically in Europe, as well as illustrate (and discuss)
many morphological terms commonly used among different European schools
over the past century. This paper does not make the identification of
Microgastrinae parasitoid wasps a "walk in the park" (as hyperdiverse
groups would never be easy to work with!), but at least facilitate the
reader/user of such key a better understanding of morphological terms, and
(hopefully!) the identification of the genera dealt with there. Link:

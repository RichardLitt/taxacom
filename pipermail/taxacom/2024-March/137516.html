> Dear Vazrick,
>
> To answer your question: basically yes, it does.
>
> In paragraph 2 an important information is missing: I assume that 
> Staudinger's maxima and Elwes's maxima are currently (or were in 2005 
> and 2007) placed in the same genus, so that maxima Staudinger, 1901 
> is/was considered a junior secondary homonym of maximus Elwes, 1893.
>
> That said:
>
> In zoology such a name is usually not called a "replacement name" 
> (this is a botanical term, it means "nomen novum" there), but a 
> "substitute name".
> Kemal & Kocak (2005) could have proposed a "new replacement name" 
> (this is the zoological term for "nomen novum") for C. ci. var. maxima 
> Staudinger, 1901 (preoccupied by A. vulcanus var. maximus Elwes, 1893, 
> considered to belong to the same genus), but they were not obligated 
> to do that. They had the taxonomic freedom to propose a 
> regular/normal/entirely new name with its own name-bearing type, being 
> then a junior subjective synonym of C. ci. var. maxima Staudinger, 
> 1901. If it is the oldest junior synonym for Staudinger's taxon, this 
> name is to be used (as a substitute name).
>
> This is largely the same answer as Adam gave, however Adam's 
> expression "species that did not have an available name in a Code 
> compliant manner" was not in line with the Code: C. ci. var. maxima 
> Staudinger, 1901 is an available name, no matter if preoccupied or 
> not. This should read "that did not have a name that could currently 
> be used under the Code".
>
> If this helps.
>
> Best wishes
> Francisco

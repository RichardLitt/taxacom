Citation of an author and date for higher taxa is also of use when there's
more than one usage of the same name.  This arises in two ways - there are
homonyms, such as the use of Pulmonata and Decapoda in different phyla, and
in Phylocode-style usage where a particular scope of a taxonomic name is
specified, and different authors may define the scope of the same name in
different ways (a problem avoided by having ranked names, which provides
circumscribed flexibility in name usage).

> some of us to know where it arose, and go and have a look at the context -
> this might have been the purpose of the original question.  Linnaeus
> himself was just building on existing knowledge and name usages.  A lot of
> his content he lifted from others - usually acknowledged.

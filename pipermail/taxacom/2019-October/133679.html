    On Wednesday, 2 October 2019, 08:06:33 pm UTC, John Grehan <calabar.john at gmail.com> wrote:  
 
 Stephen, There is nothing in science to support the contention that "If the oldest and most primitive member of a group is discovered as a fossil in the S Hemisphere, then that evidence suggests a S Hemisphere origin for the group." There is just no basis for that. You are incorrect to say that " such an origin is suggested by the available evidence." It is not. The location of the oldest and most primitive fossil member of a group is only evidence of the oldest (known) and most primitive (known) fossil member being at a particular location at a particular geological date.

On Wed, Oct 2, 2019 at 3:55 PM Stephen Thorpe <stephen_thorpe at yahoo.co.nz> wrote:

 John,I'd like to know what other Taxacomers think about this, as it is crucial to the proper understanding of science itself. You seem to be trying to undermine scientific inferences on the basis that there are other possible explanations of the same facts. That might be a sensible approach to certain kinds of cases, but not ones of this kind, I suggest. You seem to be conflating certainty with empirical support (for a hypothesis). Science isn't about determining the truth, it is about being led by evidence. If the oldest and most primitive member of a group is discovered as a fossil in the S Hemisphere, then that evidence suggests a S Hemisphere origin for the group. Sure, there are other, more convoluted possibilities which could be wheeled in ad hoc, but that's not science! The authors are not claiming any certainty of a S Hemisphere origin for the group, merely that such an origin is suggested by the available evidence. I see no particular problem with this, except perhaps the overly heavy weight that science puts on working with mere inconclusive suggestions from empirical evidence. For applied science, at the end of the day, somethinbg either works or it doesn't, but, for pure science, the inconclusive suggestions just lead to inconclusive theories with which scientists occupy their time and resources, but for what???Stephen
    On Wednesday, 2 October 2019, 02:11:13 pm UTC, John Grehan <calabar.john at gmail.com> wrote:  
 
 Stephen - the assertion that a southern hemisphere origin "is suggested by the new evidence" is indeed nonsense. The fossil can only suggest that a clade was present at a particular place and time. Empirically it says nothing about the origin of the taxon with respect to younger fossils elsewhere. Its quite possible that the group had its origin from a widespread ancestor that was in both the northern and southern hemisphere and the (current) oldest fossil happens to be located in the south. It does not matter that the fossil may represent the basal lineage as this may just represent an initial vicariance. So dreaming up a local (southern) origin is not science at all, just nonsense.

John Grehan

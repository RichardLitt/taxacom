Comment on Jason's perspective below:

“the resident panbiogeographer´s position, made time and time again can be
(overly) simplified as follows: molecular rates are flexible”


I would have said variable – at least my impression is that molecular
systematists do not argue for a single molecular clock.

“models based on these rates are fallible”

which models?

“fossils can only be used to estimate a minimum.”

Correct! Glad you get that.

“Ergo, any distribution can be explained solely by vicariance
(by the judicious stretching and compressing of molecular rates to
suit this view).”

Not correct. Please provide evidence.

“And since the molecular based rates are flexible (and did I mention
fallible) they can´t falsify my hypothesis.”

Falsify what hypothesis?

“In fact, neither can fossils because lack of fossils (even in very well
researched localities) can be explained away by the difficulty of
fossilization, etc.”

The absence of fossils is not necessarily evidence of absence.

“All perfectly "logical" but a clear rejection of the abductive process on
which science is also (mostly?) based. “

Explain how so.

“This perspective would be fine if it weren´t accompanied by the constant

trickle of little jabs to get some sort of discussion going.”

If one does not like the way a topic is brought up on TAXACOM then just
ignore it.

“My take on Rob´s post is that constant baiting is irritating and that
some circumspection on their part is advisable.”

Again, if something is irritating then just ignore it. That's freedom isn't
it?

“If they have fresh epistemological ideas that are clearly fleshed out then
great, it is always good to spar as better understanding ensues. But if the
whole exercise becomes yet another tit-for-tat mud slinging exercise based
on the same sacred texts, then it is pointless and default reaction is
to shrug shoulders and move on (although sometimes you make the
mistake of taking the bait and you throw a salvo their way to detox).”

Agreed – which is why I don't accuse individuals of 'sophistry' or being
'self serving' just because I may not agree with their arguments or
viewpoints. What gets lost in all of that is the fact that
fossil-calibrated molecular divergence estimates are minimums and nothing
else. What that means in science is that molecular divergence estimates
cannot falsify earlier origins or the involvement of earlier tectonic
events. Its just that plain simple. No more, no less. And all that
panbiogeography does is offer another approach to calibration – one that
does find favor among some molecular systematists. Sometimes repetition is
necessary for some to get the point.

John Grehan

> > Stephen
> >     On Monday, 21 October 2019, 12:15:03 am UTC, Rob Smissen via Taxacom
> <taxacom at mailman.nhm.ku.edu> wrote:
> >
> >  Once again Michael Heads promotes his self-serving slogan, claiming "As
> discussed, fossil-calibrated, molecular clock dates for clades can only
> ever be minimums."
> > Current Bayesian molecular dating software gives a probability
> distribution of dates. How can a distribution of dates (a range) be a
> minimum? Well, Heads takes the oldest date from a published range and
> transmogrifies that into minimum.
> >
> > But that is only the beginning. The "only ever minimum" argument ignores
> the fact that other sources of error can contribute to misleadingly old
> date estimates. These include
> >
> >   1.  Ancestral polymorphism (splits in gene lineages are older than
> splits among populations). This has the opposite effect to the missing
> fossil problem. Gene lineage divergences necessarily predate population
> divergences, and therefore one might (wrongly) infer using Heads' logic
> that molecular date estimate are necessarily maximum.
> >   2.  Rate variation among lineages.
> >   3.  Miss-specified models.
> >   4.  Incorrect attribution of fossils to extant lineage.
> >   5.  Imprecise ages of fossils (often themselves given as ranges).
> >   6.  Stochastic effects when sequences are short.
> >
> > Sometimes the data underpinning molecular dating papers is consistent
> with a 0 age (by which I mean that the samples could as well have been
> taken from a single panmictic population), yet Heads transmogrifies an age
> of millions of years into a so called minimum. I don't believe intelligent
> people would support that, at least not if they were honest.
> >
> > We should all take molecular clock dates with a big grain of salt, but
> slogans don't make for good science. I don't mind an argument that
> molecular date estimates may be underestimates, but I do mind the slogan
> that they are necessarily minimum. That is sophistry.
> >
> > Using the current generation of software, it is possible to explore the
> effect of using different priors. In fact it is quite easy. In particular,
> it is easy to explore the effect of increasing a calibration age to make
> dates older. It is also easy to constrain or relax the allowed rate
> variation among lineages.  If Heads was serious he could undertake these
> analyses. He could make the calibrations as old as he likes. Or stipulate
> that conspecific allopatric populations diverged in the Mesozoic as he oft
> claims. Then he could quantify how much rate variation, and how old the
> earth has to be, to accommodate his "biogeographic" assumptions. Despite
> claiming an understanding of molecular data and the workings of Bayesian
> analyses, and the fact that the software is free, Heads seems remarkably
> slow to provide analyses of how much older than their oldest fossils clades
> needs to be to fit his paradigm.

>> Hi
>> 
>> Anybody know more or less what percentage gain in storage space there is
>> when converting traditional closed herbarium cupboards for a compactus
>> system, assuming that the compactus will have more or less the same
>> dimensions as the original cupboards?
> 
> This is easy to calculate: the primary function of a compactor system
> is to eliminate the aisles between storage units. If you plan to
> retain the same storage units, then the space you will gain is almost
> exactly equal to the space occupied by the aisles you will lose (if
> installing a single continuous bank of compactor units, then you'll
> keep only one aisle from the original N, and lose N-1). Only a few
> inches are needed between adjacent closed compactor units. The wider
> the aisles relative to the storage space, and the more of them in the
> original floor plan, the bigger the percentage gain in space. Since
> those are variables depending on the kind of storage units, and
> quirks of architecture, there is no good "rough" number one can use
> to generalize, though I can't imagine it would ever equal or exceed
> double (i.e., 100% increase) the original capacity (that would
> require that *over* half the original floor space was empty aisles).
> Figures that high are generally only possible when either additional
> floor space is included in the compactor "footprint" or when the
> storage units themselves are replaced with more space-efficient units.

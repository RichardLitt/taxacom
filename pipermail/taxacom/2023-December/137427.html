Hopefully the work gives the etymology (although I am doubtful).At the risk of provoking nasty responses...The general default under the ICZN is to treat any non-Latin (andnon-latinized-Greek) epithet as a noun, and both Article 31.2.2 and(especially) 31.2.3 are pretty clear on this point:"31.2.2. Where the author of a species-group name did not indicatewhether he or she regarded it as a noun or as an adjective, and where itmay be regarded as either and the evidence of usage is not decisive, itis to be treated as a noun in apposition to the name of its genus (theoriginal spelling is to be retained, with gender ending unchanged; seeArticle 34.2.1).31.2.3. If a species-group name (or, in the case of a compoundspecies-group name, its final component word) is not a Latin orlatinized word [Arts. 11.2, 26], it is to be treated as indeclinable forthe purposes of this Article, and need not agree in gender with thegeneric name with which it is combined (the original spelling is to beretained, with ending unchanged; see Article 34.2.1)."The point is that even if the author of the name "najeeba" said it wasadjectival, it is not, because that particular name falls under 31.2.3,and that Code Article does not recognize the coining author as havingcontrol over the assignment, which would be possible if it fell under31.2.2.The reason I expect some nasty responses is that some people feel thatany name ending in an "-a" or "-us" or "-um" has been "latinized". Thisis by no means established under the Code. All of these endings appearon *numerous* Latin and latinized nouns, so these can't be consideredstrictly adjectival suffixes. Importantly, "-a", "-us", and "-um" canall also appear in transliterated Greek, and are not definitivelyadjectival there, either. A species name "newyorkus"?would not betreated as adjectival, but "noveboracus" (which is demonstrablylatinized) would be.There is a chance that under the next edition of the Code, theCommission will provide an Official List of Adjectival Names andSuffixes, and that will remove any ambiguity or argument on this point.Peace,--Doug Yanega      Dept. of Entomology       Entomology Research MuseumUniv. of California, Riverside, CA 92521-0314     skype: dyanegaphone: (951) 827-4315 (disclaimer: opinions are mine, not UCR's)              https://nam10.safelinks.protection.outlook.com/?url=https%3A%2F%2Ffaculty.ucr.edu%2F~heraty%2Fyanega.html&data=05%7C02%7Ctaxacom%40lists.ku.edu%7C78d62461438d4513390408dc00bf1214%7C3c176536afe643f5b96636feabbe3c1a%7C0%7C0%7C638386067845774126%7CUnknown%7CTWFpbGZsb3d8eyJWIjoiMC4wLjAwMDAiLCJQIjoiV2luMzIiLCJBTiI6Ik1haWwiLCJXVCI6Mn0%3D%7C3000%7C%7C%7C&sdata=HhLgvNRQEAUgFfNoEV2EP4uPNceF53BfEWEIaSUHA0Y%3D&reserved=0<https://nam10.safelinks.protection.outlook.com/?url=https%3A%2F%2Ffaculty.ucr.edu%2F~heraty%2Fyanega.html&data=05%7C02%7Ctaxacom%40lists.ku.edu%7C78d62461438d4513390408dc00bf1214%7C3c176536afe643f5b96636feabbe3c1a%7C0%7C0%7C638386067845930366%7CUnknown%7CTWFpbGZsb3d8eyJWIjoiMC4wLjAwMDAiLCJQIjoiV2luMzIiLCJBTiI6Ik1haWwiLCJXVCI6Mn0%3D%7C3000%7C%7C%7C&sdata=dsnkbn9np9jes8s%2FL7Aw0JWx6ICy%2FaZyUsOb4eHDHIc%3D&reserved=0>   "There are some enterprises in which a careful disorderliness         is the true method" - Herman Melville, Moby Dick, Chap. 82

> >
> > > As of the 2000 Edition of the Code (Code 4) there are more criteria
> > > that need to be met for a new name to be available than was true in
> > > earlier Code versions.
> > >
> > > (1) It must be *published* in the sense of the Code. With print pubs
> > > this is generally easily met, for e-only publications the
> > > requirements are very stringent and complicated.
> > >
> > > (2) Names for new families *must* designate a type genus, new genera
> > > must designate a type species, new species must designate a type
> > specimen.
> > >
> > > (3) There must be an *explicit statement* that the names are new,
> > > and they must be proposed as available names of subspecies or higher
> > > rank, not synonyms, morphs, or varieties, or as "conditional" names.
> > >
> > > (4) proposals of new taxa must include a *verbal description* of
> > > their diagnostic feature(s).
> > >
> > > (5) for new species, the *name and location* of the depository of
> > > the type specimen must be given.
> > >
> > > While these provisions are excellent at preventing "accidental"
> > > introduction of new names (such as in a meeting abstract) it's
> > > pretty remarkable how often authors trying to publish new taxa fail
> > > to comply with all the requirements. In just the past year, I've
> > > seen examples of works failing each one of the criteria above,
> sometimes more than one.

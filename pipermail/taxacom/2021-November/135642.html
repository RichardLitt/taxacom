On 26 Nov 2021, at 07:16, Lyubomir Penev <lyubo.penev at gmail.com<mailto:lyubo.penev at gmail.com>> wrote:

Dear Rod,

Congratulations with your new service which seems rather promising, especially if it manages to cover a large number of publishers of taxonomic journals!

Regarding Pensoft and some points raised by others in this tread, I think that you can use also OAI-PMH, besides RSS, to harvest all content from one-stop web service end point (see ours at: https://arphahub.com/about/OAI-PMH). Through OAI-PMH you can also select Pensoft's ca. 20+ journals that publish taxonomic records (not just ZooKeys or PhytoKes). The high-level taxa associated to each article can be taken from the <subj-group> in each article XML. RSS are fine and great, however they cover only the last 100 newly published items and not always give straightforward  access to the full-text article XML. In other words, you have to rely mostly on metadata in this case.

In addition, Pensoft provides human-readable email alerts (daily, weekly or monthly, on choice of the user) from user-selected journals, taxa or subject categories. When selecting a taxon or taxa from "Taxon categories" tab in user's personal profile alert setup, the system will inform you about all new articles in all selected journals, published in relation to that taxon or taxa.

I hope this helps.

Regards,
Lyubo

> The original plan for this app was to use RSS feeds provided by journals
> to get the latest list of articles each journal publishes. While some
> publishers do support RSS many journals don’t, so I have to come up with
> other ways to get this information. RSS was very popular in the heyday of
> blogging and news feed readers but with the demise of Google Reader
> https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Reader and related apps RSS has been
> fading away a bit, which is a pity.
>
> Regarding bioinformatics and “big data”, I have my own issues with various
> projects (which I blogged about), but I think in this case the issue is
> that (a) it’s actually quite challenging to build a tool to poll the
> literature for new species - as I’m rapidly discovering - and (b) what are
> the incentives for providing such a service? I tend to think that people
> act rationally with respect to incentives, and so if things that you want
> to see happen aren’t happening then that’s a close that the  incentives
> aren’t there.

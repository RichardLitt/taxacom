So, even though the type designation itself, taken out of context, is ambiguous, that ambiguity may be able to be resolved by reading the whole article, in which is would presumably become clear which catocalina Moore was intended as the type species;(2) I think that Holloway (1985) did include just the type species in the new genus, so we have potential type species fixation also by monotypy, though it is possible that, even though he may have redescribed the species in detail, he fails to cite the name of the species in such a way as to resolve the ambiguity between the two catocalina Moore (i.e. by not citing the original combination or date).  Stephen you pointed to the same problem I also would like to mention:

"catocalina Moore" alone is probably not a valid type designation. Art. 
67.2.1 demands a name having been cited by "an available name of a 
species or subspecies", so citing a name without genus is not a "name 
cited". A name of a species is a binomen, it requires a generic name.

Since there are two "catocalina Moore", the citation was ambiguous, and 
Art. 68.2 fails, because Art. 67.5.3 applies. In any case, we have no 
original type designation, and hence, no type fixed by original 
designation. Also, Art. 67.2.5 needs to be considered. I cannot read the 
publication very well, Stephen you might have a better access.
If Antitrisuloides can have a validly fixed type in the original source, 
then by monotypy. I could not check how many species were originally 
included. Should probably be checked.

Antitrisuloides could be a nomen nudum in that 1985 source.

Hi Ken,

Regarding your 2014 statement that “they don't seem to "realize how
devastating the K-T extinction event was to the biota of Madagascar, and
how that cleared the way for the vertebrate dispersers during the
Cenozoic."

This is not a fact, but a conjecture. And it is one that is not supported
by the biogeographic evidence. While the K-T event may have been
devastating, it did not eliminate everything. A great deal of 'modern' life
apparently did survive.

 "In spite of the growing amounts of evidence for Cenozoic dispersal (for
invertebrates and plants, as well as vertebrates)...”

There is none.

“you [John] always retreat to your fallback position complaining about
"minimal estimates", but your imagined "maximal estimates" would often have
to be so old that they seem even more problematic.”

They are not maximal estimates, but estimates of an actual date. So it
seems here that you accept that the minimum age criterion of fossils is
correct, except you cannot accept that the older ages proposed from
tectonic correlation. But your personal rejection of those older ages being
“more problematic” (how?, specify cases) is not of itself a
counter-evidence.

"Perhaps there was more Cenozoic vicariance in Australasia"

More than what? Explain that.

“but if you keep on harping about dispersal to Madagascar, it makes people
wonder if you are also exaggerating the role of vicariance elsewhere.”

Until dispersalists come up with some actual evidence, of course I might
keep 'harping' on it. And especially if it continues the misrepresentation
(notice I did not say fraudulent again, just to soothe unsettled nerves) of
fossil calibrated data as anything but a minimum estimate, then of course I
will keep drawing attention to it. One need not ignore bad science (or at
least what one considers to be bad science) just because a lot of people
practice it.

Cheers, John

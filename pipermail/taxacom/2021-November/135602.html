Stephen you pointed to the same problem I also would like to mention:

"catocalina Moore" alone is probably not a valid type designation. Art. 
67.2.1 demands a name having been cited by "an available name of a 
species or subspecies", so citing a name without genus is not a "name 
cited". A name of a species is a binomen, it requires a generic name.

Since there are two "catocalina Moore", the citation was ambiguous, and 
Art. 68.2 fails, because Art. 67.5.3 applies. In any case, we have no 
original type designation, and hence, no type fixed by original 
designation. Also, Art. 67.2.5 needs to be considered. I cannot read the 
publication very well, Stephen you might have a better access.
If Antitrisuloides can have a validly fixed type in the original source, 
then by monotypy. I could not check how many species were originally 
included. Should probably be checked.

Antitrisuloides could be a nomen nudum in that 1985 source.

Am 13.11.2021 um 09:03 schrieb Stephen Thorpe via Taxacom:
>   I think the confusion possibly arises from Holloway (1985) designation of type species for new genus Antitrisuloides. It is cited as catocalina Moore, with no mention of genus or date, see: https://books.google.co.nz/books?redir_esc=y&id=MvtNAAAAYAAJ&focus=searchwithinvolume&q=catocalinaHaving said that, it is implied to be Trisuloides catocalina Moore, on p. 162, with no search results in the publication for Tambana catocalina. This does suggest a problem and a potential misidentification of type species, made worse by the apparent homonymy with Tambana catocalina Moore, 1882 being incorrectly referred to in some literature as Trisuloides catocalina (Moore, 1882), not to be confused with Trisuloides catocalina Moore, 1883. A bit of a mess!Stephen

 Recent papers on this subject by Huelsenbeck and his peer, invoking
 Bayesian analysis and maximum likelihood, compare the relative
strengths
 of likelihood values and posterior probability values in order to
select
 best hypotheses. Even though posterior probability values of such best
 hypotheses are incredibly low, no one has really looked at them and
said
 with astonishment, like Larson's cow: "My God! It's grass! We've been
 eating grass!"

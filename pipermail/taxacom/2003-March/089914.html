>John:

>While I disagree with this, I think we can agree that if one throws out the

As a phenetic enterprise it happens to be consistent with itself

>Your claim that molecular data is problematic 'if it is phenetic' is
>puzzling to me.  Molecular data is often analysed 'cladistically' by maximum
>parsimony.

The data may be analyzed using cladistic algorithms, but the data (the
characters) themselves are not identified as cladistic in the first place
(i.e. the sequences are not sorted into hypothesized synapomorphies that
can be tested against each other - at least that is my current
understanding. If I am wrong I welcome correction.


> > I guess I am lost at sea with this. If morphology is misleading and
> > genetics is not, morphology remains uninformative about phylogeny.
>Weighing
> > misleading evidence seems problematic.
>
>Perhaps we don't understand each other here.  Morphology and phenotypic data
>CAN be misleading.  So can genotypic data.  But since phenotypic traits are
>moulded more directly and (probably) frequently by natural selection, they
>become more rapidly skewed away from the underlying historical signal.

This is just a theoretical proposition. Whether it is true is another matter.

>  That doesn't mean that phylogenetic information is unrecoverable.  In
> any case,
>we have no a priori way to identify misleading characters, except by
>comparing them to other characters.

At least in morphology one has an a priori way of distinguishing apomorphic
and plesiomorphic characters.

>Hence, if different characters conflict
>in their phylogenetic signal, we hope that by looking at more characters, we
>can find the 'uncorrupted' historical signal by consensus.  Yes?

In morphology I cans see how different hypothesized derived characters may
come into conflict with each other. I am not sure I can see consensus being
informative other than to draw attention to those areas in conflict.

>Since the hard-tissue evidence is a source of disagreement, have you

>Homo, Pan = 95%; Gorilla, Pan, Homo = 96%.

Not yet. If they have carried out a cladistic analysis of soft tissue
characters it will be very interesting. I know of a blood vessel study that
gives a synapomorphy for Homo-Pongo.

Having spent nine years in a museum setting and interacted frequently with
exhibit developers and outreach educators, I think I understand John's
viewpoint.  It seems to me that what he wants to do primarily is to give
visitors a glimpse of Science As Process, and that The Origins of Humans
is, in many ways, a secondary message.  WAY too many folk think of science
as A Body of Knowledge or as An Attitude, and *not* as a Process for
Understanding.  Presenting them with contrasting hypotheses and laying out
the ways by which scientists evaluate these hypotheses is, I think, an
excellent idea.  As long as the accompanying text is honest about the data
and its interpretation, I think this is exactly the way to proceed.  More
power to the good folks in Buffalo!

If folks are more concerned with enforcing orthodoxy than with
*understanding*, it's small wonder the public has such an inaccurate view
of science.  I have students in our bio orientation class ruminate and
write on the following quote: "Science is not about getting the right
answer; science is about asking the right questions."

Dear Drs. Grehan, Delprete, et al.,

There is little doubt left by a wide array of consistent molecular data
that bonobo and chimpanzee are sister species, separated by only a few
million years, and that the chimpanzee lineage (including both Pan
spp.) is the sister group to humans.  This being the case, it makes no
evolutionary sense to say that one chimpanzee is more 'closely related'
to human than the other, since we are equally related to both species.

The human-chimp-gorilla trichotomy problem has been resolved
molecularly, with high statistical support for a number of years (e.g.,
see papers by Mary-Ellen Ruvolo or Morris Goodman for reviews), and is
no longer really considered a problem.  Molecular clock estimates
generally place the human-chimp split at about 6 million years, the
gorilla-(human-chimp) split around 7 million years, and the
orang-(human-chimp-gorilla) split around 14 million years.  These
results are consistent, and the dates are pretty robust to statistical
methodology and calibration details.

There is not any molecular evidence that suggests that orangutan and
human could be sister species, to the exclusion of chimps and gorillas.
  To the contrary, all available data strongly excludes that hypothesis.

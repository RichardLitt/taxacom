Thomas,
      Speaking as a mammalogist, I think your botanical perspective seems
right on the mark.  The list of putative synapomorphies uniting man and
orangutans seems weak at best, and it's thus not really surprising that it
is a minority viewpoint.  It's obviously not *quite* as bad as a clade of
"man + peanut butter sandwiches" (I got a good laugh from that one!!).
However, I think it would be a poor choice for John to choose to show
science as a process.
      Instead of beating a dead (or dying) horse like the "orangutan-man"
clade, perhaps something a little more recent would be more instructive.
      I propose that John consider presenting the "bivalves came first"
(never had a radula) hypothesis as an example in its earliest stages of
scientific exploration.  I've barely had time to scratch the surface of that
one, and it already has more potential than this orangutan-man proposal.
But then again, I am obviously rather prejudicial on that particular
subject.  :-)

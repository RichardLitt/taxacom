Thank you to everyone who made suggestions on and off the list. Choices were

Labrys or double-headed axe - perhaps labriform.

securiform having the form of an axe or hatchet.

dolabriform, or shaped like a mattock or a pickax.

All of these are potentially good, although they describe the form of an ax
rather than a specific blade shape. Perhaps labriform might be closest
since a double headed axe blade seems to be consistent in shape as an
equally expanded blade that has a convex cutting edge and pointed corner at
each end. Perhaps I am making a mountain out of molehill. Anyway, I might
try labriform and see if colleagues and reviewers can stomach that.  If
there were a labriform leaf I am sure that there would be a term already
for just the single blade (rather than double for labrys).

This got me thinking (a rare thing :) that the valva is expanded in a way
not unlike a Roman Centurion crest which is called a crista transversa.
Maybe 'cristate' or 'critiform' as an adjective (or can someone with Latin
knowledge correct me?)?

Cheers, John

Respected Taxacomers:

I am bemused that this topic, so much debated on Taxacom, be resurrected pretty near at the final gasp of this listserver. As long as cluster analysis using molecular data is well funded, paraphyly will be a device of the devil.

Paraphyly can be good stuff. This is because apophyly-paraphyly pairs imply an ancestor-descendant relationship. Those who would like an alternative ("high-resolution phylogenetics") to molecular-only cluster analysis should read my recent papers on ResearchGate and Academia.edu. For a short and readable damnation of cladistics, I suggest Stopping Rules for Two-Sigma Structural Monophyly in Morphology-Based High-Resolution Phylogenetics Ecol. Divers.  2025, 2(4), 10013

    Yes, thankfully most workers still recognize a paraphyletic Class Reptilia, a paraphyletic Class Amphibia, a paraphyletic Class Crustacea, etc.
      Unfortunately strict cladists have made a mess of the classification of life at the highest levels.  Particularly the Three Domain classification of prokaryotes.  The classification of Domain Archaea/Archaebacteria is now a mess of Kingdoms, and there is no clear evidence that Archaea are more archaic than Bacteria.  A single Kingdom Prokaryota giving rise to the eukaryotic Kingdoms still makes more sense.  Instead we have a mess and many still think "Archaea" is an appropriate taxon name.

Paraphyly has been the way major evolutionary history went, as Paul noted.

exist. Reptiles - to birds and mammals, and they are still around. Insects
are nested within crustaceans. Would you prefer rather splitting
crustaceans? One can split or merge, but the history is there.

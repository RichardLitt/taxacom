Hei Mikhail,

Some phorids, such as Nannohelea, are so distinctive that it would be difficult to recognize them as dipterans (or even as insects), let alone as phorids. They lack virtually all of the diagnostic morphological characters of Phoridae, yet they are still placed in the family simply because they are phorids.

https://nam10.safelinks.protection.outlook.com/?url=https%3A%2F%2Facademic.oup.com%2Faesa%2Farticle%2F105%2F4%2F550%2F22206%3Flogin%3Dtrue&data%7C02%7Ctaxacom%40lists.ku.edu%7Ce9927a997cae4a4d73fb08de3985db38%7C3c176536afe643f5b96636feabbe3c1a%7C0%7C0%7C639011444246823247%7CUnknown%7CTWFpbGZsb3d8eyJFbXB0eU1hcGkiOnRydWUsIlYiOiIwLjAuMDAwMCIsIlAiOiJXaW4zMiIsIkFOIjoiTWFpbCIsIldUIjoyfQ%3D%3D%7C0%7C%7C%7C&sdata=WXjrNyd2pie3KclcNHZaMlX5gTJUtfCwI8HSKcibchY%3D&reserved=0

There are many other similar examples.

For this reason, I think fragmenting a morphologically uniform Gammarus solely because of unusual Baikal lineages would be more of a taxonomic disaster than the alternative. Treating the unusual Baikal forms as Gammarus seems the more reasonable and less disruptive solution. Comparable decisions have been made many times in other groups.

Marko

Hei Marko,

Your idea indeed came to minds of some researchers, but never materialised.

Sometimes genera are really close to each other, and one may neglect that difference to merge. But if the difference is so large, merging would indeed be unnatural. For certain mindset it may seem like solving problems, but to a specialist it looks like no problems at all. On the contrary, if one ends up creating 1000 species of Gammarus looking more different than a sawfly and an ant, this will be a taxonomic disaster. I would not do that.
I would rather accept things as they are, rather than putting facts into a frame of a dogma.

Sincerely,

Mikhail

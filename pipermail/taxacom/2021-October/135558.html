 Hi Stephen,
Re: "Bos(s) domesticus," not to make a mountain out of a molehill, but in
my opinion this joke is, at best, weirdly out of place, and more
realistically casual misogyny which is not a good look. Especially given
the underrepresentation of women in taxonomy and the ICZN commission
specifically, let's be a little more cautious about the tone we set, and a
bit more cognizant that what is light-hearted to some might feel pretty
crappy and exclusionary to others, who are too used to this kind of thing
being representative of a larger cultural issue (not implying that's
necessarily the case here, but it certainly can look and feel that way).
Don't need any particular reply, thanks for thinking about it!
Cheers

> Hi, all. It came to my attention today that there is a dispute over the
> taxonomy of the "Bali cattle" (a type of banteng), as to whether it
> should be "Bos domesticus" or "Bos javanicus domesticus". My initial
> thought was "Wait - could *either* of these really be correct? Surely
> that name must have been used for regular cattle and not the banteng."
>
> In trying to sort this out, I've consulted Wilson & Reeder's definitive
> work, "Mammal Species of the World: A Taxonomic and Geographic
> Reference, Volume 1" (see pp. 691-692). There are several authorities
> that have published the species (or subspecies) epithet domesticus and
> it was either originally placed in the genus Bos or moved there later.
> The oldest such citation I can find listed is Erxleben, 1777 (there is
> also domesticus Fitzinger, 1860 as well). The point is this: Bos
> domesticus Wilckens, 1905 (used for the banteng) surely cannot be a
> valid name under the ICZN because the name would be a junior homonym of
> Bos domesticus Erxleben, 1777. This is an entirely different question
> from whether or not it is a species or a subspecies. Regardless of its
> taxonomic status, it cannot be named "domesticus", unless the genus it
> was placed in formerly, Bibos, is restored as a valid genus. Evidently,
> that is why no one had recognized or addressed this problem; it didn't
> become a problem until Bibos was placed as a subgenus of Bos, which
> means that Wilckens' name requires a replacement.
>
> But, I don't dabble in mammal taxonomy, so even though what I find
> Googling doesn't suggest anyone knows about the problem, I'm curious as
> to whether this IS known, and someone is doing (or has done) something
> about it.

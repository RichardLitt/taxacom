Yes, at least two species of insect were collected by the Everest
expedition of 1921 at 5,639m. One was a new species of cockroach
- Eupolyphaga everestiana, and the other may have been a bumblebee. See my
online cockroach catalogue here:
https://nam10.safelinks.protection.outlook.com/?url=http%3A%2F%2Fcockroach.speciesfile.org%2FCommon%2Fbasic%2FTaxa.aspx%3FTaxonNameID%3D1178021&data=05%7C01%7Ctaxacom%40lists.ku.edu%7C132087517d044d631ec408da24a523a4%7C3c176536afe643f5b96636feabbe3c1a%7C0%7C0%7C637862589402323694%7CUnknown%7CTWFpbGZsb3d8eyJWIjoiMC4wLjAwMDAiLCJQIjoiV2luMzIiLCJBTiI6Ik1haWwiLCJXVCI6Mn0%3D%7C3000%7C%7C%7C&sdata=0jvcFbe46CGPzLhWGzO0Kgc9UiZTA6jtrmA2RwKlRyA%3D&reserved=0

For an overview of the expedition see
https://nam10.safelinks.protection.outlook.com/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fen.wikipedia.org%2Fwiki%2F1921_British_Mount_Everest_reconnaissance_expedition&data=05%7C01%7Ctaxacom%40lists.ku.edu%7C132087517d044d631ec408da24a523a4%7C3c176536afe643f5b96636feabbe3c1a%7C0%7C0%7C637862589402323694%7CUnknown%7CTWFpbGZsb3d8eyJWIjoiMC4wLjAwMDAiLCJQIjoiV2luMzIiLCJBTiI6Ik1haWwiLCJXVCI6Mn0%3D%7C3000%7C%7C%7C&sdata=wh%2BQq4VPzCT3pInxkcmVeBfv8QvZzaKP8vz%2FUza61nA%3D&reserved=0

> Hi everyone,
>
> Earlier today Michael Heads was asking about a great collector that lived
> in Sichuan and as part of Michael's email I noticed that he was asking
> about a locality at around 4,800 m (15,900 feet).
>
> It reminded me of a (admittedly, somewhat silly) question that I have been
> meaning to ask to this list for a while. Have any entomological collections
> ever made at altitudes higher than 4,500 m (~14,500 feet)? I am not asking
> for observations of something seen flying (which I know there are quite a
> few observations of bumble bees and I believe even one Lepidoptera that
> were seen flying at really high altitudes in the Himalaya) but rather more
> "solid" collecting such as traps, or netting...
>
> The main reason is a bit silly, as I am interested in having some
> "records" for my pet group of parasitoid wasps (Braconidae). Especially the
> subfamily Microgastrinae which are caterpillar (Lepidoptera) parasitoids.
> As far as I know there is a potential to have some species occurring at
> altitudes a bit over 4,500 m. But I have not found any solid evidence yet.
> Based on specimens housed here in the Canadian National Collection, I
> described in 2018 a species of Microplitis from four specimens collected in
> Colorado (Mount Evans)  at altitudes between 3,658 m (holotype) and 4,267 m
> (paratypes) (link to paper here in case someone is interested in seeing the
> species, which I named as "altissimus" to refer to the altitude it was
> collected:
> https://nam10.safelinks.protection.outlook.com/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fzookeys.pensoft.net%2Farticles.php%3Fid%3D22869&data=05%7C01%7Ctaxacom%40lists.ku.edu%7C132087517d044d631ec408da24a523a4%7C3c176536afe643f5b96636feabbe3c1a%7C0%7C0%7C637862589402323694%7CUnknown%7CTWFpbGZsb3d8eyJWIjoiMC4wLjAwMDAiLCJQIjoiV2luMzIiLCJBTiI6Ik1haWwiLCJXVCI6Mn0%3D%7C3000%7C%7C%7C&sdata=NxgRGFU%2Fpv5EUJ75W66qc3jwpxNJvnQsWrRts9d3gfk%3D&reserved=0)
> . There is a road that reaches the summit of that mountain and thus it is
> relatively easy for collectors to access that mountain and collect at such
> altitude (the mountain peak reaches 4,350 m or some 14,200 feet, but I have
> not seen any specimen from higher than 4,267 m). Similarly, I am describing
> now a new species in the genus Dolichogenidea collected in Ecuador and Peru
> at 4,000-4,100 m, but here it falls a bit short of my previous species
> "record". And I cannot find any historical reference higher either. Thus,
> at least until this point I have failed to find any evidence of collecting
> for parasitoid wasps at altitudes higher than 4,200 m or so.
>
> However, I believe that there must be some collecting done at higher
> altitudes, as probably the main limitation is the logistical and physical
> demands of reaching such places. But I am pretty sure that there are many
> places in the Andes, close to the Equator, where some vegetation can be
> found way over 4,300 m (in fact, more like close to 5,000m), and thus any
> entomological expedition done there should have collected parasitoid wasps.
> Same could apply to Africa (I would assume that Kilimanjaro would be a good
> guess? But there are also a few other possibilities), and perhaps to
> Himalaya. The record for the highest tree line is discussed in many places
> (I believe that South America wins that one) but actually even if only
> grasses that would be sufficient for potentially having caterpillars and
> therefore, parasitoid wasps attacking them. After all, in the northermost
> locality in the world (Alert, Ellesmere Island, Canada, 82°30′N) with a
> brutal artic environment, there are many species of parasitoid wasps. So, I
> gather that finding them at altitudes of around 4,500 would be reasonable.
>
> Which brings me back to my question if anyone in this list knows about
> such potential entomological expeditions that have happened at that (or
> higher) altitude.
>
> Thanks a lot for any info you can share! And my apologies for such a long
> and probably silly post...
> Cheers,
> Jose

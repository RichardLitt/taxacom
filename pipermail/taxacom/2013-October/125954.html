I tend to agree with Doug Yanega (and the remarks by Scott Thomson) that the Wikipedia entry on Ray Hoser conforms to the Wikipedia standards, but I would make a couple of points if I may:

Given the nature of Wikipedia (and that of it's eclectic band of compilers) completeness with any entry will always be an elusive dream, so it's to be expected that more could be said on any subject - in particular someone like Ray. I mean you could write books on him or probably any of the taxacom members for that matter, and still have plenty of room for revisions. So it couldn't be expected to be complete, and everyone should appreciate that - including Ray. But while the Wikipedia piece has obviously been constructed from (some of) the historical record, that defense cannot be used to make the argument that the piece is therefore an accurate and unbiased assessment of Ray Hoser and his work. Notwithstanding the fact that my own work and character have been similarly attacked with unjustified smear, and a calculated use of unscientific and unethical conduct by the very same individuals that Ray is complaining about, I find the piece about Ray Hoser to be little more than another hatchet-job that hides behind the trusting credibility of a third party. That the world's most prestigious scientific journal 'Nature', as well as some of the most renowned scientists in zoology have been hood-winked into jumping onto this bandwagon of suppression largely on the basis of a compilation of fear-porn formed by exaggerations, deliberate misrepresentations, and even outright lies by a relatively small band of conspirators with vested interests, is quite frankly more disturbing than anything that has been said or written by Ray Hoser. This is no place to meticulously dissect the Wikipedia entry on Ray Hoser or the behaviour of his detractors and I will not do that. But one has only to look at his own relatively vast published contributions that have not been anywhere near adequately quoted in relation to those of his detractors, and one is forced to ask why? And how can the Wikipedia piece on Ray Hoser profess to include any credible discussion on Ray Hoser's allegations against corrupt public servants and the like when it fails to also mention the fact that repeated Government Inquiries have either confirmed his allegations and/or resulted in major court actions that have also vindicated his claims? Yes, the Wikipedia piece has much fact on Ray Hoser, and although a very entertaining read, it also shows much skill to the uninitiated in how the creeping extent of intellectual suppression can manifest itself. From what I can see a gaggle of self-appointed gate-keepers of taxonomy are now even attempting the perversion of the Rules of the Code of Zoological Nomenclature to achieve their ends by publishing and promoting the standards proffered in the Kaiser et al paper, and virtually nothing is being done about it by the Commissioners of the ICZN ! And some have the gaul to treat us as taxonomic vandals - with one 'learned scholar' even having the nerve to claim that I was "the AIDS of Zoology". But it's not me with a disease, although I may have the cure...

In any case thank you for your time in considering my opinion,

Richard Wells

> added to Wikipedia must have been published previously by a reliable 
> source <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:SOURCES>. Editors may 
> not add their own views to articles simply because they believe them to 
> be correct, and may not remove sources' views from articles simply 
> because they disagree with them.
> 
> The phrase "the threshold for inclusion is verifiability, not truth" 
> meant that verifiability is a necessary condition (a minimum 
> requirement) for the inclusion of material, though it is not a 
> sufficient condition (it may not be enough). Sources must also be 
> appropriate, and must be used carefully, and must be balanced relative 
> to other sources per Wikipedia's policy on due and undue weight 
> <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:WEIGHT>.
> 
> Wikipedia's articles are intended as intelligent summaries and 
> reflections of current published debate within the relevant fields, an 
> overview of the relevant literature. The Verifiability policy is related 
> to another core content policy, Neutral point of view 
> <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Neutral_point_of_view>, which 
> holds that we include all significant views on a subject. Citing 
> reliable sources for any material challenged or likely to be challenged 
> gives readers the chance to check for themselves that the most

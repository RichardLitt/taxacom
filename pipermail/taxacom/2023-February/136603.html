It's not entirely true that names above the family group are unregulated. Article 1.2. of ICZN4 notes articles that regulate names above the family group. Among the articles that apply are the articles on publication, so it is required that the name be published according to the Code. However, the rules on priority and authorship don't apply to higher taxa.

External.

 The problem with putting your preferred authorship on names like Anura on Wikimedia is that your preferred authorship is just that and not an objective fact because there is no such thing as authorship for such names. It is a common misconception, but a misconception nonetheless. There are no codified rules to define the authorship of such names. Nothing to say that the authorship is the first author. Nothing to say that the work in which the name was published must be published according to the Code, etc., etc. It is all just pointless dust in the wind.Stephen

On Thu, 23 Feb 2023 at 08:08, Stephen Thorpe <stephen_thorpe at yahoo.co.nz> wrote:

 Tony said "I realise that the ICZN Code does not regulate names and/or cited authorships above the rank of family (or more correctly, family-group), however I was wondering whether anyone can assist me with the "best" i.e. most correct authorship for the amphibian order Anura."
I say: There is no "correct" authorship, precisely because it is not regulated by the ICZN Code. There are bibliographic facts about where and when the name appeared, but these facts have no relevance to taxonomy or nomenclature. Priority does not apply.

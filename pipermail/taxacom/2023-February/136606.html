Yes Stephen, one could do that, with a certain amount of re-engineering
(not under my control any more in this case)... however 99%+ of the time
the "name+author+year" system does work (which is why it has been adopted
as the de facto standard in virtually all taxonomic data systems as well as
the taxonomic literature) - e.g. in the example given above, the crustacean
order is Decapoda Latreille, 1802 while the cephalopod order is Decapoda
Leach, 1818 (now Teuthoidea - or possibly Teuthida - Naef, 1916 (Teuthida
is accepted in ITIS at this time but not in WoRMS, more on that would be a
separate thread...)

Go e.g. to ION/Zoological Record and you will find "name+author+year" as de
facto taxon identifiers, same in Neave, same in Global Names Index, same in
ITIS, same in GBIF, etc. etc. At least some attempt at standardization in
these matters is clearly helpful :) There are a few instances where such a
construct is not unique, where indeed some workarounds are required - e.g.
in my case I tend to use name|| ||authority, name|| ||authority|| [2] and
so on, others may have their own preference...

Regards - Tony

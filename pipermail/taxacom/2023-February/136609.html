Yes Stephen, one could do that, with a certain amount of re-engineering (not under my control any more in this case)... however 99%+ of the time the "name+author+year" system does work (which is why it has been adopted as the de facto standard in virtually all taxonomic data systems as well as the taxonomic literature) - e.g. in the example given above, the crustacean order is Decapoda Latreille, 1802 while the cephalopod order is Decapoda Leach, 1818 (now Teuthoidea - or possibly Teuthida - Naef, 1916 (Teuthida is accepted in ITIS at this time but not in WoRMS, more on that would be a separate thread...)
Go e.g. to ION/Zoological Record and you will find "name+author+year" as de facto taxon identifiers, same in Neave, same in Global Names Index, same in ITIS, same in GBIF, etc. etc. At least some attempt at standardization in these matters is clearly helpful :) There are a few instances where such a construct is not unique, where?indeed some workarounds are required - e.g. in my case I tend to use name|| ||authority, name|| ||authority|| [2] and so on, others may have their own preference...

On Thu, 23 Feb 2023 at 12:52, Stephen Thorpe <stephen_thorpe at yahoo.co.nz> wrote:

 The problem with putting your preferred authorship on names like Anura on Wikimedia is that your?preferred authorship is just that and not an objective fact because there is no such thing as authorship for such names. It is a common misconception, but a misconception nonetheless. There are no codified rules to define the authorship of such names. Nothing to say that the authorship is the first author. Nothing to say that the work in which the name was published must be published according to the Code, etc., etc. It is all just pointless dust in the wind.Stephen

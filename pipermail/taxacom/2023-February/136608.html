Anoures Dum?ril, 1805, Zool. Analyt. Meth. Nat. Class. Anim.: 91, 93.
Unavailable family-group name for frogs within Dum?ril's order Batracii.
Dubois, 2004, Alytes, 22: 1?14, regarded this as the original use of Anura,
but Frost, Grant, Faivovich, Bain, Haas, Haddad, de S?, Channing,
Wilkinson, Donnellan, Raxworthy, Campbell, Blotto, Moler, Drewes, Nussbaum,
Lynch, Green, and Wheeler, 2006, Bull. Am. Mus. Nat. Hist., 297: 357, who
beyond the fact that this is an explicit family-group name, saw no
justification in recognizing non-Latin names in ranks not regulated by the
International Code of Zoological Nomenclature. Dubois and Raffa?lli, 2012,
Alytes, 28: 86, and Fouquette and Dubois, 2014, Checklist N.A. Amph. Rept.:
253, continued to argue for this being the oldest available name for the
taxon composed of all frogs, avoiding Dumeril's own words that this is the
"Famille. Anoures".

Anuri Fischer von Waldheim, 1813, Zoognosia Tab. Synopt. Ill., Ed. 3, 1:
58. Unranked taxon (above family-group) for frogs. Identical in content to
Fischer von Waldheim's Ecaudati. Presumably a reranking and latinization of
Anoures Dum?ril, 1806, an unavailable family-group name.

Anuria Rafinesque, 1815, Analyse Nat.: 78. Suborder for frogs. Presumably a
reranking and latinization of Anoures Dum?ril, 1806.

Anoura Gray, 1825, Ann. Philos., London, Ser. 2, 10: 213. Explicit order
for all frogs, attributed to Dum?ril and considering Salientia Laurenti to
be a synonym (although Laurenti's Salientia included Proteus). A reranking
and latinization of Anoures Dum?ril, 1806.

Anura Hogg, 1839, Mag. Nat. Hist., N.S., 3: 270. Explicit order for frogs.
Presumably an emendation of Anoura Gray, 1825, although possibly a
reranking and latinization of Anoures Dum?ril, 1806.

Anoures Dum?ril and Bibron, 1841, Erp. Gen., 8: plate opposite page 53.
Non-Latin suborder for frogs. Presumably a reranking of Anoures Dum?ril,
1806.

Anouri Mayer, 1849, Verh. Naturhist. Ver. Preuss. Rheinlande, 6: 198.
Suborder for frogs. Presumably a latinization of Anoures Dum?ril, 1806, or
of Dum?ril and Bibron, 1841.

Anura Lichtenstein and Martens, 1856, Nomencl. Rept. Amph. Mus. Zool.
Berol.: 36. Order for frogs. Presumably a subsequent usage of Anura Hogg,
1839.

Anura Romer, 1945, Vert. Paleontol., Ed. 2: 592. Order of superorder
Salientia to contain living frogs.

Anura Ford and Cannatella, 1993, Herpetol. Monogr., 7: 99. Group of frogs
defined as the last common ancestor of living frogs and all of the
descendants of that species.

John wrote: "I figure better to name such specimens now and let the future
work out synonymies if they exist."

     I agree completely!  These days, when species are being lost so fast,
we need to docuent  everything we can.  If specimens turn out just to be
aberant members of a known species, they are still important as
contributions to the knowledge about that species.
     Years ago, my professor answered a similar question of mine with,
"Don't bother with it!  If the species is extinct, it has no systematic
value".  I don't know if he really believed that or was just trying to get
me to defend a point of view--we professors often do that!  However, he was
strongly against cladistic/phylogenetic studies, so I am sure that he
didn't consider the usefulness of an extinct species in understanding of
phylogenetic relationships to be important (or possible!).

     Yes, we need to document everything we can before it disappears, and
maybe that will even provide a basis for more protection.  (i.e., follow
the DAMA protocol).

Regards,
Scott Monks

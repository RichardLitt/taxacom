Hi Brendon,

Mike is probably right that it is not 'guessing' in the sense of just
making up any age, but my point is that transforming a given fossil record
into a prediction of the upper age of a taxon is problematic to say the
least. I have seen studies of the fossil records where it is argued that
for a given fossil range being over a 'sufficient' range of geological
horizons and 'sufficient' number of samples, that fossil range is supposed
to be predictive of the age of origin, but how that is necessarily so is
still beyond me. In other words, the date ranges are not in any empirical
way a necessary correlate of the upper age limit of a taxon.

â€œWhat is it that most authors do when they claim refutation of tectonic
correlation? I am not sure I follow this thought.â€�

They propose various chance dispersal scenarios. In other words, if their
fossil calibrated molecular age postdates a tectonic event, that event is
considered too early to be relevant. If there is a distributional gap
involved they then invoke chance dispersal. This argument is invalid since
the fossils provide minimum ages and so the molecular extrapolation is also
a minimum that does not falsify earlier origins.

â€œ I am not sure about the statement that many extant species have a
Mesozoic originâ€�

I did not say may, but I have found some examples of ghost moths with
Mesozoic tectonic boundaries.

So, to clarify for the purposes of discussion, do you accept that using
your dating methods that a taxon could still be older than that estimated
and therefore a given range estimate based on posterior probabilities has
nothing to do with how old a taxon may actually be and that a tectonic
correlation supporting an older origin is not falsified?

Cheers, John

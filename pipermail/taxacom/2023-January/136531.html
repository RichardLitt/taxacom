> I also am against the renaming of taxa for these types of reasons, largely
> for the reasons stated and implied by the authors of the paper. We
> nomenclatural taxonomists may like to get into the deeper meaning of names
> and where they come from, most people actually do not care. All they want
> is a name they can use for a taxon, which is stable and unchanging if
> possible and this includes the combination. If our work on list governance
> shows one thing is that stability of usage is the number one concern across
> the spectrum of end users of taxonomy. I am in general no fan of patronyms
> though I have done it twice, so obviously it's not something I have set in
> stone, more something I avoid if I can, it is also just personal
> preference. The vast majority of Patronyms the general user of the names do
> not care or even likely know who the taxon was named after (will depend on
> how famous the person was),it is not important to the day to day usage of
> names for taxa. So my view is let them be for the sake of stability,
> changing the names causes more harm than good,particularly for long
> standing and well used names. There is no value or point to this argument
> and it just wastes time and resources and creates confusion. That is not
> stability.
>
> Cheers Scott

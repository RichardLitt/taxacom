Although there has been debate about the exact interpretation, the
consensus seems to be that names invalidated under these provisions are
ones proposed knowing that the taxon fell into one of the categories, not
ones corrected by subsequent discovery.  The work of modern animals  is
contrasted nomenclaturally with fossilized traces, which are still nameable
under the ICZN code.  I don't remember the genus now, but there's a name
originally proposed to be a fossil gastropod, reinterpreted as a plant, and
now recognized as a spiral coprolite.  The genus can be used for coprolites
and could not be re-used for animals.

As another example, there was considerable debate over using the genus
name *Cornu
*in gastropods, because the original specimen was a mutant individual.
However, in the description, Born (1778) shows no awareness that he had a
weird mutant form of a common species, so the genus *Cornu *is available.

John,
The truth is that human nature, linked to both economic and broadly "egoistic" factors, is a massive obstruction to what could be achieved in terms of online taxonomic/scientific databases. Unless people can learn to work together, we are going to be forever stuck in the current situation of having too many more or less independent databases, all of which are far from ideal.
Cheers,
Stephen

 does not show the full

 listed already so its a bit tricky in that respect (or
 I missed a step) - and it appears to be lacking

 my group - I
 tried a well established genus Endoclita first designated
 in
 1874 and got nothing.
 
 And of course there are other layers of
 complexity such as generic
 designations
 which, in my group, underwent a  reversal with a checklist
 in
 2000 downgrading a bunch of genera under
 one name (and as a checklist there
 was no
 justification) and then reinstatement (with justification)
 of those
 names in 2012. IRMNG has a
 promising layout as I can see full species lists
 and looks attractive from that aspect, although
 very much out of date due
 to its reliance on
 the out of date NHM database - one particularly
 problematic example was a genus name Achladaeus
 that was inserted for a
 series of species
 under another well established name (Aenetus), but
 Achladaeus was already known to be a nomen
 nudum and it was recorded as
 such on the
 original files of the NHM. When I contacted NHM I was told
 that
 there were no resources for making the
 correction at the time (a few years
 back),
 but they have now made that correction but the genus is
 still
 presented as valid in IRMNG. However,
 that could be something for me to
 rectify.
 At the time of the original entry of Achladaeus it was
 interesting
 to see how the name spread like
 wildfire through all sorts of web sites.
 Made for real confusion.
 
 I will think on this further, but at this time
 IRMNG seems to have a layout
 or structure
 that is most easily accessible for me (so emphasis on this
 as
 a personal perspective with respect to my
 strengths and weakness)  to
 understand what
 is present or not. Again, no criticism implied above for
 any databases as its a truly massive challenge
 and I am just a taxonomic
 hobbyist for a
 small, obscure group, and without intensive taxonomic
 database skills.

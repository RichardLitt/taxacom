Hi Tony (and whoever else is reading your thread... hopefully not only me! ;-)

I am curious to know if there is someone in this list with enough experience to help less advanced users of Taxapad. In collaboration with friend and colleague Darren Ward, I have kept a website on world species of microgastrine parasitoid wasps (Hymenoptera, Braconidae, almost 3,000 species altogether) for around three years now, but I find the setting/maintaining of the site is neither simple nor intuitive -and certainly the importing and exporting of data and images is not straightforward! I like A LOT the idea behind Scratchpads, and would love to add/update more data in the site we keep, but have grown frustrated working with it (changes are sometimes made and it is difficult to return to earlier versions, uploading of images and attaching them to individual taxa takes time, things are not intuitive, the help function is not that useful, tutorials in the Scratchpads site do not cover many important things that would be great to have explained, and so many other time-consuming things that are just difficult to do). I am aware that perhaps the problem is not Scratchpads per se but me... However, I would love to hear from someone willing to offer advice on how to improve Scratchpads sites (from both an aesthetic point of view and (most importantly) from a functional point of view). We would welcome any help, and, if anyone is interested in spending time helping us to improve our Scratchpads site, we would of course add her/him as coauthor in the site citation. I do not mean any publicity for our site here, but provide the link in case anyone is willing to take a look and provide some advice (http://microgastrinae.myspecies.info/). Thanks a lot in advance!

In any case, I am very much in favour of Scratchpads, and also of the possibility of linking data there to publications in journals such as 'Biodiversity Data Journal', something that is being explored by several colleagues (e.g., the British Checklist of Hymenoptera). Again, the theory behind that is much easier than the actual practice of linking things (and the actual difficulties to do that). Not complaining, just hoping things would improve in those fronts, at least for the least advance users like me :-(

Cheers,
Jose

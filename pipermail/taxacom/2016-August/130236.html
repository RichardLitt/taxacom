Thomson Reuters has "standardized" Zoological Record to match their other
indexing, or in other words eliminated much of the zoological usefulness.

Many nomenclatural databases do not make it easy to determine how (or if)
additions and corrections can be submitted, though others are quite
responsive.  Ultimately their reliability will depend upon adequate support
for the taxonomic expertise necessary for data generation and verification,
as well as good systems to facilitate and assess contributions.  WoRMS does
a good job, but I don't think it's an option for lepidopterans.
Paleobiology Database suffers from the usual issues for crowdsourcing - the
crowd varies in expertise and thoroughness.

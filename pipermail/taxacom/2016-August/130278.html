> No doubt there are many such cases. This makes me very
> uneasy whenever somebody tries to pass off the Catalogue
> of Life as a source of "vetted names". This "vetting" does
> not extend even to eliminating obvious homonyms (and the
> combinations with these), which should be easy enough.
>
> For example, the Catalogue of Life has two Romanoa's,
> one plant and one fungus, each with one species.
> In this case it is very easy to look in the Index Fungorum,
> where the fungal Romanoa is marked as being a later
> homonym and illegitimate (for some reason, IF has not
> marked the combination Romanoa terricola as
> "unavailable for use" and copied it into Species fungorum).
> It cannot be all that hard to make a list of such cases
> (names that cannot be used for a taxon) and eliminate
> them from each upload to the Catalogue of Life. This would
> still leave the cases that are more difficult to detect, but it
> would show that the Catalogue of Life is at least trying.
> The way it is now, it just disseminates a lot of nonsense,
> apparently completely uncaring.
>
> Paul

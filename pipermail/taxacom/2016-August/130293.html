Hi Paul,

The taxonomic names in GBIF come from checklists such as Catalogue of Life, etc., not the specimen labels (a misconception I also had). When a collection uploads its data GBIF attempts to match the names on the labels to the names in the checklist, with varying degree of success.

If the checklists are incomplete or out of date, then GBIF struggles to match the names (leaving aside the issue of whether the names on the labels are OK). Given that GBIF has only old copies of IPNI and IF, doesn’t have ZooBank or ION (the source for BioNames) it struggles to match newer taxa (the Catalogue of Life is out of date, and sometimes horribly so).

We’d be doing a lot better if people and organisations with good quality lists of names would make them freely available. Nomenclators are the best source of names, but amongst the worst offenders in terms of being open. Hence, GBIF is messy.

Regards,

Rod

Hi Tony,

I'd argue that focus sing on getting. A list of names alone is not enough, I'd spin the problem around and say we should get the taxonomic literature, because that contains the names linked to evidence. We're awash in unsupported lists of names, which we endlessly recycle up the food chain until the end up in places like GBIF and nobody has a clue where they came from or whether they are actual names.

I think we're still proceeding as if the literature was physical and we need to create 5x3 index cards of names. We can do a lot better than that.

Regards,

Hi John,

I am glad you had a good experience with IRMNG (also in its new
incarnation, now running on a parallel database system + software to that
created for WoRMS). Since its inception I have performed the function of
IRMNG "global editor" (with some welcome assistance from others) but the
intention has been stated to potentially set up a network of distributed
editors responsible for particular groups, as per WoRMS, and in addition
for marine groups, eventually automatically propagate changes and new
content from WoRMS as it is entered there to avoid the requirement for
double handling and to keep the two systems in step.

With regard to the specific taxonomic.nomenclatural exaample issues you
raise, it is easy for me (or a future other editor) to change the
status of Achladaeus
(accepted name to nomen nudum)  - I can do that now if you wish - but it
also raises the question of what to do with its present 32 quoted species,
which I think are still being treated as accepted (i.e. unchanged)  in the
2016 Catalogue of Life. I checked LepIndex just now at the NHM and they are
still listing Achladaeus as a valid genus with around 23 valid species as
well as a number of unaccepted ones, so ideally if one were to depart from
this treatment one would need a separate published source to follow.

For an example name in that genus still treated as valid in the current
version of LepIndex, see *ACHLADAEUS acaciae *Pfitzner, 1933:
http://www.nhm.ac.uk/our-science/data/lepindex/search/detail.dsml?UserID=&UserName=&TaxonNo=136628.0&SCIENTIFIC_NAME_on_card=acaciae&listPageURL=list%2edsml%3fSCIENTIFIC_NAME_on_cardqtype%3dstarts%2bwith%26sort%3dSCIENTIFIC%255fNAME%255fon%255fcard%26SCIENTIFIC_NAME_on_card%3dalboextremus%26recLimit%3d30&searchPageURL=index%2edsml%3fsort%3dSCIENTIFIC%255fNAME%255fon%255fcard%26SCIENTIFIC_NAME_on_cardqtype%3dstarts%2bwith%26SCIENTIFIC_NAME_on_card%3dalboextremus%26recLimit%3d30

(no idea if that link will survive email transport...)

apparently this species was originally erected in the genus Chargia which
according to most conventions would require the author and year to be
placed in parantheses (as I recall Lepidopterans often do not do this??)

Anyway, all things are possible (or actually, most things), it just depends
what one wishes to do and where best to do it...

Regards - Tony

> was no justification) and then reinstatement (with justification) of those
> names in 2012. IRMNG has a promising layout as I can see full species lists
> and looks attractive from that aspect, although very much out of date due
> to its reliance on the out of date NHM database - one particularly
> problematic example was a genus name Achladaeus that was inserted for a
> series of species under another well established name (Aenetus), but
> Achladaeus was already known to be a nomen nudum and it was recorded as
> such on the original files of the NHM. When I contacted NHM I was told that
> there were no resources for making the correction at the time (a few years
> back), but they have now made that correction but the genus is still
> presented as valid in IRMNG. However, that could be something for me to
> rectify. At the time of the original entry of Achladaeus it was interesting
> to see how the name spread like wildfire through all sorts of web sites.
> Made for real confusion.
>
> I will think on this further, but at this time IRMNG seems to have a layout
> or structure that is most easily accessible for me (so emphasis on this as
> a personal perspective with respect to my strengths and weakness)  to
> understand what is present or not. Again, no criticism implied above for
> any databases as its a truly massive challenge and I am just a taxonomic
> hobbyist for a small, obscure group, and without intensive taxonomic
> database skills.

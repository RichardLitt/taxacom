Sorry for coming in late, I see this thread only right now, being back 
from holidays.

We analysed Garsault's various publications and came to the conclusion 
that some of them are consistently binominal under the Code. This is why 
the new names published there are not marked as unavailable in AnimalBase.

Please read the publications which we published in 2008 and 2009, where 
we discussed in detail the results of our analyses. Some questions 
raised here were answered there.

For marking a publication as non-binominal under the Code there are no 
clear guides, but if there were, Garsault's 1764 publication would 
certainly not meet the criteria of restiction or ban from the point of 
view of binominality. Garsault's work has never been suppressed.

Actually for many decades no zoologist knew that it existed at all. 
Garsault's work had been overlooked by zoologists for a pretty long 
time. One of the reasons for that was the mode of citation, particularly 
of citation of the authorship for the work, which had the effect that 
the zoological community lost entirely track of this relatively rare 
work. This however, cannot lead to a work being identified as 
non-binominal. We agree entirely with Alain Dubois in this point. We do 
not agree in other points considering the interpretation of certain Code 
provisions.

Francisco

Rod said "By combining taxonomic names (“checklists”) and collection data from natural history collections (“occurrences”) it generates species distributions."

Actually, no! That would be so only on the BIG assumption that the occurrence data and the associated identifications are both reliable. For terrestrial invertebrates from most places in the world, that is only true of the few recently revised groups.

 The World Spider Catalog is excellent for all of the things
 mentioned. One aspect that is less than ideal even in many
 curated lists of species is the inability to search with
 geographic names e.g. countries. Of course curated lists are
 put together by taxonomists for taxonomists and the focus is
 on taxa rather than areas but the most important end user
 IMHO is the local naturalist who wants to generate an
 authoritative list of species for the area he/she is
 interested in. There are some curated lists that go beyond
 type localities and incorporate distribution information for
 each species based on the literature covered but even in
 these cases searching by geographic terms is usually not
 possible (e.g. WSC). (Ironically, when the WSC existed as
 many html files it was possible to do a simple text search
 on each file!)
 
 I realize this is only tangentially relevant to the current
 thread and I do appreciate the huge effort required to
 include searchable geographic distribution information for
 each species. But I do think that should be the ultimate
 goal for all curated lists of species.

 Hello Mary, all,
 
 The World Spider Catalog (to which Angelo refers) is an
 excellent example
 of doing things well, so far as I can see. First, there is a
 huge effort to
 be comprehensive and a long history of continued releases
 and improvements,
 each citable as needed (see http://www.wsc.nmbe.ch/archive/). Second, the
 data, originally as flat files, have been transferred into a
 completely
 reprocessed relational database from 2014 onwards, as well
 as a change of
 agency for intended assured persistence, according to the
 account given at
 http://www.wsc.nmbe.ch/introduction.
 Third, all the taxonomic names data
 (with a slight version lag) are added to, and made available
 for download
 via, the Catalogue of Life, see
 http://www.catalogueoflife.org/col/details/database/id/29
 ; and last, but
 certainly not least, an email address is given for
 notification of errors
 and omissions. Presumably the same can be made for many
 other, if not quite
 all, Cat. of Life contributors for which we should be very
 grateful in the
 main; speaking from minor past interactions the CoL is
 always interested in
 improving data quality and does strive to replace known
 sources of lower
 quality with higher quality equivalents as and when possible
 (which is the
 principal means at its disposal)
 
 Regards - Tony (no present affiliation with either World
 Spider Catalog or
 Cat. of Life)

When I tried that link I noticed that had a link to view in EoL but one has
to sign up for access, the other was to view name sources which listed
several databases all invoking Achladaeus. Presumably they all got it (or
sequentially from one data base that did) from LepIndex and even though the
original mistake has apparently been corrected, the correction has not been
absorbed. Its almost like that the initial adoption of a databased name
goes out like a virus, but the subsequent correction is like
an inoculation that fails to overcome databse resistance. In a single
obscure case this might not matter, but if it happens over and over one
might wonder.

John Grehan

>  Let's not beat about the bush and
>  call a spade a spade - what is being talked about here is
>  the NHMUK's LepIndex database, of which I am now the
>  "owner". LepIndex online was originally a project to make
>  the extensive card indexes of the NHMUK available online to
>  everyone because many people were coming to visit solely to
>  consult those indexes, rich as they were in unpublished data
>  added over the years by NHMUK staff. I will not defend the
>  decision taken years ago to then feed this database to
>  Species2000 and thence on to all and sundry, as it is
>  seriously embarrassing, but it happened. What also happened
>  was a contraction in funding and a refocussing of priorities
>  that meant updating and editing of this and many other
>  databases did not happen. Furthermore, being on the NHMUK
>  website and so within its firewall meant that no-one outside
>  could edit it. We did have a system for submission of
>  corrections but as staff left for various reasons this fell
>  into disuse.
>  Anyway, that is history. A few years ago Chris Lyal and I
>  obtained some funding to begin to look into updating
>  LepIndex and moving it to a more open, community-friendly
>  platform. With the help of Justin Ollerenshaw and Peter
>  Wing, supported by Data Manager Adrian Hine, we updated the
>  higher classification and as much of the species level
>  classification as we could before the money ran out. We were
>  then aiming to transfer LepIndex, merged with the Generic
>  Names database, to a Species File. This we still aim to do
>  but to the newer system being developed by that team, Taxon
>  Works. It was slow going as this was not an official NHMUK
>  digital project but we are getting there. The database will
>  be moved shortly (I hope). Once in Taxon Works, and once the
>  front end of that system has been worked out, we would be
>  looking for specialists to register to act as curators of
>  certain groups. At that point, they can begin to remove the
>  remaining issues.
>
>  And, yes, in the current offline version of LepIndex,
>  Aenetus is the valid name, as we updated that group from
>  Gaden Robinson's checklist and the Moths of Australia
>  Checklist.
>

Hello Mary, all,

The World Spider Catalog (to which Angelo refers) is an excellent example
of doing things well, so far as I can see. First, there is a huge effort to
be comprehensive and a long history of continued releases and improvements,
each citable as needed (see http://www.wsc.nmbe.ch/archive/). Second, the
data, originally as flat files, have been transferred into a completely
reprocessed relational database from 2014 onwards, as well as a change of
agency for intended assured persistence, according to the account given at
http://www.wsc.nmbe.ch/introduction. Third, all the taxonomic names data
(with a slight version lag) are added to, and made available for download
via, the Catalogue of Life, see
http://www.catalogueoflife.org/col/details/database/id/29 ; and last, but
certainly not least, an email address is given for notification of errors
and omissions. Presumably the same can be made for many other, if not quite
all, Cat. of Life contributors for which we should be very grateful in the
main; speaking from minor past interactions the CoL is always interested in
improving data quality and does strive to replace known sources of lower
quality with higher quality equivalents as and when possible (which is the
principal means at its disposal)

Regards - Tony (no present affiliation with either World Spider Catalog or
Cat. of Life)

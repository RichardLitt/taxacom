 I am sure Stephen will
 say I have my own agenda here. But this time I do
 not.
 
 The
 general view in herpetology is that with some 830
 nomenclatural acts at
 various taxonomic
 levels across frogs, snakes, lizards, turtles and
 crocodiles it is not in the interests of anyone
 working with herpetofauna
 to follow these
 names. They are ignored, some journals will not even
 accept
 papers that use them. They are not
 followed by ether the IUCN or CITES
 making
 it impossible to use them due to legislative frameworks that
 are
 pretty strict when it comes to
 vertebrates. This is not my viewpoint per
 se, but a general consensus across  the field.
 I acknowledge the names are
 published, I
 have said so in press in the comments I was involved with
 on
 Case 3601.
 
 I get Stephen's point of view, and he is
 right. But in the year since the
 major
 comment I made on the Spracklandus case (3601) about 100
 more names
 have been produced. The issue
 does have to be dealt with. In the meantime,
 herpetology deals with it its own way.
 
 Cheers, Scott

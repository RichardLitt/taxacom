> As an alternative, I like to host as many new names as I can find (with
> realistic levels of effort) into my own system "IRMNG" (www.irmng.org),
> for which e.g. you can see current holdings for Hepialidae here:
> http://www.irmng.org/aphia.php?p=taxdetails&id=100937 . (Note, there may
> be additional genera already held but not yet allocated to that family,
> presently under "Lepidoptera - awaiting allocation"). My species names for
> Lepidoptera will be somewhat out-of-date, being based on the version of
> LepIndex as held in Cat. of Life 2006 (maybe much the same as today) but I
> am happy to have additions from newer sources as well. IRMNG content is
> also regularly exported to other systems for their use as desired including
> those of GBIF, EOL (Encyclopedia of Life), Glonal Names/Global Names
> Resolver, OTOL (Open Tree of Life), WoRMS (for marine taxa) and more. Take
> a look and see what you think...

>>> It's a good question, on which I have some views (which differ somewhat
>>> from Richard's), however I would be interested to see what others may
>>> suggest too... a lot depends on, I would suggest:
>>>
>>>   - to what group you are interested in contributing (also whether
>>> extant, fossil, or both)
>>>   - what aspects most interest you e.g. names/nomenclatural aspects,
>>> taxonomy/phylogeny, ecology, geographic distribution...
>>>   - whether you wish to contribute summaries of "mainstream"
>>> views/published literature or personal opinions/new interpretations (the
>>> latter may be less welcome than the former)
>>>
>>> Among my criteria for selecting a relevant vehicle for contributions
>>> would be (1) state of completeness already - since why re-do work
>>> unnecessarily if others have already done much of it, and (2) current
>>> impact/usage of the system - in other words if you put effort in, who will
>>> utilise it. In terms of the latter aspect, I would tend to favour databases
>>> already contributing to Catalogue of Life (for maximum impact/take-up),
>>> which are mainly "controlled units" of endeavour with their own individual
>>> contact points, or perhaps PaleoBiology Database for fossils (which is more
>>> open access I understand). Just some thoughts...

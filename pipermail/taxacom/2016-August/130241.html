 Hi John,
 
 I am glad you had a good
 experience with IRMNG (also in its new
 incarnation, now running on a parallel database
 system + software to that
 created for
 WoRMS). Since its inception I have performed the function
 of
 IRMNG "global editor" (with
 some welcome assistance from others) but the
 intention has been stated to potentially set up
 a network of distributed
 editors responsible
 for particular groups, as per WoRMS, and in addition
 for marine groups, eventually automatically
 propagate changes and new
 content from WoRMS
 as it is entered there to avoid the requirement for
 double handling and to keep the two systems in
 step.
 
 With regard to the
 specific taxonomic.nomenclatural exaample issues you
 raise, it is easy for me (or a future other
 editor) to change the
 status of
 Achladaeus
 (accepted name to nomen nudum) 
 - I can do that now if you wish - but it
 also raises the question of what to do with its
 present 32 quoted species,
 which I think are
 still being treated as accepted (i.e. unchanged)  in the
 2016 Catalogue of Life. I checked LepIndex just
 now at the NHM and they are
 still listing
 Achladaeus as a valid genus with around 23 valid species
 as
 well as a number of unaccepted ones, so
 ideally if one were to depart from
 this
 treatment one would need a separate published source to
 follow.
 
 For an example name
 in that genus still treated as valid in the current
 version of LepIndex, see *ACHLADAEUS acaciae
 *Pfitzner, 1933:
 http://www.nhm.ac.uk/our-science/data/lepindex/search/detail.dsml?UserID=&UserName=&TaxonNo=136628.0&SCIENTIFIC_NAME_on_card=acaciae&listPageURL=list%2edsml%3fSCIENTIFIC_NAME_on_cardqtype%3dstarts%2bwith%26sort%3dSCIENTIFIC%255fNAME%255fon%255fcard%26SCIENTIFIC_NAME_on_card%3dalboextremus%26recLimit%3d30&searchPageURL=index%2edsml%3fsort%3dSCIENTIFIC%255fNAME%255fon%255fcard%26SCIENTIFIC_NAME_on_cardqtype%3dstarts%2bwith%26SCIENTIFIC_NAME_on_card%3dalboextremus%26recLimit%3d30
 
 (no idea if that link will
 survive email transport...)
 
 apparently this species was originally erected
 in the genus Chargia which
 according to most
 conventions would require the author and year to be
 placed in parantheses (as I recall
 Lepidopterans often do not do this??)
 
 Anyway, all things are possible (or actually,
 most things), it just depends
 what one
 wishes to do and where best to do it...

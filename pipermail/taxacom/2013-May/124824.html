On 14/05/2013 23:30, Stephen Thorpe wrote:
> What we need is a coping strategy for dealing with poor quality 
> taxonomy and its associated nomenclature. Mostly it is easy. If Hoser 
> or whoever splits a genus into 10 genera, then you just treat them all 
> as synonyms if you disagree. This makes the author of those new genera 
> look foolish. Most such authors will probably take the hint before too 
> long ...
>

That's entirely true for the unnecessary splits - those names go 
straight into synonymy where they will bother (almost) no-one.

The problematic cases are those where one of the many names happens to 
end up as the oldest available name for a taxon that later genuine 
scientific work identifies as biologically real. Those who did the real 
work are then relegated to "redescribing" a taxon that has been named on 
the basis of no evidence, and are thus robbed of part of the fruit of 
their labour.

> I'm not good at being brief, but I'll try:
>
> For very small contributions of acts that affect zoological
> nomenclature, *in principle* the Bulletin of Zoological Nomenclature -
> the ICZN's journal - is a venue that handles several of the sorts of
> things you refer to. The reality is that the turnaround time for
> submissions to BZN is not fast, though it pains me to say it, as I am
> one of the assistant editors.
>
> Not too long ago, I asked the other Commissioners if we might want to
> set up some sort of "fast track" method of getting minor nomenclatural
> notes into circulation, like within a month of submission, and the
> response was lukewarm.
>
> I honestly think that there are a lot of taxonomists, myself included,
> who are sitting on one to many minor things, like corrections to
> spellings, unreplaced homonyms, unrecognized synonymies, gender
> agreement corrections, First Reviser actions, and other small but
> important items that need to be published, but are not part of some
> larger revisionary work. I'm not a snail taxonomist, for example, so
> what do I do if I discover a snail name that is being demonstrably
> misspelled in the literature? To my mind, these sorts of things are
> unified in that they should not require *significant* review, if any at
> all. In many cases, all it truly requires is one *qualified* reviewer.
>
> The idea of a venue to disseminate these sorts of acts is, in that
> respect, potentially very valuable if it can be implemented in such a
> way that three crucial things are part of the model:
>
> (1) there is an interface where *multiple* people can see any and all
> works submitted for publication, and *everyone* can act as a reviewer -
> the model of "only a tiny number of anonymous reviewers" is the most
> harmful self-imposed constraint on science that exists. This is why
> papers keep getting published that contain homonyms, synonyms,
> misspellings, multiple variant spellings, and other errors - NOT ENOUGH
> REVIEWERS. There is literally no reason why a paper can't have several
> dozen people review it, and catch every error before it makes it into
> print. The system is not hard to implement - everyone who subscribes
> gets auto-notified about every submission, and is free to review any of
> them. A submission could have a dozen reviews or more within 24 hours.
>
> (2) submissions are time-stamped, and assigned useful keywords. It is
> not helpful, for instance, if someone submits a new replacement name for
> a homonym, and then someone else sees this and submits their own
> suggestion for a replacement of the same name. A time stamp will ensure
> that it's "first come, first published", and mandatory keywords will
> make it easier to spot overlapping submissions. We can't have people at
> one another's proverbial throats, and there is already - absurdly enough
> - a cutthroat competition to replace homonyms. That sort of
> unprofessional behavior should be discouraged.
>
> (3) the new e-journal complies FULLY with the provisions in the Code for
> digital-only publication, so nomenclatural acts published in it are
> *available*, and not technically unpublished (a MAJOR problem with many
> existing e-journals, whose editors do not know or enforce the Code's
> strict regulations).
>
> If you can address those three issues, I'm fairly sure you'll find there
> is a real demand for that sort of venue.
>
> I might note that features #1 and #2 are both fully implemented in
> Wikipedia and Wikispecies, so they've been proven to work. What these
> venues lack is Code-compliance, and well-defined administrative oversight.
>

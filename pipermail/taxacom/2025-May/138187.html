All this discussion about dispersal got me to take a closer look at Cyclura
- last time was over two decades ago. Interesting (to me) is that of the 11
species I found listed, only two are perhaps sympatric on Hispaniola.Others
are allopatric - barring any marginal overlap (but need to yet read over a
paper that may have other information). So, with all this imagined rafting,
species land up on different places, and yet not on the mainland of Central
and South America or North America or lesser Antilles. But makes sense in a
tectonic context of ancestral Cyclura range being fragmented and
reorganized with movement of the northern edge of the Caribbean plate.
Happy to send my draft map to anyone interested. But if anyone knows of a
comprehensive published source of distributions for all species that would
be of interest to read. My current map is mostly from listings of islands
occupied.

Cheers, John Grehan

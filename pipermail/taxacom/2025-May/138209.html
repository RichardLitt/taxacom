Hi Guoyi,I confess that every time someone suggests that what we need is yet another journal, my heart sinks. We are awash in journals both old and new. Science is now data (and database) driven, so the real goal is having quality data. How do you imagine short notes on names will end up changing these databases? In the responses to your email, people have listed various journals that I suspect few will have heard of. Why do we think publishing a PDF on a web site will make things better?I think you want to invert your goal. It should be "how do we fixed issues in, say, taxonomic databases, and how do we give credit to the people making those fixes?". Pensoft's nanopublications seem relevant here - tiny data packages that annotate existing data or publish individual facts. But there the problem is who is going to make use of these?If you want inspiration I'd suggest looking at Bionomia https://nam10.safelinks.protection.outlook.com/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fbionomia.net%2F&data=05%7C02%7Ctaxacom%40lists.ku.edu%7C515d2e0d1005438c82ff08dd9aa0ec54%7C3c176536afe643f5b96636feabbe3c1a%7C0%7C0%7C638836738175670630%7CUnknown%7CTWFpbGZsb3d8eyJFbXB0eU1hcGkiOnRydWUsIlYiOiIwLjAuMDAwMCIsIlAiOiJXaW4zMiIsIkFOIjoiTWFpbCIsIldUIjoyfQ%3D%3D%7C0%7C%7C%7C&sdata=LkKvSGY1ZLqCYVUu3%2B6wXs1lPX%2FNALPk2%2F1IaYLrMJY%3D&reserved=0What David Shorthouse has managed to do is create a web site that has a community and feeds the activity of that community directly to GBIF, Zenodo, and ORCID.  No PDFs involved ;)It sounds like you have two separate goals. One is to make it easy to publish short taxonomic and nomenclature items. The other is to have commentary on datasets and analyses. These feel like very different things, with different audiences and outcomes.If you want to focus on names the one model might be to capture short statements (e.g., according to paper x species y now belongs in genus z) and send these somewhere useful. Zoobank is probably not ideal as (a) it is really clunky and (b) most name changes in zoology  (e.g., synonyms) are out of its scope. Using CheckList Bank might make sense, having a single database of name changes that everyone can search (and which might end up being of use to Catalogue of Life).If the focus is datasets (e.g., phylogenies) then the task is complicated, especially as we lack a global database of phylogenies (TreeBase is on life support). But a model might be to have commentaries on existing datasets, and think of a way to have those commentaries appear on the same site as the data. A bit like, say, altmetric. That's a big ask, but I think you need to ask "how will anyone know if a dataset has problems?".Hope this doesn't come across as too negative. I just think that if "let's have a new journal" is the answer then you are probably not asking the right question.Regards,RodSent from Outlook for iOS<https://nam10.safelinks.protection.outlook.com/?url=https%3A%2F%2Faka.ms%2Fo0ukef&data=05%7C02%7Ctaxacom%40lists.ku.edu%7C515d2e0d1005438c82ff08dd9aa0ec54%7C3c176536afe643f5b96636feabbe3c1a%7C0%7C0%7C638836738179971514%7CUnknown%7CTWFpbGZsb3d8eyJFbXB0eU1hcGkiOnRydWUsIlYiOiIwLjAuMDAwMCIsIlAiOiJXaW4zMiIsIkFOIjoiTWFpbCIsIldUIjoyfQ%3D%3D%7C0%7C%7C%7C&sdata=PzuiLuatdWuac%2BUmEVLhmRzs1ocuu60ULC%2FNCoK31is%3D&reserved=0>________________________________From: Taxacom <taxacom-bounces at lists.ku.edu> on behalf of Kuoi Zhang via Taxacom <taxacom at lists.ku.edu>Sent: Saturday, May 24, 2025 8:11:29 AMTo: Richard Littauer <richard.littauer at gmail.com>Cc: taxacom at lists.ku.edu <taxacom at lists.ku.edu>Subject: Re: Taxacom: an idea on data reuse preprint for systematicsHello Richard,Thank you for your reply.

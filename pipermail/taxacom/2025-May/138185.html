Geoff  - Got a bit lost with your reasoning. I have no problem with 'long
distance' dispersal or short distance dispersal. After all, taxa that have
a near global distribution had to disperse over a 'long distance."
Dispersal certainly explains species having a geographic range. That's an
ecological reality. But the Fiji iguana argument is that a unique one off
chance dispersal event led to the origin of iguanas in Fiji. However one
might imagine this as a possibility (just as I might imagine that moa
ancestor swam across the Tasman Sea), imagining is not science. Science is
about evidence and there is simply no evidence that the origin of the Fiji
species are derived from rafting across the Pacific from America. Cheers,
John

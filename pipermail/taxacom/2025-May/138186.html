Dear Francisco and Thomas,


Thank you both for your answers, which make things much easier! I think 
it was precisely here that I was snagged and couldn't get any further:

"The trick is that a "nominal species" (actually this should read 
"nominal species-group taxon", because in rare cases also names 
established as nominal subspecies would count) is a concept of a taxon 
denoted by a name.
This is what must have been cited by Nitzsch. And this means, also 
nominal species cited as synonyms count (actually these could be names 
of species and of subspecies, in this case it was a name of a species 
established by Linnaeus 1758). The synonym Pediculus pavonis Linnaeus, 
1758 was an available name, had its own name-bearing type and was 
included by Nitzsch in the new subgenus. Art. 67.2.1 does neither state 
nor imply that only names used as valid by Nitzsch would count.

In other words: In those cases where a new genus-group taxon was 
established and included various species (in the classification of that 
author), the synonyms do count."

It was particularly confusing to me as Hopkins in the 1950s applied to 
the Commission to clarify matters on some other similar cases within 
lice, but asserted that this exact problem didn't exist for Goniodes, 
without any elaboration.

Happy that Goniodes has a type species, and this does not need to be 
addressed further.
Thanks a lot!

Cheers,
Daniel


> 
> My interpretation seems to differ slightly from how Thomas judged this
> interesting case.
> 
> Philopterus (Goniodes) falcicornis was made available by indication,
> because two bibliographic references were provided, satisfying Art.
> 12.2.1.
> Redi, Exper. tab. XIV. referred unambiguously to Redi 1671: Tab. XIV (= 
> 14).
> The reference to Panzer was also unambiguously bibliographic in
> nature. These two references made the name available.
> The reference "Pedic. Pavoonis, Linn. Fabr." was not bibliographic in
> nature, this was only a synonym, not satisfying a criterion unter Art.
> 12.1 (indications) and had no influence to the availability of Ph.
> falcicornis, the name used by Nitzsch. This was just a synonym,
> nothing more.
> 
> Type species:
> Johnston & Harrison 1911: 326 (I did not check this source and rely on
> what was reported, in particular I rely on the cited text in quotation
> marks: "G. pavonis L. (syn. G. falcicornis N.)") designated Pediculus
> pavonis Linnaeus, 1758 as type. The fact that these authors cited G.
> falcicornis as a synonym has no influence. The designation referred to
> pavonis.
> For the originally included nominal species Art. 67.2 is decisive, in
> this case Art. 67.2.1.
> 
> 67.2.1. In the meaning of the Code the "originally included nominal
> species" comprise only those included in the newly established nominal
> genus or subgenus, having been cited in the original publication by an
> available name (including citation by an incorrect spelling [Art.
> 67.6]) of a species or subspecies (see Articles 45.6 and 68.2), or
> having been cited there as the deliberate application of a previous
> misidentification (see Articles 11.10, 67.13 and 69.2.4).
> 
> So, nominal species must be included, and Nitzsch must have cited
> these "nominal species" by an available name of a species or
> subspecies.
> 
> Glossary:
> "originally included nominal species, n. Of a nominal genus-group
> taxon: the nominal species deemed to be originally included under
> Article 67.2."
> 
> "nominal taxon. A concept of a taxon which is denoted by an available
> name (e.g. Mollusca, Diptera, Bovidae, Papilio, Homo sapiens). Each
> nominal taxon in the family, genus or species groups is based on a
> name-bearing type (although in the latter two groups such a type may
> not have been actually fixed)."
> 
> The trick is that a "nominal species" (actually this should read
> "nominal species-group taxon", because in rare cases also names
> established as nominal subspecies would count) is a concept of a taxon
> denoted by a name.
> This is what must have been cited by Nitzsch. And this means, also
> nominal species cited as synonyms count (actually these could be names
> of species and of subspecies, in this case it was a name of a species
> established by Linnaeus 1758). The synonym Pediculus pavonis Linnaeus,
> 1758 was an available name, had its own name-bearing type and was
> included by Nitzsch in the new subgenus. Art. 67.2.1 does neither
> state nor imply that only names used as valid by Nitzsch would count.
> 
> In other words: In those cases where a new genus-group taxon was
> established and included various species (in the classification of
> that author), the synonyms do count.
> 
> In Art. 68.3 (type species by monotypy) the Code expressly excludes
> the names cited as synonyms, which is necessary because the monotypy
> regulation would otherwise contradict the definition of originally
> included nominal species in Art. 67.2.1, and monotypy could not apply
> if a synonym was mentioned.
> 
> Art. 69.2.2 refers to slightly different cases: This would apply if
> Nitzsch would not have cited Ped. pavonis, and Johnston & Harrison
> 1911 would have designated P. pavonis as type and stated that they
> regarded this as a synonym of Ph. falcicornis. In this case Ph.
> falcicornis would be the type under Art. 69.2.2.
> 
> But since Nitzsch 1818: 293 included Ped. pavonis (as an expressly
> cited synonym), the designation of Ped. pavonis as type stands
> immediately under Art. 69.1, Ped. pavonis having been among the
> "originally included nominal species" under Art. 67.2.1.
> 
> And this means that the traditionally accepted type species P. pavonis
> is in line with the Code.
> 
> This part of the Code is very confusing and we are going to propose a
> modified wording of these regulations that will make it easier to
> understand. If monotypy applies, synonyms do not count, in all other
> modes of type designations synonyms do count.
> 
> If I have not overlooked something decisive.
> 
> Best wishes
> Francisco

Martin - it is very likely Magnolia campbellii, or a hybrid of the
species, with characteristic floppy outer tepals giving a "cup and
saucer" appearance to the flowers.  It is one of the signature plants at
Strybing Arboretum, where it first flowered in North America in 1941.

 I hope this helps.

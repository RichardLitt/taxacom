    Well, although I wouldn't highly recommend the Miklausen book (and
myself glad that I borrowed it rather than buying it), I actually enjoy
reading this kind of book from time to time.
    In 1857, Darwin wrote (I hope I've got it right):  I am a firm believer
that without speculation there is no good and original observation.
     Miklausen's speculations may be on the wild side overall, but that
doesn't prevent me from digging deeper in case there are some "nuggets of
wisdom" in there somewhere.  The fact that he and I hold some similar views
on the origins of life does not cast aspersions (guilt by association) on
those views in my opinion, and therefore poses no dilemma from my
perspective.
     Most of Miklausen's speculations may be "resoundingly falsified", but I
still find them interesting and hope to eventually find something of
botanical value in there somewhere.
    As to whether lateral transfers cannot be falsified or whether they are
just "very difficult" to falsify, perhaps that is a matter of differing
philosophies.  I would only say, viva la difference.  Anyway, thanks for the
interesting feedback.  I'm looking forward to once again examining the
"fungal" lateral transfer hypothesis on metaphyte origins.  More of that
"speculation" I think Darwin was referring to, the kind that makes biology
such an interesting field.
                   --------Ken Kinman

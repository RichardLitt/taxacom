I definitely agree with John that evolution is quite often wrongly
presented as empirical fact. However, I think that the program of reform
that the Fundamentalist Christians (FC) would force on all us has many
implications beyond whether evolution or creation is taught as dogma.
The FC believe that they are operating under a mandate from providence
to eliminate diversity of thought in general. They know well that by
removing evolution an explanatory vacuum is created that their
particular religion will step up to fill. We won't see other creation
myths being taught in schools, e.g. African or Native American stories
of creation. Just look to history... no look at the world today... to
see the effects of fundamentalism. I shudder.

There is no equivalence between "belief in God" (their particular one),
an untestable and empirically empty concept, and evolution as an
explanatory scientific hypothesis (I think a really good explanation).
Somehow we have to convince people that these are not interchangeable
and each has an appropriate way and place to be taught.

k.

Moreover, I have no idea on how orthogenesis is defined in an Italian

>From (the little) I know, it looks like Darwin itself was aware of the
importance of constraints, and he referred to them in several editions of
his book "On the Origin of species by Means of Natural Selection", and in
several of his published letters (see Craw, 1984. Charles Darwin on "Laws of
Growth". Tuatara 27: 19-20 for a compilation of relevant quotations).
Darwin admitted to have extended too far the action of natural selection, he
recognized that many morphological changes in plants could be "attributed to
the laws of growth, independently of Natural Selection" (Darwin, 1872. On
the Origin etc. 6th edn; see also Craw 1984. L?on Croizat's biogeographic
work: A personal appreciation. Tuatara 27: 8-13; Grehan 1984. Evolution by
law: Croizat's "Orthogeny" and Darwin's "Laws of Growth." Tuatara 27:
14-18).

In any case, Orthogenesis simply refers to the fact that a variation of form
is limited and constrained by the initial state.

This simple fact, therefore, limits the action of natural selection, doesn't
say it is irrelevant.

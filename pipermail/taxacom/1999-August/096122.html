What is a Wandering Refugium?

Glacial/geological evidence and biological evidence for an area during an
identified time may conflict.  In 1964, I offered an explanation that keeps
the geologist and the biologist happy.

Consider that biologists say, "There is a old assemblage here, probably
pre-Wisconsinan."  The geologists say, "This area was entirely covered
during the Wisconsinan."  There is an answer that solves the conflict.

A glacier front or tongue can move into an area at the same time ice is
receding from other local areas.   These movements usually happen very
slowly.  The result is that the biota (fauna and flora) there move away from
one front and move to an area or areas recently vacated by ice.   This is
called a "Wandering Refugium".  There are many examples of this in
Scandinavia and in the high Canadian Arctic (e.g., Lake Hazen area on
northern Ellesmere Island).

I have used the term "Wandering Refugium" since 1964.

Robin Leech

Dear All,
         The MERS coronavirus didn't cause a pandemic, and yet the death rate was about 30%.  Since 2012, MERS has caused 2,494 confirmed cases in 27 countries and killed 858 people.  And it is very transmissible (one man in South Korea infected 186 others).
        So I think the concern about the current coronavirus is also overblown.  Even if the number of deaths increases from 80 to 800, that would be largely due to the very densely populated city of Wuhan.  And in the very unlikely event that 8,000 died, even that would hardly compare with the 250,000 to 500,000 people who die each year from influenza.  Influenza causes frequent pandemics, but is there any coronavirus that has  ever caused a true pandemic?   True pandemics are caused by diseases like smallpox, cholera, plague and influenza.   I agree with William Schaffner that this new coronavirus will be a "blip on the horizon in comparison" to influenza.
                         -----------Ken

Dear All,

      The news media seems to be overly hyping the threat of the new coronovirus.  However, the threat might not be any more than the related SARS virus (which killed about 800 people worldwide).  Furthermore, the threat of the flu is far greater (well over 8,000 deaths in the U.S. so far this season and probably hundreds of thousands of deaths worldwide).  Hysteria like this could needlessly divert resources from detecting something even more deadly (which could cause a real pandemic).

I was thinking along these lines:
This paper contains links to sequences for Ochlerotatus triseriatus / Aedes
triseriatus collected from Minnesota, Iowa, Wisconsin.

*An analysis of gene flow among midwestern populations of the mosquito
Ochlerotatus triseriatus*

ERIC T BECK, CHRISTOPHER F BOSIO, DAVID A GESKE, CAROL D BLAIR, BARRY J
BEATY, William C Black

The American Journal of Tropical Medicine and Hygiene

*2005 vol. 73 (3) pp. 534-40*

*
*

The data suggests that this is one large metapopulation.


This information along with morphological features, collections and
photographs now serve to support that this population is one species and

in doing so it helps clarify the characteristics of this species.


The next step would be to determine if this species is the same as the type
specimen.


Over time, the data starts to help determine where this species starts and
another begins. What specimens are representative and what specimens are
probably a different species.


- Pete

> Excellent idea in theory, but the problem is that you need some form of
> authority behind the "concept" for people to be happy to point their
> "appropriate items" at it.   So this requires details about the name and its
> nomenclatural history and synonymy, which then becomes a fairly specific
> taxon concept.  So you really need the "appropriate items" to build up the
> concept before other people are willing to use it as a "base" identifier - a
> bit chicken and egg really.

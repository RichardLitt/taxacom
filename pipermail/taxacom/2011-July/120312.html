>Acceptance by non-taxonomists is an issue

interestingly, there are further parallels here to big trouble I ran into 
recently while trying to improve some Wikipedia articles on philosophy 
(metaphysics), which were *really bad*

the problem with Wikipedia is that, *on the whole* (not exclusively), the 
distribution of contributor's abilities is heavily skewed towards the lower end 
(most higher end people are either too busy elsewhere, or just don't care to 
contribute to Wikipedia, which causes something of a vicious circle...) So, 
Wikipedia kind of works, but only for very simple fact-based stuff, requiring 
little or no interpretation. Anything at all complicated, and you run into big 
problems. In my case, my attempts at improving an article were instantly pounced 
on and reverted by two lower end contributors, who just didn't "get it", if you 
know what I mean, and the end result is that I got blocked from Wikipedia for 
breaking a "3 revert rule" (and arguing about it!), and they were able to win 
the edit war simply because there was two of them. So, in general, two idiots 
can trump one relatively qualified (MA)?contributor on Wikipedia!

So, what is the relevance of all this, you ask? Well, just that if we are not 
careful, everything will get dragged down to the level of the lowest common 
denominator, which may sound snobby, but unfortunately fact. Non-taxonomists who 
dislike name changes should *not* be allowed to impede scientific progress. 
"Ivory towers" (or something similar) may have their place (though are often 
abused) ...

Stephen

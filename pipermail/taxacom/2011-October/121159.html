Michael's response reminded me of a line from one of Robert Parker's Spencer mysteries. Spencer observes two events that appear linked. He rejects coincidence (or chance events) as an explanation. A character in the story asks him if he believes in coincidence and Spencer replies, I do but it doesn't do us any good.

> Hi Ken,

> The more detailed study by Vidal et al. (2008) does indeed provide additional evidence for vicariance that Macey et al. (2004) were unaware of, as it shows that the two trans-Atlantic clades are themselves mutually allopatric. One is in western Mexico, western Cuba, Spain/Morocco and Turkey, the other is in South America, Greater Antilles and subsaharan Africa. The two overlap only in western Cuba. The overlap can be attributed to dispersal (range expansion), but no other dispersal is needed. Why is there such neat allopatry between the two trans-Atlantic clades? And why did each clade only disperse once across the Atlantic? This distinctive combination of the two phenomena would be simply explained by vicariance and there is no need to invoke chance events.

Michael, I really don?t know what you are leading to. Morgan was the leading figure of genetics with a Nobel to boot for ?solving? inheritance (chromosomes as the units of information), so yes we agree he was a big fish (also popularized D.melanogaster as the workhorse of genetics). What I don?t understand is why you say he was ridiculed (and by whom). He rejected sexual selection, so this could be the source of some criticism, but by and large the man was a neodarwinian. His ?A Critique of the Theory of Evolution? supports Darwinian Selection, supplying the rather important piece of inheritance, without which it was fatally flawed. Morgan even suggested the title! The book is practically the synthesis. It just needed Mayr, Gaylord Smith and others to give it mass appeal (OK Fisher and Haldane made important contributions but Dobzhansky is the true father of population genetics and the modern synthesis), establishing populations as the units of evolution, balancing selections (natural selection, mutation, migration, drift..) as the forces that resulted in genetic variability (this variability was the Achilles heel that De Vries, Johannson and other anti-darwinists attacked!). So once again, Lynch is not postmodern synthesis but additional work in population genetics.  MH: you seem to have missed out something here - 'sympatric'?? I think he was correct to dismiss sympatric speciation. Most supposedly sympatric groups actually show high levels of allopatry and the area of sympatry probably represents secondary overlap. The limnetic and benthic forms of three-spined sticklebacks in the British Columbia lakes turned out not to form monophyletic pairs in lakes. Instead, they are related to limnetic and benthic forms, respectively, in other lakes. The two forms didn't evolve by sympatric speciation within a lake but diverged before their secondary overlap (e.g. caused by marine flooding).

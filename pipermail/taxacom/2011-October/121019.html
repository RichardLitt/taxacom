The phylogenetic "common ancestor" is unnamed, unobservable and a hidden
cause. It is more than a locum tenens for a taxon that is being
investigated; it is never investigated. Maximumm parsimony would be the
naming of as many common ancestors as possible ("superoptimization"). 

Regarding which classification might be best, Linnaean classification
models nothing in nature. Neither clades nor sequential macroevolution
are directly modeled. What is good about it is its simple, two means of
indicating relationship, nesting and contiguity in lists, and it never
never forces a name into synonymy or to be split because of holophyly.

If one really needs more telegraphing of inferred evolutionary
relationships in classification, use PhyloCode for classification by
cladograms or the mythic MacroevoCode for classification by sequential
generation of one taxon from another.

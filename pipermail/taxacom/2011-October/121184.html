My own experience is that geographic patterns can be explained with?phases of range expansion (by normal dispersal, using normal means seen every day, and not?involving?differentiation) alternating with?phases of vicariance. To my way of thinking the 'endless' discussion is not about vicariance vs dispersal, but about the assumptions that most biogeographers have, and continue to make about using Darwin's theory of center of origin and means of dispersal to understand the spatial structure of life rather than analyzing the spatial structure of life to derive an understanding about the role of means of dispersal in the origin of geographic differentiation. 

To play the worn our record again, dispersal is very popular among biogeographers because most biogeographers are not interested in analyzing the spatial structure of differentiated entities and getting to know the tedious details of distribution (tedious because they are so often repetitive). With centers of origin and dispersal one is freed up from reading the records of life to make up whatever center of origin and dispersal story for an individual group or situation without having to worry about those nagging spatial incongruities. 

John Grehan

Thomson Reuters ION metrics for species (and subspecies) (http://www.organismnames.com/metrics.htm?page=graphs) show a current figure of around 17000 newly described per year. This doesn't include plants or fungi, but DOES include fossil taxa. Even then, the figure seems a bit high to me, but the argument of Costello et al. is then straightforwardly as follows:
?
there are only 1 million more species left to describe (where they get this from, I don't know)
?
with a modest increase to 20000 new species described per year, it will take 50 years to describe everything.
?
and yet, I still think that not much will change in the next 50 years, at least not in N.Z., Australia, Africa, tropical Asia, or North America ...

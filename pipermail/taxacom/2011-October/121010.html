Richard wrote:              
What can I say, Ken? APG is the bugbear of our times, not because it is
all bad. That would be too much to ask. It mixes together plenty of
good, new advances with lumping or splitting because of artificial
systems of classification, i.e. holophyly, as you say, and grouping by
molecular continuity and splits (rather than a combination of that plus
clear cases of macroevolution). 
Linnaean classification does not directly represent either cladistic
nesting or macroevolution, but we can use its nesting and contiguity
(read "lists") of names to group evolutionarily related taxa without
eliminating names because of paraphyly. Thus, the problem that Linnaean
classification does not directly reflect processes in nature can work in
its favor by providing a neutral ground representing a diversity of
apprehensions of evolutionary processes.

Dear Frank, dear colleagues,

although I have no doubts that you, Frank, do a proper job as an editor, 
I want to state my concerns as to the style and possible risks of MDPI 
journals. Besides some direct personal experience that seriously speak 
against the correctness of the editorial conduct in some cases, I point 
to the recent case of a clearly poorly edited taxonomic paper in MDPI 
Diversity:

Rivas JA, De La Quintana P, Mancuso M , et al. Disentangling the 
anacondas: revealing a new green species and rethinking yellows. 
Diversity 2024a;16:127. https://nam10.safelinks.protection.outlook.com/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fdoi.org%2F10.3390%2Fd16020127&data=05%7C02%7Ctaxacom%40lists.ku.edu%7Cb4b814294365425b53eb08dd3ed97acb%7C3c176536afe643f5b96636feabbe3c1a%7C0%7C0%7C638735828003716527%7CUnknown%7CTWFpbGZsb3d8eyJFbXB0eU1hcGkiOnRydWUsIlYiOiIwLjAuMDAwMCIsIlAiOiJXaW4zMiIsIkFOIjoiTWFpbCIsIldUIjoyfQ%3D%3D%7C40000%7C%7C%7C&sdata=wFPHauQnvxGlqcS1O812nj3svmUEN0IUD5Nx9bvfyUg%3D&reserved=0. See comments 
on this paper in Wuester et al (2024) How not to describe a species. 
Zoological Journal of the Linnean Society 21 zlae099: 1-26 doi 
10.1093/zoolinnean/zlae099.

Additional concerns can be found here:

https://nam10.safelinks.protection.outlook.com/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fpredatoryjournals.org%2Fnews%2Ff%2Fis-mdpi-a-predatory-publisher&data=05%7C02%7Ctaxacom%40lists.ku.edu%7Cb4b814294365425b53eb08dd3ed97acb%7C3c176536afe643f5b96636feabbe3c1a%7C0%7C0%7C638735828003733363%7CUnknown%7CTWFpbGZsb3d8eyJFbXB0eU1hcGkiOnRydWUsIlYiOiIwLjAuMDAwMCIsIlAiOiJXaW4zMiIsIkFOIjoiTWFpbCIsIldUIjoyfQ%3D%3D%7C40000%7C%7C%7C&sdata=iaFXlzjTCicxPOhtXVOwynC23nwHdawh8QuT3bOplh4%3D&reserved=0

There you find the important point: *MDPI* is using some of the same 
techniques of predatory journals. So the question is simple: if you are 
a scientist, should you work with *MDPI*?

Just in case someone cares.

Best regards

Theo Michael Schmitt

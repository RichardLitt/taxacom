I was trying to figure out why the authors formerly treated Dasytes as
masculine, and why Monotes is still treated as masculine.
Here's something from GreekPod on -tes as a masculine ending (it isn't
listed in the list of feminine endings):
*-ης*      Examples: ο λογιστής (*o loyistís*) meaning “the accountant”; ο
υπολογιστής (*o ipoloyistís*) meaning “the computer”; ο πελάτης (*o pelátis*)
meaning “the customer”; ο μαθητής (*o mathitís*) meaning “the student.”

Following the argument for Dasytes being feminine, should Monotes be
treated as feminine? Or does it depend on the root rather than the suffix?

> And THIS is the definition that applies to "dasytes", which is the state
> of being covered in hairs (dasys + tes).
>
> The endings "-es" and "-tes" in Greek DO NOT have fixed gender. Some are
> masculine, some are feminine, and not all of them are suffixes.
>
> In Greek, about the only *suffixes* that appear to have fixed gender are
> "-e" and "-is" (from -ις, *not* -ης), both feminine.
>
> As for Mike Ivie's question:
>
> "Define "the Dictionary" please.  I can write a dictionary, can we use
> that dictionary?"
>
> The Code DOES define which dictionary, e.g., for Greek:
>
> "30.1.2. a genus-group name that is or ends in a Greek word
> transliterated into Latin without other changes takes the gender given for
> that word *in standard Greek dictionaries*;"
>
> Unless the dictionary you write is considered a standard Greek dictionary,
> it's not an accepted source. Note that the entries in Perseus and
> Wiktionary both cite which standard Greek dictionaries they are based on,
> and when they cite standard Greek dictionaries, they do comply with the
> Code.

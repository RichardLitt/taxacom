I would think that it's not the ending -της in Wiktionary that is relevant;
it's just the lexical entry for the full word in LSJ and other standard
Greek dictionaries. Again, there's no question that δασυτης is feminine.

"Standard dictionary" isn't defined anywhere, but for this, LSJ normally
works. I use https://nam10.safelinks.protection.outlook.com/?url=https%3A%2F%2Flogeion.uchicago.edu%2F&data=05%7C02%7Ctaxacom%40lists.ku.edu%7C57f0d6d934334dc1baf408dd40c3bb60%7C3c176536afe643f5b96636feabbe3c1a%7C0%7C0%7C638737933201960702%7CUnknown%7CTWFpbGZsb3d8eyJFbXB0eU1hcGkiOnRydWUsIlYiOiIwLjAuMDAwMCIsIlAiOiJXaW4zMiIsIkFOIjoiTWFpbCIsIldUIjoyfQ%3D%3D%7C80000%7C%7C%7C&sdata=68NYMn6rMbrtLli4PQs%2FDdyucmRSJTKYYoI6mvneD24%3D&reserved=0 more and more, because it
scrapes from multiple dictionaries. Wikipedia isn't always right.

Before updating Wikispecies, Wikipedia, BugGuide, iNat, etc - it's
important to leave a papertrail explaining the rational and showing which
names were changed. A short correspondence article to Zootaxa should work.
If Dr. Plonski needs help with drafting or reviewing that, I'd be happy to
help, and I'm sure that Douglas would also be up for reviewing at least
before hand.

>  1. Forms nouns representing a state of being
>
> And THIS is the definition that applies to "dasytes", which is the state
> of being covered in hairs (dasys + tes).
>
> The endings "-es" and "-tes" in Greek DO NOT have fixed gender. Some are
> masculine, some are feminine, and not all of them are suffixes.
>
> In Greek, about the only *suffixes* that appear to have fixed gender are
> "-e" and "-is" (from -ις, *not* -ης), both feminine.
>
> As for Mike Ivie's question:
>
> "Define "the Dictionary" please.  I can write a dictionary, can we use
> that dictionary?"
>
> The Code DOES define which dictionary, e.g., for Greek:
>
> "30.1.2. a genus-group name that is or ends in a Greek word
> transliterated into Latin without other changes takes the gender given
> for that word *in standard Greek dictionaries*;"
>
> Unless the dictionary you write is considered a standard Greek
> dictionary, it's not an accepted source. Note that the entries in
> Perseus and Wiktionary both cite which standard Greek dictionaries they
> are based on, and when they cite standard Greek dictionaries, they do
> comply with the Code.

Denatured alcohol or isopropyl alcohol works. Both are widely available in many African countries as rubbing alcohol in supermarkets/convenience stores or drug stores, or in pharmacies for medical purposes. Isopropyl alcohol seems to bleach the color of the specimens a bit after many years, so should probably not be used for permanent storage, but should be better than hand sanitizer.

At least historically, e.g., in the USA during the Prohibition period, "denatured" alcohol was used. In some countries I have travelled to, when EtOH (the "drinking sort") was not available, ethanol mixed with methanol and isopropyl alcohol, to make it unfit for human consumption, was readily available. These additives can inhibit later use of tissues for DNA extraction, however. So that might be a factor to consider. But as far as I know, denatured alcohol works fine as a preservative. Hand sanitizer may have any number of proprietary ingredients that could impact the specimens in unpredictable ways.

Hi Doug,
You gave a long list of masculine names ending in -tes, which are
uncontroversial, but also: 'Agriotes, Gaurotes, Lipotes, and Microtes are
feminine. You have to look each one up, you can't just look at the ending
and guess'.  I disagree - Agriotes (100s of species) is always treated as
masculine (from the epithets at GBIF), as is Lipotes - one species
vexillifer - masculine. (I think it was a simple mistake to treat Gaurotes
and Microtes (very few species) as feminine).

The ending -tes isn't 'lawless' - it can either mean 'abstract quantity'
(-itas in Latin, -ity in English, -idad in Spanish) in which case it is
feminine. Or it can simply mean 'the one/s', and this is masculine. I think
ALL the genus names in -tes are the latter - who would name a genus
'hairiness'  (Dasytes) or 'fieldness' (Agriotes)? The actual meaning is
'the hairy one/s' or 'field one/s' - masculine. The 19th century authors
just slapped -tes on any word as a general ending (cf. Socrates,
Philoctetes, stratiotes - soldier, polites - citizen), e.g. the genus
Eremotes. This is why no-one is suggesting changing Agriotes or Lipotes to
feminine, and likewise there is no reason to change the many epithets in
Dasytes, as. Dasytes doesn't mean 'hairiness' but 'the hairy one/s'. This
also explains why the earlier author made such a point of treating it as
masculine.
   Richard - thanks for the tip on Monotes. Yes, masculine. Because it
doesn't mean 'oneness' or 'uniqueness' (feminine), but 'the only one'
(masculine), i.e. the only dipterocarp in Africa.

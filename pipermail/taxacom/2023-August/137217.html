vicariance cladistics (Nelson, Platnick etc.) adopted the idea that
topologically congruent area cladograms (phylogenies) for different groups
were evidence for vicariance, incongruence meant chance dispersal. Croizat,
Robin Craw and myself criticised these analyses in the early 1980s on the
basis of their a priori areas ('Australia' 'South America' etc.), neglect
of any geological history apart from continental drift, neglect of
pre-drift differentiation, assumption of ancestral monomorphism etc.

> >> So as not to confuse with other issues, I teased this item out. My view
> >> about definitions not mattering is in the context that definitions only
> >> state what one has already decided or what one thinks one already knows.
> >> Within those constraints definitions may matter, but they are just a
> >> summary of a certain knowledge or decisions, not a key to the unknown
> >> which
> >> is what science is concerned with. For example, if I were to define
> >> evolution as space + time + form, that may well be very informative to
> >> some, and not so informative to others. So, dealing with the unknown,

The random requests are a result of the tug-of-war between editors seeking to uphold quality and MDPI office staff seeking to speed everything up. When a paper is submitted, it goes to the handling editor, who will determine suitable reviewers, and the MDPI office then takes over and contacts those reviewers. However, if those are not available, the MDPI office staff then email other reviewers from their database based simply on any match of key words, and without referring back to the editor - hence the random requests. E.g., because I am interested in snake venom evolution, I have "venom" as a field of expertise in the MDPI database, and then end up getting requests to review papers on the development of a cancer drug from ant venoms.

What is even more worrisome is that when the reviewers, however chosen, return a verdict of "minor revision", the MDPI office staff then send the reviewer comments straight back to the authors without referring to an editor. Only when the authors then submit their revision does the editor get to see reviewers' reports - and it's not necessarily the same editor! That then results in wailing and gnashing of teeth all around when the editor spots major problems in the revised ms and has to return it to the authors for more major revision or even has to reject it.?

This process explains why MDPI journals are often a mix of great and appalling, and why so many people get near-random review requests. Does that make MDPI predatory? Your call. As an author, those few papers I have published through them received competent and professional reviews, and my author experience was very positive. I am not a fan, but neither would I go out of my way to boycott them.

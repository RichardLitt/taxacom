> Deserted field? Rehabilitate morphology? And do this by mapping
> morphological traits to a molecular phylogeny?
>
> One might do better by mapping molecular traits to a morphological tree.
> This is because for every extant progenitor there may be extant molecular
> variants of that exact taxonomic progenitor the same as that of its own
> progenitor and its own direct descendant species. Say, 4 or 5 molecular
> identities the same as other  other species in one progenitor
> morphospecies. No wonder morphological traits get scattered over
> dichotomous trees.

> This is with pleasure and excitement that I announce that a special issue
> on Morphology, Diversity and Evolution of Annelida has been announced in
> the journal MDPI Diversity. The aim of this issue is to focus on morphology
> and rehabilitate it as much as possible, as it is often a bit of a deserted
> field. Furthermore the diversity of annelid morphological structures is
> quite amazing and deserves more attention. This does not have to be pure
> anatomical papers, just a morphological perspective would be required :)
> (e.g. mapping morphological traits on a molecular phylogeny and a
> discussion on the homology would be appreciated :) ).

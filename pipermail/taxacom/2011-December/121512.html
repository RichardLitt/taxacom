John, 
For the example of human-great ape relationships, where the precise phylogenetic relationships seem to be forever debatable, the most sensible thing to do is to keep the phylogenetic uncertainties out of classification, by defining the first suprageneric level broadly enough to avoid paraphyly on any phylogenetic theory. In this case, that would mean putting Homo, Pan, Gorilla, Pongo, and possibly even Hylobates etc. in one big (counting fossil taxa)?family called Hominidae, and not splitting it up into subfamilies or tribes. Scientists can still investigate and debate the phylogenetic details, but the uncertainties should not, in my opinion, be allowed to destabilise the classification ...
Stephen

A paraphyletic arrangement (really referring to a paraphyletic group) is
not consistent with either possibility. Only an unresolved tree might
be.

For example, one might have 

Pongo (Gorilla [chimp, human]}

and designate Pongo, gorilla, chimp as a paraphyletic group 'Pongidae'.
In this particular case the paraphyletic group is not consistent with a
human-orangutan clade at all.

I doubt we ever know 'for sure' what is the sister group of any taxon.
What we have is that some are in more dispute than others (and often
enough where there is not dispute there is no more than a single
individual or research group involved).

What I have 'admitted' is that there is no objective recipe for choosing
between contradictory molecular and morphological results. 

As for the 'vast majority' of biologist regarding the morphological
evidence as very weak, who cares? Opinion is opinion. What matters in
science (in my myopic opinion) is the published scientific demonstration
or support for the opinion, and when it comes to the orangutan
characters, these have, for the most part, been ignored by that 'vast
majority'.

My 'refusal' to accept the morphology as being plesiomorphic is based on
specific arguments about the phylogenetic significance of morphological
and molecular characters and these arguments have been published. So I
am entitled to take a particular stance (which could be right or could
be wrong).

John (extremist biogeographer and morphologist and proud of it)

John wrote: 
I don't see how a paraphyletic Pongidae solves anything since I think
the human-orang clade is real so there is no necessity for a
paraphyletic Pongidae. The latter would not be consistent with the
human-orang clade unless the sequence ended with humans and orangutans,
and then in which case there would be no need for a Pongidae for great
apes to exist. Pongidae as proposed by Schwartz would be restricted to
orangutans and their nearest fossil relatives. 
The difference between myself and the chimp-hominid folks is that I am
willing to acknowledge that there are two incompatible phylogenetic
reconstructions and I am happy to see both side by side in
representations of primate phylogeny. According to the chimp-hominid
folk, at least those who have made public declarations, this can never
be allowed. The orangutan must stay in the closet. 
I am interested in the reality of phylogeny. Perhaps the ultimate
reality for some taxa is instability. Perhaps 'ture' phylogeny of life
is inherently ambiguous. Morphologically, however, the human-orangutan
clade seems very stable indeed.

Dear All,
the term "nomenclator" seems to me a bit misleading when it comes to
zoological names.
Wonderful to have names linked to original publications, but if you want to
bring that information into current context, i.e. with annotations such as
which names are accepted valid names and which are subjective synonyms, you
need to elaborate on a current classification, - at least in provisional
concise checklist style. You cannot expect a fit-for-use checklist emerges
by itself while compiling published information. In any larger group of
animals, you will have updated and outdated information, narrow and wide
taxonomic concepts at all ranks, that you need to bring together and where
your taxonomist's judgement is needed.
What we can do for zoological names is an authoritative ZooBank, - a
perfectly stable nomenclatural basis limited to the 'realm' of
nomenclature. The task of bringing that basic zoological nomenclature and
all usages of these names into a current context, that's the task of
taxonomists...

Best regards,
Wolfgang

You will find an array of what, I think, you are looking for in  Larsen, J.
& Patterson, D. J. 1990. Some flagellates (Protista) from tropical marine
sediments. *Journal of Natural History*, *24*: 801-937.

Jacob and I elected to handle the nomenclature of ambiregnal taxa under
both the ICZN and the then ICBN.  That resulted in alternative names for
the same taxon.  Many euglenid genera have homonyms in other areas of the
'tree', and Peranema is worth analysis as the type species is different
depending on which Code you apply.

Best

David Patterson

> Dear colleagues,

> for a lecture on nomenclature I look for a taxon - genus or species - that
> can be treated under the Botanical as well as under the Zoological Code of
> Nomenclature and has different names under either regime, be it the genus
> name, the specific epithet, or both.
>
> There are countless examples of "ambiregnal synonyms" above the family
> level, but these names are not subject of the ICZN. Neither "on the
> internet" nor in any of the textbooks at hand could I find an example of
> such a "synonymy" on the genus or species level. Seemingly, there are only
> few on the family-level. One example could be Noctilucaceae - Noctilucidae,
> but the difference pertains only to the ending, which is not just what I am
> searching for.
>
> To avoid misunderstandings, I add that I am not looking for "ambiregnal
> homonyms" (of which many can be found in several sources, e.g.
> http://www.curioustaxonomy.net/rules.html), like Aa Baker, 1940 (a
> mollusk)
> and Aa Reichenbach, 1854 (an orchid).

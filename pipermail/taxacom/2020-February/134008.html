Hi all,
      I was rereading a 2015 paper on the oldest known fossil (mandible and teeth) of the genus Homo dated at about 2.75 million years in age.  It seems to argue against Australopithecus garhi being the closest sister species to genus Homo, and provides some evidence that A. africanus is perhaps instead the nearest sister group to Homo (and A. garhi splitting off before an A. africanus-Homo clade).  Are there any papers since 2015 which dispute this?
      Since there are remains of Australopithecus africanus that are both older and younger than 2.75 million years, I suppose that one could hypothesize that an unknown population of A. africanus actually gave rise to genus Homo, thus making Australopithecus africanus a paraphyletic "ancestral" species.

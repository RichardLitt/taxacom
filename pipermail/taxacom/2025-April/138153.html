Thanks David. That soap carving example is almost beyond belief. I get the
impression that these days museums generally do not loan out types (at
least in my field) which can be frustrating, but is understandable. I was a
little irate about that restriction in the past, but appreciate the caution
now. I was very fortunate to have been able to work on a major study with
loaned types. Otherwise would have been severely limited in that respect.

As for access - there certainly can be justifiable contingencies. To me,
the bizarre nature of this claimed instance is that a prominent journal
allowed it to be stated without any reason for denial being given. As it
stands, it reads as a statement of fact, but implies a judgement of
unfairness to the institution referenced.

I know of two instances of type access being denied. One where a bribe was
required and then after that access was still denied (wanted more money on
the spot), the other that the museum was beholden to a clique of
researchers who wanted to retain control over access. This is sometimes
just how science 'works'.

Cheers, John

While curating a collection, my brother discovered the head of the type
specimen of a caecilian.  Contact with the museum housing the type revealed
that the borrower had substituted a soap carving of the head in order to
keep studying the skull beyond the deadline to return it.  And it is
discouraging to go to a museum and find a note that the specimen was loaned
out over a decade ago to someone who is now dead.  Some taxonomists do give
museums some justification for being cautious.  I have no information about
the particular incident John mentions, though.

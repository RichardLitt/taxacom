I am always somewhat amused when people have opinions on biogeography, but they are so busy that they don't have the time to 'trawl' through the relevant literature. And it was only one paper that was suggested. I would also suggest Stephen Thorpe take the time to read Heads (1989) NZJ Zoology 16:549-585. It should only take 15 minutes to sort out the relevant information. Not an undue burden for anyone interested in biogeography surely.

As Heads points out, the pattern is a normal type of 'fringing island' biogeography seen in many countries. One may invoke dispersal (that once imagined accounts for everything), or sampling error, but they are not imperative. Even if islands in NZ are 'in general, better known, there have been decades of mainland sampling that has yet to refute the reality of the fringing system of tracks in New Zealand (or sampling anywhere else for that matter).

John Grehan

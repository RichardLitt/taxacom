> No, meaning parts of a phylogeny might match while others do not.

Each
lineage is independent from the rest so you wouldn?t expect matching in
this respect. What?s more although we may assume that a shared
evolutionary history between sister lineages will be reflected in their
subsequent evolution (evolutionary inertia), this is a transient
phenomenon that will be obliterated at different rates and on different
characters depending on the evolutionary pressures that each lineage is
evolving under.

Furthermore divergence of two lineages often is
not a clean-cut split but more like a reticulated pattern between
populations in which exchange of information is cut one thread at a
time. As such you would expect disagreement between individual
characters (homoplasy) with the "average" or congruence chosen as the
"best" possible explanation.
